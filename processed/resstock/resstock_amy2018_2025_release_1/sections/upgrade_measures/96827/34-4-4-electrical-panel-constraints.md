## 4.4  Electrical Panel Constraints

Electrical panel constraint modeling was added to ResStock via OpenStudio-HPXML measures in 2025 to assess whether the existing panel has enough capacity or breaker space to accommodate new loads from an energy retrofit. For details on panel constraint modeling, refer to the ResStock Technical Reference Guide (Reyna, et al., 2025).

Switching to a gas water heater generally does not lead to an electrical panel constraint because it does not add new electric load or circuit to the electrical panel. Gas water heaters can be plugged into an existing 120V circuit (which is assumed to be a dedicated outlet if one is required by local amendments to the NEC). They are not considered an electrical load when sizing the electrical panel. Instead, this measure can reduce the total connected load and the number of dedicated circuits when the incumbent water heater being switched out is electric, thus freeing up capacity and breaker space. Any panel constraints appearing in the upgrade results reflect pre-existing conditions, as this measure does not worsen them. 2.3% of the existing panels are estimated to already exceed capacity based on code. Pre-existing capacity constraints are an artifact of the panel modeling process, in which panel service ratings are estimated from selected housing variables rather than detailed inventory data. While anecdotal evidence suggests these constraints occur in practice - manifesting as flickering lights or tripping breakers - the lack of empirical data prevents us from confirming their prevalence.
