## 1.1  Introduction to Technology

Nearly all U.S. homes use a water heater to supply hot water for sinks, baths/showers, and appliances, such as dishwashers and clothes washers. A water heater can be a stand-alone in-unit appliance or a centrally located system that serves multiple units in a building. Storage water heaters heat water in a tank to store for later use, while tankless water heaters heat water for immediate use. Water heaters can use electricity, natural gas, propane, fuel oil, wood, or other fuels to heat the water. Fuel-based water heaters are equipped with a flue vent to release exhaust gas, and this vent can be atmospheric or powered by a small blower fan.

Tankless water heaters are designed to produce hot water on demand. Instead of having a tank to store hot water, they are equipped with burners several times larger in capacity than tanked water heaters. Tankless gas water heaters are a fast-growing subsector within the gas water heater market (Granda, 2024) (Horizon Databook, 2025) because they provide 'endless' hot water, are more efficient, and are more compact than conventional gas storage water heaters (ReportLinker, 2023). The sales of gas water heaters in 2023 were estimated to be 4.2 million for water heaters with storage (81%) (AHRI, 2025) and 1 million for tankless (19%) (Wachunas, 2025). Although gas storage water heaters are still dominant based on total sales, the sales are slowly declining, with shares replaced by electric and gas tankless options, the latter of which is expected to grow by at least 10% per year (Wachunas, 2025). Mirroring this trend, there are more tankless options than storage on the gas water heater market.
