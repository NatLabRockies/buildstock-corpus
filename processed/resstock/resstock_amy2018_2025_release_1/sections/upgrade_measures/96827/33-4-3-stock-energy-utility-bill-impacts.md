## 4.3  Stock Energy Utility Bill Impacts

The modeled overnight energy utility bill savings potential is $10 billion from the high-efficiency natural gas tankless water heater upgrade, with 64% of the savings from electricity and 31% from natural gas, as shown in Figure 10. This is calculated using 2024 state-average utility rates and by applying the natural gas tankless water heater to all homes eligible for such an upgrade, which is estimated to be 55.7% of the housing stock. When downselected to occupied and upgraded dwellings (50% of the stock), the average utility cost savings from the upgrade is $136 per year, or 4.9% of the total annual average energy bills.

On average, 66% of the energy bill savings comes from electricity, which is attributed to only 36% of the total energy savings. In comparison, natural gas represents 29% of the bill savings but 60% of the energy reduction. Because natural gas is the cheapest energy source per unit of site energy in most places analyzed, bill savings are generally higher when switching from electric, propane, or fuel oil water heaters and lower when switching from natural gas water heaters, as shown in Figure 11 and Figure 11. Overall, less than 0.1% of the applicable dwelling units are estimated to see a net bill increase from the water heater upgrade, while 4.2% are expected to see a net increase in site energy consumption.
