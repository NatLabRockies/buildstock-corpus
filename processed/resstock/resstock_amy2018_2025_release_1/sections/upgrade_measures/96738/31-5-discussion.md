## 5 Discussion

This study evaluates the energy impacts of an advanced air sealing upgrade that reduces infiltration to 5 ACH50 for eligible existing units (≥6 ACH50). Results show that modeled site energy savings scale with baseline infiltration level, building vintage, and climate zones. Units with higher initial leakage achieved the largest absolute energy savings. Units starting at 40 -50 ACH50 experienced the greatest reductions when tightened to 5 ACH50, while homes starting near 6 -10 ACH50 saw smaller savings. Leakier homes experience greater convective heat loss (winter) and sensible/latent heat gain (summer), so reducing infiltration has a larger marginal impact. Baseline energy trends (e.g., 40 ACH50 vs. 50 ACH50 groups) are likely driven by other correlated building characteristics such as floor area, vintage, HVAC type, and fuel mix rather than infiltration alone. However, the general relationship between leakage and savings holds across the stock.

Site energy savings also increase with climate severity. Colder climate zones (e.g., 5A, 6A, 7A) show the highest absolute and percentage reductions because the number of heating degree days are greater than cooling degree days. Winter temperature difference is larger than summer difference and heating seasons are longer than cooling seasons. For example, a winter condition of 70°F indoors and 20°F outdoors yields a 50°F difference, while a summer condition of 75°F indoors and 95°F outdoors yields a 20°F difference. Because infiltration loads scale with temperature difference, heating savings are greater than cooling. In warm climates (e.g., 1A), annual consumption is predominately cooling, while heating is minimal or absent. Air sealing still reduces cooling loads, but percentage savings are smaller because temperature difference is modest and cooling seasons are less extreme than cold-climate heating seasons. Lastly, older homes show both higher baseline consumption and greater savings. Pre-1960 buildings show higher infiltration levels and therefore benefit more from tightening to 5 ACH50. Newer homes, especially post-2000 construction, begin closer to modern code performance and therefore show smaller savings.
