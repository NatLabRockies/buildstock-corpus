## 3 Modeling Approach

This section details how the upgrade measure is functionally modeled via ResStock into OpenStudio-HPXML. ResStock models residential air infiltration values expressed as ACH50. Each dwelling unit in the ResStock dataset is assigned an existing ACH50 value based on housing stock characteristics, informed by the Residential Diagnostics Database (ResDB). ResStock integrates this information using probabilistic sampling logic to represent the diversity of the U.S. housing stock. These infiltration rates are dependent on climate zone, floor area, and vintage. In ResStock, the current infiltration values are based on single-family detached blower door tests in units of ACH50. For single-family detached homes, the ACH50 values reflect total leakage to the outdoors, and buffer zones such as unconditioned attics, crawlspaces, and garages. However, for multifamily and single-family attached units, ResStock models leakage as total unit air leakage, which includes airflow to both the exterior and adjacent units. To estimate only the leakage to the exterior, the model scales the total sampled ACH50 by the ratio of the exterior-facing envelope area to the total envelope area. This adjusted exterior-only infiltration rate is then used in the simulation outputs.

In this work, b uildings with infiltration rates ≥6 ACH 50 are eligible for an upgrade to 5 ACH50. This threshold serves as a nationally consistent benchmark across the housing stock. In practice, some new construction households in climate zones (e.g., climate zones 3 -5) require tighter envelopes such as 3 ACH50 or better (Table 2), and many older or leakier homes may only feasibly reach ~5 ACH50 through air sealing (Table 3). In practice, this infiltration level is typically achieved through blower-door-guided air sealing of attics, crawlspaces, rim joists, and other leakage pathways. Previous studies have shown that such practices can bring homes that start above 6 ACH50 down to or below the 5 ACH50 threshold [7] [13]. Therefore, 5 ACH50 provides a balanced and conservative target that represents a meaningful improvement for existing homes, while still allowing comparability across climate zones and building types.

Electrical panel constraint modeling was added to ResStock via OpenStudio-HPXML measures in 2025 to assess whether the existing panel has enough capacity or breaker space to accommodate new loads from an energy retrofit. For details on panel constraint modeling, refer to the ResStock Technical Reference Guide [1]. Advanced air sealing does not introduce new electrical loads or circuits, so it has no impact on the electrical panel. However, electrical panel metrics are included as a standard output for this and all other envelope measures. Any panel constraints appearing in the upgrade results reflect pre-existing conditions, as this measure does not worsen them.
