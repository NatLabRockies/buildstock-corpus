## 4.2  Single Model Time Series

Section 4.1 shows that for the example dwelling units, only heating and cooling related site energy consumption changed as a result of the advanced air sealing upgrades, while other end uses such as lighting, appliances, and water heating remained unchanged. Therefore, this section focuses on 15-minute time-series data for the heating and cooling related end uses across the three example units, as shown below. Heating end uses include space heating, heating fans and pumps, heat pump backup heating, and any associated backup fans or pumps. Cooling end uses include space cooling and cooling fans or pumps. January 2 and July 2 were selected to represent typical winter and summer days for analyzing heating and cooling loads, respectively.

Figure 6 shows that advanced air sealing upgrades consistently reduce modeled cooling site energy use throughout the day for the example unit in Hawai ' i (climate zone 1A). This baseline dwelling unit has an infiltration value of 50 ACH50 and its HVAC system is a SEER1 13, 7.7 HSPF electric heat pump. On July 2 (bottom portion of Figure 6), cooling demand under baseline conditions (50 ACH50) fluctuates from morning through the late evening, peaking at ~1.0 kWh around 6 p.m. After the upgrade from 50 ACH50 to 5 ACH50, cooling energy drops by ~0.45 kWh at peak to ~0.55 kWh. With reduced infiltration, the cooling energy is nearly cut in half as the tighter envelope limits warm, humid air from entering the building. On January 2, both baseline and upgrade scenarios show peak heating occurring around 11 am during the minimum outdoor temperature for the day. The baseline peak heating is ~0.12 kWh while the upgrade peak is ~0.05 kWh, a 58% decrease in peak electricity consumption. This example dwelling shows that infiltration improvements will also decrease peak electricity demand during heating hours in households with heating equipment within climate zone 1A. Note that Hawai'i has a low penetration of heating equipment relative to the rest of the United States [17].

Figure 7 shows that the advanced air sealing upgrade reduces both modeled cooling and heating site energy consumption for the example dwelling unit in Texas (climate zone 3A). On July 2 (cooling season) , the unit's cooling energy use for upgrade follows the trend of baseline 's profile, with consistently lower consumption for the advanced air sealing upgrade. For example, for both scenarios, energy consumption drops after the morning thermostat setpoint increase at 8 a.m. Consumption peaks between 4 and 6 p.m. when outdoor temperatures and cooling loads are typically highest. This indicates that reduced infiltration helped retain cooler indoor air and reduce HVAC runtime during the hottest parts of the day. On January 2 (heating season), heating energy usage for the upgrade scenario follows the trend of the baseline scenario, but the upgrade is consistently lower when keeping thermostat setpoint consistent between upgrade and baseline. This pattern shows that upgrading from 25 ACH50 to 5 ACH50 reduces heating loads for the winter season throughout the day.
