## 3.1  Annual Stock Savings

Figure 25 shows the annual site energy consumption for homes with and without GHP upgrades. Homes with a single-stage GHP upgrade are also shown as a point of comparison for the twostage GHP upgrade. Note that the figures show two sets of dwellings: the top figure includes the entire housing stock, including homes that GHP upgrades do not apply to, in both the baseline and upgrade scenarios. All results are in site energy in TBtu regardless of fuel used. This captures the impact of the upgrades on all homes across the country, which may be of interest to certain stakeholders who care about the entire load post-upgrade. The bottom figure only shows dwellings that could receive a GHP as an upgrade for both the baseline and upgrade scenarios. This result makes it easier to determine the impact of the upgrades on the applicable stock by excluding buildings that do not receive an upgrade. Note that homes that a GHP upgrade does not apply to (most often due to a lack of ducts) make up about 25% of the building stock. Refer to the 'Applicability' section of the single-stage GHP measure documentation to see the full list of criteria that determines which dwellings can receive a GHP as an upgrade.

When two-stage GHPs are considered as an upgrade, this leads to a substantial amount of fuel switching for heating, even when considering the portion of homes where the upgrade does not apply, which leads to an increase in heating electricity consumption. This change is larger than the reduction in electricity consumption resulting from replacing existing cooling equipment with the more efficient two-stage GHP across the entire stock.

For dwellings that can receive a two-stage GHP as an upgrade, cooling electricity consumption is 35% less and heating electricity consumption is 168% larger than the baseline, which still substantially reduces the overall energy consumption of the residential stock when this upgrade is applied. A two-stage GHP also saves substantially more energy than the single-stage GHP considered here, with savings of 12% over the single-stage case when considering the portion of the stock that can receive a GHP and 8% when considering the entire stock.

Figure 26 shows the annual energy consumption of the portion of the housing stock that can receive a GHP upgrade split out by heating fuel type of the baseline buildings. The majority of the stock uses natural gas for heating. All segments reduce energy consumption for both heating and cooling when moving to a GHP upgrade. For homes that use natural gas for heating, twostage GHPs reduce energy use for heating by 76% and cooling by 40% compared to the baseline.

Figure 27 shows the annual energy consumption for the applicable portion of the stock for the baseline, single-stage GHP upgrade, and two-stage GHP upgrade separated by Building America climate zone. GHPs show substantial savings potential in cold climates, which also contain the largest number of dwellings, with two-stage GHPs using 32% less energy for heating than single-stage systems along with some additional cooling savings. Across all climate zones, GHPs may reduce electricity consumption during the cooling seasons but increase electricity consumption during the heating season while saving energy overall due to the higher efficiency of the equipment.
