## 1.1  Introduction to Geothermal Heat Pumps

Geothermal heat pumps (GHPs) are highly efficient systems that provide space heating and cooling by using the relatively stable temperatures found below the Earth's surface. GHPs exchange heat with the ground, where temperatures remain more constant year-round. This enables GHPs to operate efficiently and reliably in a wide range of climates. GHP systems typically consist of three main components: a ground heat exchanger, a heat pump unit, and an air distribution system within the building.

There are several types of GHPs, including single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed systems.

- Single-stage GHPs operate at full capacity whenever they run, which can lead to energy inefficiencies under part-load conditions (when the system needs less than its maximum output).
- Two-stage GHPs improve upon this design by adding a second, lower-capacity stage. This allows the system to operate more efficiently during mild conditions while still having the capability to switch to full output when demand peaks.
- Variable-speed GHPs represent the most advanced option, using inverter-driven compressors and fans to continuously adjust their output in response to real-time heating and cooling needs. Unlike single-stage or two-stage systems that operate in discrete steps, variable-speed systems can modulate across a wide range of capacities. This precise control enhances energy efficiency, provides more stable indoor temperatures, reduces humidity swings, and minimizes noise from cycling.

Like single-stage systems, two-stage GHPs draw on the ground's thermal properties to deliver heating and cooling via a ground heat exchanger and an indoor heat pump. The difference lies in the system's ability to adjust its output. By operating in a low stage under mild conditions, a two-stage GHP reduces energy use, improves comfort by maintaining more stable temperatures and humidity, and can result in quieter operation. When heating or cooling demand increases (such as during extreme weather) the system shifts to high stage for full-capacity operation.

Although two-stage GHPs typically come with higher initial capital costs than single-stage models, they are usually more energy efficient and can lead to lower lifetime operating costs (Trane 2023). Due to these advantages, two-stage systems have become the most commonly installed type of GHP in the residential market, capturing the largest share among GHP technologies according to the technical advisory group (U.S. EPA, 2025).

The intent of this GHP measure is to reflect the characteristics of a typical water-to-air, two-stage GHP selected from the 2025 ENERGY STAR ®  Most Efficient GHP list (U.S. EPA, 2025). The modeled system assumes a vertical ground heat exchanger configuration, which is a common and space-efficient option for residential installations.
