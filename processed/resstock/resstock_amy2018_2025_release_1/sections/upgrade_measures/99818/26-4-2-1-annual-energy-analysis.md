## 4.2.1  Annual Energy Analysis

Figure 3 shows the annual site energy consumption by end use before and after the upgrade for all applicable dwelling units. The annual stock site energy savings from the package are 461 TWh, representing a 24% reduction. Most savings occurred in heating and cooling end uses. These include a 23% site energy reduction in electric heating fans and pumps, 23% site energy reduction in natural gas heating, 22% reduction in propane heating, 21% in electric heating, 21% in fuel oil heating, 18% reduction in cooling fans and pumps, and finally a 16% reduction in electricity for cooling.

Figure 3. Modeled annual site energy consumption for all applicable dwelling units

![Image](99818_images/image_000004_33e9629de1411eb0aa411ed5b81a43a6601ebece358f31b01763bb620121af3d.png)

Figure 4 shows the average annual site energy consumption per dwelling unit by end use before and after the envelope package for upgraded dwelling units. At least one measure in the package was applied to 92.5% of dwelling units. The average annual site energy savings from the envelope package upgrade is 3,570 kWh, representing a 14% reduction.

Figure 4. Modeled average annual site energy consumption per upgraded dwelling unit

![Image](99818_images/image_000005_5c67dc53470d03a040991a1d59d537a3218decc7dd191cf35338b4845e0ef72a.png)

Figure 5 shows that older homes benefit most from the envelope package upgrades. On average, homes built before the 1960s achieve close to 16% annual energy savings, compared to less than 10% for homes built after the 2000s.

Figure 5. Modeled average annual site energy consumption saving percentage per upgraded dwelling unit, grouped by vintage

![Image](99818_images/image_000006_f4b1c423a98498dd7dfb915511b1ff1ddec902cc844117e16c6809db2bb194d2.png)

Figure 6 shows average site energy savings by baseline infiltration rate. Dwelling units with an infiltration rate of 10 ACH50 or above were eligible for all three measures of this package, whereas dwelling units with less than 10 ACH50 were not eligible for the light air sealing upgrade. Therefore, there are higher average site energy savings for infiltration rates 10 ACH50 and above.

Figure 6. Modeled average percent site energy savings per upgraded dwelling unit, grouped by baseline infiltration rate

![Image](99818_images/image_000007_e2baee4eaa779ed9d080b86a39b83a0091eb7542490304a061497591863c0640.png)

Figure 7 displays the average site energy savings per upgraded dwelling unit grouped by baseline attic insulation level. Uninsulated attics show the greatest site energy savings, upwards of 35%. Attic insulation levels of R-49 and R-7 show site energy savings between 14% and 22%, respectively. Dwelling units without attics (shown in the figure as 'None') are not eligible for the attic insulation upgrade but can still show site energy savings due to application of the light air sealing and/or duct sealing and insulation measures.

Figure 7. Modeled average percent site energy savings per upgraded dwelling unit, grouped by baseline attic insulation level

![Image](99818_images/image_000008_dcfb2121adb61ac9c9c6b49611f9ca0bb78bc21d7d8f84ed4f3fb34d6dbf017f.png)

The third measure of this package is duct sealing and insulation, whose average site energy savings are shown in Figure 8, grouped by baseline duct sealing and insulation level. Leakier and less insulated ducts generally show greater site energy savings. Dwelling units without ducts (shown in the figure as 'None') are not eligible for the duct sealing and insulation measure but can still show site energy savings due to application of the light air sealing and/or attic insulation measures.
