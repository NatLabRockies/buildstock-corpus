## 1.2  Technical Measure Details

If this work were to be done as a retrofit, the correct order for implementing this upgrade package is: (1) light air sealing, (2) duct sealing and insulation, and (3) attic floor insulation. The building science behind this ordering has been previously described in another report (Lou, Phoung and Parker 2026), and the information is restated here. Sealing leaks before adding insulation ensures that the insulation performs as intended. It also makes leaks easier to identify and reduces the burden on field staff since they are not working around additional insulation. Once air sealing targets are met, it is essential to address the HVAC system by sealing any leaky ducts to prevent the loss of conditioned air, particularly in unconditioned spaces like attics or crawlspaces. Attic floor insulation should be installed last. While highly effective, it must follow air and duct sealing to avoid covering up critical areas that require sealing and to ensure ducts running through the attic are properly sealed and insulated beforehand.

Therefore, we assume that upgrades will be implemented in the following sequence: light air sealing is applied first to applicable dwelling units, with the goal of achieving a 25% reduction in the infiltration rate measured by an ACH50 change.

After air sealing, HVAC ducts in applicable homes are sealed and insulated to achieve the following:

- A maximum of 5% duct air leakage to the outside
- Duct insulation level of R-8.

Attic floor insulation is the upgraded in applicable dwelling units to the following levels:

- R-49 in climate zone 1
- R-60 in climate zones 2 through 8.

Note: when modeling these upgrades in ResStock, the order does not affect the outputs of the model. All upgrades are applied at the same time to the model file. More details about the modeling assumptions for these three measures can be found in the accompanying documentation (Brossman, White and Phoung 2026) (Phoung, Lou and White 2026) (Lou and Liu 2026).
