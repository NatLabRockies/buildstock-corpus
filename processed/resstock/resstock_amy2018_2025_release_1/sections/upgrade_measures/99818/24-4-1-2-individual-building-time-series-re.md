## 4.1.2 Individual Building Time-Series Results

This section shows heating and cooling energy impact from two select days, January 3 as a representative winter day and July 11 for a representative summer day. These timeseries values are from the AMY 2018 dataset. Heating end uses include heating and heating fans/pumps. Cooling end uses include cooling and cooling fans/pumps.

Figure 1 shows the time-series heating energy consumption, which in this case comes from a natural gas furnace. This upgrade, shown in orange, consistently lowers the required heating energy throughout the whole day with the most significant impact during the early morning.

Figure 1. Modeled heating energy impact from the upgrade measure for one example dwelling unit model

Figure 1: line chart titled 'Winter Heating Energy: January 3-4, 2018'. X-axis is time over one day; y-axis is heating energy consumption in kWh, 0.5 to above 2.5. Series are Baseline and Upgrade. The upgrade runs at roughly half the baseline all day: about 1.2 kWh against 2.2 overnight, about 1.5 against 2.7 at the morning peak, and about 0.4 against 0.6 in the afternoon.

Figure 2 shows the time-series cooling energy consumption. This dwelling unit has a central air conditioning system. In general, the upgrade slightly lowers the required cooling energy consumption during the whole day. The sudden decrease in cooling energy around 12pm is due to thermostat changes. The largest impact is seen from the late afternoon into evening.
