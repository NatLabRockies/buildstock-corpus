## 4.3  Stock Energy Impacts

Out of 550,000 model building simulations (i.e., models), 31,876 models are applicable for the propane and fuel oil furnace upgrade (5.8%), representing the 139,647,020 dwellings from American Community Survey's 2021, 5 -year estimate of U.S. households (not including territories). For the 31,876 applicable units, 13,200 use fuel oil and 18,676 use propane. For the applicable models, the total baseline consumption is 302 billion kWh per year, and after the propane and fuel oil furnace upgrade, the total consumption is 278 billion kWh per year, a 7.9% savings.

Figure 6 shows that energy savings from the propane and fuel oil furnace upgrade mostly come from heating-related end uses.

Figure 6: horizontal stacked bar chart of stock-wide all-fuels end uses (kWh, 0 to about 160B), with a Baseline bar and a Propane/Oil Furnace bar for each heating fuel, Fuel Oil and Propane, and segments for each end use and fuel. Heating is by far the largest segment in every bar. Fuel oil homes fall from about 140B to 130B kWh, and propane homes from about 162B to 148B kWh.

In dwellings heated with fuel oil, energy used for heating from fuel oil reduced 9.3%, from 98.2 billion kWh to 89.1 billion kWh after the upgrade. In dwellings heated with propane, energy used for heating from propane reduced 13.1%, from 101.4 billion kWh to 88.1 billion kWh after the upgrade.

100% of energy savings for dwellings heated with fuel oil is sourced from fuel oil heating end uses. While 93% of average annual energy savings in dwellings heated with propane come from propane heating, cooling fans pumps using electricity composes 3%, heating fans pumps using electricity compose 3%, and cooling using electricity compose 1% of annual average energy savings, as shown in Figure 7. As mentioned in the Modeling Approach section, installation of the propane furnace can improve infiltration, which explains these energy savings. Because of this slight improvement in performance in propane furnace households, an annual average of 8.8 hours of unmet cooling hours are saved.

Figure 8: violin plots with box plots of site energy total consumption savings per dwelling (kWh, 0 to 35k), one row per baseline HVAC heating efficiency: Fuel Furnace 60%, 76%, 80% and 92.5% AFUE, each labelled with its model count. Savings shrink as baseline efficiency rises: the median is about 8k kWh at 60% AFUE, about 4k at 76%, about 3k at 80%, and under 1k at 92.5% AFUE.

In Figure 8, the less efficient furnaces -60% AFUE fuel furnaces -show a wider range and higher median of energy saving (8,182 kWh) than higher-efficiency furnaces like 92.5% AFUE furnaces (411 kWh). By totals, opportunities for energy savings are greatest in the Middle Atlantic, East North Central, and New England Census Divisions, as shown in Figure 9.

Figure 9: horizontal stacked bar chart of stock-wide fuel totals savings (kWh, 0 to about 5.5B) by U.S. census division for the Propane/Oil Furnace upgrade, with segments for electricity, natural gas, propane and fuel oil. After the upgrade, the Middle Atlantic region could save up to 5.33 billion kWh, the East North Central region could save up to 5.27 billion kWh, and the New England area could save up to 4.45 billion kWh.
