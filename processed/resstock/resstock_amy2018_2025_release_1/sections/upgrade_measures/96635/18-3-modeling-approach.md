## 3 Modeling Approach

This section outlines how propane and fuel oil furnace upgrades are modeled in ResStock using the OpenStudio-HPXML workflow. ResStock assigns each dwelling unit a set of building characteristics, including heating system type, efficiency, and fuel source, based on probabilistic sampling logic informed by national housing stock data. Furnaces are modeled as forced-air heating systems that distribute heat through ductwork. The primary variable representing an upgrade is AFUE, which characterizes how efficiently the furnace converts fuel to usable heat over a heating season. Ducts remain unchanged when heating systems are upgraded. ResStock models fuel type (e.g., propane or natural gas) explicitly, influencing energy use calculations. The model assumes consistent operation patterns across households, and scales furnace capacity based on heating load requirements determined by building envelope characteristics and climate zone. While equipment brand and installation quality are not modeled explicitly, the total heating energy demand and fuel consumption are recalculated to reflect the standardized furnace and fuel use.

These probability distributions are sampled to create a synthetic stock where each sample has an assigned option for each characteristic for each dwelling unit to be simulated. ResStock utilizes the OpenStudio-HPXML workflow to translate these dwelling characteristics into building models ready to be simulated via OpenStudio ®  and EnergyPlus ®  through the use of an HPXML file. For reference, the state of the OpenStudio-HPXML repository when the ResStock 2025 Release 1 dataset was created can be found here. Assumptions about the oil and propane furnace upgrades that are not explicitly defined in ResStock are taken care of in the BuildResidentialHPXML measure.
