## 4.2  Aggregated Building Time Series

Colorado is selected as a case study to analyze the aggregated time-series data. Out of all national dwelling units, 1.7% are in Colorado. Figure 3 presents the aggregated average daily baseline energy consumption in Colorado, with February 20 identified as the day with the highest energy use. To further explore this peak, Figure 4 examines the aggregated average power on February 20 in Colorado, comparing conditions before and after the upgrade.

Figure 3. Aggregated daily average energy consumption of baseline in Colorado

![Image](96635_images/image_000004_538d935932dd93a1352854631272445e9b1763786fe028fc53a6c979164cb59b.png)

Figure 4 shows that the upgrade scenario does not result in drastically different power demand throughout the day compared to the baseline.

Figure 4. Aggregated average power data in Colorado on peak day

![Image](96635_images/image_000005_ced61bb2204e6e95af901b6f8530080acf23441429a7091d455ce356cc34d28c.png)

The aggregated average power does not change significantly after the upgrade because it is focused on fuel furnaces. The average power demand shows slight efficiency gains in electricity demand. Figure 5 shows a more granular electricity time series of a dwelling receiving the upgrade, where the propane furnace efficiency increased from 80% AFUE to 95% AFUE, but has little effect on the average electricity demand.
