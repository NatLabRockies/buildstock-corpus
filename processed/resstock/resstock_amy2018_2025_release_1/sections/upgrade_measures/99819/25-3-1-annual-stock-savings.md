## 3.1  Annual Stock Savings

Figure 1 shows the annual site energy consumption for all dwelling units, regardless of whether they got the upgrade, for the baseline and two-stage GHP with light envelope improvements package. Figure 2 shows the annual site energy consumption only for dwelling units that received the upgrade. For this upgrade, about 71% of the residential housing stock received the upgrade.

In each of the two figures, significant energy savings come from heating energy savings from natural gas, propane, and fuel oil. Cooling electricity also shows savings. Electric heating increases in both cases, and this is due to fuel switching from traditional heating systems to fully electric GHPs. Figure 1 shows 35% stock site energy savings, while Figure 2 shows 45% site energy savings in applicable dwelling units.

Figure 3 shows the annual site energy consumption for the applicable housing stock split out by Building America climate zone (Antonopoulos, et al. 2022). As envelope improvements reduce loads, savings are seen in all climate zones. The largest magnitude savings are seen in the MixedHumid and the Cold climate zones. The reduction in heating load and associated energy consumption provides most of the savings in these climate zones. Note that for all climate zones, installing a two-stage GHP is the primary driver of savings, while additional envelope improvements further increase the savings. Looking more closely at individual end uses, all climate zones show an increase in electricity consumption for heating fans and pumps, and conversely all climate zones, except Subarctic, show savings from cooling.

Figure 5 shows the annual energy consumption broken out by the baseline heating fuel. Dwellings with each baseline heating fuel show energy savings. Dwellings with existing electric heating are replacing electric resistance equipment, such as an electric furnace, with a more efficient GHP. Note that because ducts are required in the baseline for this upgrade to apply, homes with electric baseboards are excluded here. The largest absolute savings occurs in homes that use natural gas for heating, which also make up most of the applicable housing stock. In these homes, the traditional natural gas heating system is replaced by a fully electric GHP, leading to an increase in the electric heating end use. However, the overall efficiency improvements in both beating and cooling result in total site energy savings.
