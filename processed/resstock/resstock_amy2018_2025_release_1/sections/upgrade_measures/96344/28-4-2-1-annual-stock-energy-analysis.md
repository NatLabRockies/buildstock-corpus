## 4.2.1  Annual Stock Energy Analysis

Figure 3 shows the annual site energy consumption by end use before and after the duct sealing and insulation upgrade for all homes. The annual stock energy savings is 240 TWh, compared to a pre-upgrade consumption of 3,454 TWh-representing a 7% reduction. Most savings occurred in heating and cooling end uses. These include 8% (22 TWh) electricity heating savings, 11% (129 TWh) natural gas heating savings, 8% (14 TWh) fuel oil heating savings, 10% (13 TWh) propane heating savings, 35% (8 TWh) electricity heat pump backup heating savings, 11% (43 TWh) electricity cooling savings, 12% (4 TWh) electricity heating fans and pumps savings, and 13% (9 TWh) electricity cooling fans and pumps savings.

Table 5 shows the annual energy consumption savings percentage per upgraded dwelling unit. As discussed earlier, most savings occurred in heating and cooling end uses. However, there is only a small change in hot water-related end uses. When both the water heater and ducts are in the same place (e.g., unheated basement, attic), hot water energy consumption can be slightly affected due to reduced heat transfer from the ducts. Furthermore, changes in indoor temperature also slightly impact hot water energy consumption. Overall, the effect on hot water energy use is minimal-less than 1%.

There is also a very small change in appliance electricity use, primarily due to refrigerators. Refrigerators transfer heat from the interior to the surrounding air. When the surrounding air is cooler, refrigerators operate more efficiently and use less energy. The average indoor temperature over the year decreased slightly from 71.28°F to 71.26°F. This marginal cooling results in a slight reduction in refrigerator energy use. However, the impact is negligible-the change in appliance electricity consumption is 0.01%.

Figure 5 shows that duct sealing and insulation upgrades yield the highest energy savings in homes with poor baseline duct conditions, especially those with high leakage and no insulation. For a given leakage level, uninsulated ducts result in the greatest energy savings, followed by ducts with R-4, then R-6 and R-8 insulation. Since ducts with R-6 and R-8 insulation are only sealed (not further insulated) in this upgrade, they yield similar savings. Conversely, for a given insulation level, higher duct leakage in baseline dwelling unit consistently leads to greater energy savings.
