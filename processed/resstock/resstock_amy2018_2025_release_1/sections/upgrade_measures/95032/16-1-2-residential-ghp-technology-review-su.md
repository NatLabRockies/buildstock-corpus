## 1.2 Residential GHP Technology Review Summary

A review of GHPs in the U.S. building stock was performed as part of this work. A detailed discussion can be found in Appendix A. The review includes: (1) the number of GHP installations, where they were located, and the building types and when the homes were built; (2) the most common mechanical systems used in residential GHP installations; (3) the GHPs' heating and cooling performance; and (4) the common ground heat exchanger configurations and the frequency of installation. Key takeaways from the review are as follows:

- There are approximately 1.28 -2 million GHPs in the U.S. residential building stock (Gertler, Steeves and Wang 2025)
- Tennessee, North Carolina, Florida, Texas, and Alabama make up almost 40% of residential GHP installations. Almost 85% of GHPs are in the IECC 2012 (Burleyson, 2012) moist climate zones, 2A -5A. Climate zone 4A has the most GHP installations, with about 30% of total installations (U.S. EIA 2023).
- About 70% of GHP installations are in homes that were built since the 1980s and primarily are in single-family housing (U.S. EIA 2023).
- Approximately 75% of GHP installations are in new construction and 25% of GHP installations are in retrofit systems (U.S. EIA 2023).
- The most common ground heat exchanger configurations are vertical and closed-loop systems (Battocletti and Glassley 2013).
- Water-to-air heat pumps are the most common type of geothermal mechanical equipment used to deliver heating and cooling to residential buildings (Battocletti and Glassley 2013).
