## Residential GHPs Installations by Various Housing Characteristics

Characteristics about the housing units with GHPs also illuminate where the market has higher or lower penetrations. Figure A-4 illustrates that residential GHP installations in the U.S. are focused primarily in the single-family detached and mobile home housing stock. There are fewer GHP installations in multifamily apartment buildings with 5 or more units relative to the U.S. residential stock.

The RECS 2020 data shows that there are fewer GHP installations in the pre-1980s residential stock (Figure A-5). Residential building stock housing units built before 1980 makes up roughly 54% of the U.S. residential building stock. Approximately 1.9% of the housing units built after 1980 have GHP installations compared to 0.7% built before 1980. This indicates that newer homes are more likely to have GHPs installed. Approximately 75% of residential GHP installations are in new construction, while 25% are retrofits (Liu, et al. 2019).

Targeting only new construction to install GHPs would be limiting. According to the American Community Survey, the number of residential housing units has grown by about 0.7% each year over the last 10 years (U.S. Census 2021). There are a large number of homes in the existing U.S. residential building stock in which GHPs could be installed if the land area is available for installation of the ground heat exchanger.
