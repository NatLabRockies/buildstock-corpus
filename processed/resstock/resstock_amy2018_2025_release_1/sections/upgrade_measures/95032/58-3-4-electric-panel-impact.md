## 3.4  Electric Panel Impact

This section details the impact the single-stage GHP upgrade will have on electrical panels.

First, Figure 28 and Figure 29 show information about the ResStock 2025 Release 1 baseline housing stock electric panel distribution. Figure 28 shows in the first column, 'All', that about 35% of all dwelling units have 0 or only 1 breaker space available. Figure 29 shows in the first column that 97.7% of electric panels have no existing capacity constraint for the baseline housing stock, but that the other 2.3% of electric panels already have an existing capacity constraint.

Capacity constraints in the existing baseline stock are due in part to the uncertainty associated with the modeling process, where the prediction of the panel size is not based on the presence of all major electric appliances. However, it's important to recognize that this level of uncertainty applies to all panel results, including those that look reasonable. Hence, the capacity constrained homes in the baseline can be thought of as over-capacity homes that experience frequent flickering of lights or tripping of breakers if/when large appliances are turned on.

Before this single-stage GHP retrofit would take place, the panels experiencing a capacity constraint would likely need to address this issue first. Users are encouraged to alternatively analyze the constraint outcomes by excluding homes whose pre-existing capacity constraint is not worsened by the upgrade.

Figure 30 shows the distribution of panel constraints overall, see the first column, and then by heating fuel for dwelling units that receive the single-stage GHP. It is estimated that about 38% of dwelling units upgrading to a single-stage GHP will experience either a capacity and/or space constraint, and the other 62% will experience no constraint. Looking more closely at the results and understanding that natural gas is the predominant heating fuel across the U.S. as seen in Figure 20, these results are heavily weighted by dwelling units that have natural gas as their original heating fuel.

Figure 31 shows a range of 11% -70% of dwelling units that would have either a capacity and/or space constraint after the single-stage GHP. Specifically looking at space constraints, Figure 32 shows a smaller range from about 4% to 53% of dwelling units that would have a space constraint. Finally, Figure 33 shows a range of roughly 7% to 48% of dwelling units that would have a capacity constraint.
