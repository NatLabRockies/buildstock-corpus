## 3.1 Annual Stock Savings

Overall stock-level energy consumption for baseline and Single Stage GHP is shown in Figure 18 (full stock) and Figure 19. Single-stage GHPs reduce site energy consumption 24.3% at the stock level (Figure 18), and by 31.3% in dwellings applicable to the GHP upgrade (Figure 19). All results are site energy in TBtu regardless of fuel used. Results are filtered such that the baseline contains only buildings for which the GHP upgrade is applicable. Refer to Section 2.1 for the applicability criteria for GHPs. GHP upgrades were applicable to 71% of the homes included in the ResStock 2025 Release 1. 38 Heating energy decreases by 45% for these homes, but electricity used for space heating more than doubles when GHPs are installed due to the substantial amount of fuel switching away from natural gas (and to a lesser extent, fuel oil and propane). Cooling energy consumption decreases by 19%. Note that the baseline includes homes that do not have cooling or are partially conditioned by room air conditioners (but use ducted heating systems, so a GHP upgrade is still applicable), in which the GHP upgrade increases energy consumption for cooling. Less than 100% of the space is cooled for about 33% of the homes with cooling equipment. While GHPs are more efficient than room air conditioners, cooling now also has associated duct losses in this case. Duct losses can range from 0%, no loss, to 30% loss in ResStock.

[38 More information on ResStock sampling can be seen in Sections 6.3 and 7.3 in https://nrel.github.io/ResStock.github.io/assets/trd/ResStockTechnicalReferenceGuide\_2025\_1.pdf.](https://nrel.github.io/ResStock.github.io/assets/trd/ResStockTechnicalReferenceGuide_2025_1.pdf)

Figure 20 shows the energy consumption of baseline and GHP upgrade cases disaggregated by baseline heating fuel. Natural gas is used by the majority of the stock and therefore makes up the majority of the residential stock. Propane and fuel oil are the least common, primarily used in more rural areas without access to natural gas infrastructure. Fuel oil is generally used in the New England region and rarely used in other parts of the country. For homes with natural gas, installing a single-stage GHP reduces heating energy by 50% due to the higher efficiency of the GHP relative to the baseline equipment, primarily furnaces. Note that heating fan and pump energy do increase substantially, largely attributable to the pump energy use associated with the GHPs, but these increases have a minor impact relative to the overall savings of the more efficient equipment. Cooling energy savings are much less substantial due to the much smaller difference in the efficiency of equipment used in the baseline and upgrade cases across the stock and because some units do not have cooling in the baseline stock.

Figure 21 shows the baseline and upgrade energy savings disaggregated by Building America climate zone. Savings occur across all climate zones for both heating and cooling. However, overall electricity consumption increases substantially in cold and mixed-humid climates, largely due to replacing non-electric heating with electric heating. Cooling energy consumption decreases across all climate zones, with the most substantial savings occurring in hot-humid climates.
