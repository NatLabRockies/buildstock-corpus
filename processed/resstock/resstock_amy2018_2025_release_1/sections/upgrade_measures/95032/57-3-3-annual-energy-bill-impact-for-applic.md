## 3.3  Annual Energy Bill Impact for Applicable Dwelling Units Only

Energy bills for applicable dwelling units only are shown below in the following figures. On average, energy bills decreased 3% for the single-stage GHP. This is shown in Figure 26. Electricity bills increased slightly, while natural gas, fuel oil, and propane bills all decreased. Electric bills increasing on average is to be expected because of fuel switching from non-electric heating to electric heating, and then some units receiving cooling if they did not have air conditioning before the single-stage GHP.

Figure 26: horizontal grouped bar chart. X-axis is bills in USD, 0 to about 2,800; rows are electricity, natural gas, fuel oil, propane and all fuels (labelled out.bills.electricity.usd and so on), each with Baseline and 'Single-Speed GHP' bars. Electricity rises from about 2,000 to about 2,500 USD, natural gas falls from about 560 to about 180, and the all-fuels total falls slightly.

Figure 27 shows the average annual utility bill broken out by Building America climate zone. Energy bills generally decrease in six of the eight climate zones. However, Hot-Dry and Cold only experience slight increases, likely due to increased electricity usage, which is generally more expensive than natural gas.
