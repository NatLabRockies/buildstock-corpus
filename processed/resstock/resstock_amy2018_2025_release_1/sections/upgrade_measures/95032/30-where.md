## Where:

Q\_ft : Heating/cooling output capacity modifier based on temperature

EIR\_fT : Nominal energy input ratio modifier based on temperature. EIR is equal to 1/COP

Tair : Dry-bulb temperature of return air entering the heat pump

Twater : Incoming water temperature entering the heat pump

a-k

: Curve coefficients

These biquadratic curves have the same form as curves used for air-to-air heat pumps and residential heat pump water heaters in EnergyPlus and is the form of curves used with the EnergyPlus object 'Coil:Heating:WaterToAirHeatPump:VariableSpeedEquationFit' and its cooling counterpart. For GHPs, the curves are a function of incoming water temperature and indoor air temperature, while for an air-to-air heat pump they are a function of the indoor and outdoor air temperature. Other than difference in independent variables, the curves are identical in form. EnergyPlus also includes a single-stage version of the object, which uses curves of a different form. The variable-speed object allows inputs for one to ten different speeds. The single-stage version of the model uses only inputs for a single stage. Other models use different numbers of speeds, with details included in their measure documents.

OpenStudio-HPXML has an existing single-stage GHP model that uses the single-stage version of this object 'Coil:Heating:WaterToAirHeatPump:EquationFit' and its cooling counterpart. To preserve this existing model while also adding the new model, users can choose which version of the GHP model they'd like to use. This enables the new model to be used as desired in ResStock, while not changing results for users who were using the existing model outside of ResStock but within OS-HPXML dependent workflows.

16 GHPs typically have three sets of rated conditions in their technical documentation-for water loops, ground heat exchangers, and ground water-with different rated conditions (ISO 2021). The values shown here, and expected in OpenStudio-HPXML, are for ground heat exchanger configurations.

To generate the performance curves, the specification sheet for the 3-ton single-stage Tranquility TS GHP was used (see Figure 2). The 3-ton unit was selected because it is consistent with prior work, both in the thesis (Tang 2005) previously used to generate performance curves and the prior implementation in OpenStudio-HPXML. The product manual for this unit also includes correction factors for performance at different airflow rates, entering air temperatures (both wet bulb and dry bulb for cooling performance), and different concentrations of antifreeze. This allows the specification sheet to be expanded to additional points covering the full range of conditions that may occur during simulation. OpenStudio-HPXML assumes by default that a 20% mix of propylene glycol and water is used when simulating GHPs, so the antifreeze correction was applied to all curves. This is a conservative assumption that attempts to always keep the freezing point of the fluid below the ground temperatures likely to be seen in simulation, regardless of climate zone. This trades off performance for ensuring that the system will never fail due to hitting freezing conditions for the circulating fluid in a configuration with unexpectedly low ground temperatures.

To generate inputs necessary to go from catalog data to performance curves for EnergyPlus, the spec sheet data based on incoming temperatures was expanded using the multipliers for indoor air temperature corrections. Air temperature corrections account for less impactful differences in return air temperature that may occur due to difference in setpoint or ducting configuration and generally have less impact than the curves that depend on the incoming fluid temperature due to the narrower range of conditions seen. However, accounting for this factor allows for a higher number of points to be generated and used when determining curve fits, providing better data for modeling. All of the air temperature correction factors were applied, and airflow corrections above and below the rated airflow rate were also applied. This led to a total of 450 unique points for heating and 462 unique points for cooling. The data point taken as the nominal condition for the modeled cooling is entering water temperature: 70°F, entering air temperature: 80°F dry bulb/66°F wet bulb, airflow rate: 1,200 cfm, water flow rate: 9 gallons/minute. The data point taken as the nominal condition for heating is entering water temperature 70°F, entering air temperature 70°F, airflow rate 1,200 cfm, water flow rate 9 gallons/minute. The values used as nominal for curve fitting and in the coil objects in EnergyPlus must be consistent, but the exact values can be somewhat arbitrary.
