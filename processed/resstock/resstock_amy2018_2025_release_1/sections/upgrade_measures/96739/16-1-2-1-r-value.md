## 1.2.1 R-Value

To measure and evaluate the effectiveness of attic floor insulation for unfinished attics, the primary consideration is thermal resistance, which is measured by R-value (ft²·°F·hr/Btu). The R-value quantifies the insulation's resistance to heat flow; higher values indicating greater insulating effectiveness. R-values are standardized and widely recognized by energy codes to ensure consistency and comparability, such as the International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) [7], [8]. For attic floor insulation specifically, recommended R-values vary by climate zone, ranging from R-30 in warmer climates to R-60 or higher in colder regions [8]. The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) provides specific recommendations for attic insulation R-values based on climate zones; see details in Table 2. As shown in Table 2, units with uninsulated attics are recommended to install more insulation to achieve higher final R-values. In contrast, homes with 3-4 inches of existing insulation (approximately R-11 to R-15) show the amount of additional insulation that should be added, not the final total R-value. Table 3 summarizes achieved R-values from different empirical studies. These tables serve as reference points for retrofit applications.
