## 4.2  Single Model Time Series

Section 4.1 shows that, for the example dwelling units, only heating- and cooling-related energy consumption changed as a result of the attic floor insulation upgrades. Therefore, this section focuses on 15-minute time-series data for those end uses across the three example units.  Heating end uses include space heating, heating fans and pumps, heat pump backup heating, and any associated backup fans or pumps. Cooling end uses include space cooling and cooling fans or pumps. January 2 and July 2 were selected to represent typical winter and summer days for analyzing heating and cooling loads, respectively.

Figure 6 shows that for unit 1 in Florida (climate zone 1A), hot-humid climate, the example dwelling unit uses electricity for both heating and cooling. As shown in the top panel of Figure 6, the attic insulation upgrade reduces cooling energy consumption during the afternoon hours when outdoor temperatures are highest. The improved attic insulation lowers heat gain, resulting in less demand on the cooling system. On the winter day (bottom panel), heating energy use remains near zero throughout the day. This is consistent with the minimal heating demand typical of zone 1A, where 28% of homes in Florida either don't have or don't use heating equipment [12]. These results show that while insulation has limited effect on heating in warm climates, it does help reduce summer cooling loads.

Figure 7 shows that unit 2 in Texas (climate zone 3A), mixed-humid climate, sees both winter and summer thermal loads. In the top panel of Figure 7 (July 2), cooling energy consumption is consistently lower for the upgraded scenario, especially in the afternoon and evening when outdoor temperatures are highest. On the winter day (bottom panel), heating energy use is reduced throughout the day, with more noticeable differences during the morning recovery period after the nighttime setback. These results show that insulation helps reduce both heat gain in summer and heat loss in winter. Notably, Texas shows larger absolute reductions in both heating and cooling compared to Florida, reflecting its higher thermal variability.
