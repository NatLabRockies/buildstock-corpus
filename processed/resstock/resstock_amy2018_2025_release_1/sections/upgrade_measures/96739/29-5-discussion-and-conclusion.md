## 5  Discussion and Conclusion

This analysis shows that attic floor insulation upgrades yield small energy savings across climate zones. Among the 235,950 applicable units (42.9%), total energy consumption decreased by 2.2%, with savings realized in heating and cooling end uses. As expected, greater absolute and percent savings were observed in older homes, colder climates, and units with lower baseline insulation levels. In many homes, particularly those with baseline insulation near R-30 or higher, the incremental improvement to R-49 or R-60 results in smaller incremental energy savings. As insulation levels increase, each additional increment of R-value produces smaller marginal reductions in heat flow. Therefore, from an energy-savings perspective, attic insulation alone may not always produce strong return on investment (ROI), especially in newer homes or milder climates.

That said, focusing only on annual energy savings may undervalue the measure. Attic insulation improves indoor temperature stability, reduces peak load intensity, and enhances occupant comfort by limiting radiant heat transfer and surface temperature asymmetry. In colder climates, increased insulation can reduce risks associated with condensation and moisture-related durability issues. In hot climates, it reduces attic-to-living-space heat gain during peak afternoon hours. These comfort and resilience benefits are not captured in site energy metrics but are often drivers of homeowner decision-making. Another important consideration is measure interaction. The attic floor insulation may be more impactful when implemented as part of a broader envelope package, such as combining insulation with air sealing, duct sealing, or HVAC upgrades.
