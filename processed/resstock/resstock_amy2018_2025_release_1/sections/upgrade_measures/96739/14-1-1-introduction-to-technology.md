## 1.1  Introduction to Technology

Attic floor insulation for unfinished attics involves placing insulation material between or over the attic floor joists to improve the thermal boundary between the living spaces below and the unconditioned attic spaces above. The primary intention behind attic insulation is to reduce heat transfer to improve energy efficiency, lower heating and cooling costs, increase thermal comfort, and managing moisture within the home [3], [4], [5]. Common attic floor insulation methods for unfinished attics include batt and roll insulation, loose-fill and blown-in insulation, and sprayed foam and foamed-in-place [5]. Batt and roll insulation, typically made from fiberglass, mineral wool, or cotton, is straightforward to install between attic floor joists but must be fitted carefully to avoid gaps or compression, which can compromise performance. Loose-fill (blown-in) insulation, typically made from cellulose, fiberglass, and mineral wool, is typically blown into place using special equipment. Sprayed foam and foamed-in-place, typically made with cementitious, phenolic, polyisocyanurate, and polyurethane materials, is applied using either lowpressure kits (commonly used for small-scale applications) or high-pressure sprayers for larger coverage areas. Both types are pressurized and expand on contact to fill gaps and irregular spaces. All of these methods are effective ways to add insulation in existing unfinished areas with irregular shape; see Table 1 for more details [3], [5].

Note that prior to insulating, attic air sealing is recommended for maximizing insulation effectiveness and reducing air-transported moisture from the conditioned space into the attic. Air sealing reduces air infiltration to help ensure that insulation performs optimally without compromising thermal resistance due to air leakage [3]. Refer to the air sealing documentation for more details [6]
