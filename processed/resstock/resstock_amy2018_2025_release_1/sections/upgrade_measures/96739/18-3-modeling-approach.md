## 3  Modeling Approach

This section outlines how the attic floor insulation upgrade is modeled in ResStock using the OpenStudio-HPXML workflow. ResStock assigns each dwelling unit a set of building characteristics. This includes attic type, insulation level, and climate zone by using probabilistic sampling logic informed by national housing stock data. Attic floor insulation is modeled as R-value for the ceiling assembly. These values reflect insulation material installed between and/or over the attic floor joists in unfinished, vented attic spaces. This modeling assumes that insulation is installed uniformly across the attic floor and that the attic remains unconditioned and vented. Attic insulation performance is influenced by the insulation material and its installed thickness, both of which determine the R-value. However, ResStock does not explicitly model material types; it focuses instead on the total thermal resistance for the entire assembly.

Electrical panel constraint modeling was added to ResStock via OpenStudio-HPXML measures in 2025 to assess whether the existing panel has enough capacity or breaker space to accommodate new loads from an energy retrofit. For details on panel constraint modeling, refer to the ResStock Technical Reference Guide [1]. Attic floor insulation does not introduce new electrical loads or circuits, so they have no impact on the electrical panel. However, electrical panel metrics are included as a standard output for this and all other envelope measures. Any panel constraints appearing in the upgrade results reflect pre-existing conditions, as this measure does not worsen them.
