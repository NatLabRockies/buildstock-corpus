## 2.5  Attic Insulation, Light Air Sealing, and Duct Sealing

Each individual component of this measure is documented separately (Phoung, Lou, &amp; White, 2026) (Brossman, Phoung, &amp; White, 2026) (Lou, Liu, &amp; Parker, 2026) and also documented as a package (Brossman &amp; White, 2026).

The rest of this section is restated here from previous work done on the GHP and envelope measure (Maguire, et al., 2026). The goal of this package is to capture efficiency measures that can be applied to existing buildings. This is similar to what might be done as part of weatherization upgrades to a home, where the easiest and most beneficial improvements are made.

This upgrade package integrates three coordinated energy efficiency measures: light air sealing, attic floor insulation, and duct sealing and insulation, to address sources of energy loss in a home. Air sealing targets gaps, cracks, and penetrations in the building envelope to reduce uncontrolled air leakage, improve comfort, and lessen the workload on heating and cooling systems. Attic floor insulation slows heat transfer between living areas and unconditioned attic spaces, keeping heat inside in winter and out in summer. Duct sealing and insulation prevents conditioned air from escaping into unconditioned spaces through leaky ducts, improving HVAC efficiency, maintaining more even indoor temperatures, and protecting indoor air quality. While each measure provides benefits individually, their combined effect is greater, making them most effective when implemented as a package.

The recommended implementation sequence is based on building science principles. First, light air sealing is applied to achieve a 25% infiltration reduction, followed by duct sealing and insulating to reduce leakage to a maximum of 5% and achieve R-8 duct insulation, and finally upgrading attic floor insulation. This order ensures air leaks are addressed before insulation is installed, prevents ducts from being buried before sealing, and optimizes the performance of both insulation and the HVAC system. By following this sequence, field staff can more easily identify and seal problem areas and avoid rework.

Together, these measures improve comfort, lower energy bills, and enhance indoor air quality throughout the year. Air sealing stabilizes indoor temperatures and minimizes drafts, duct sealing reduces waste and protects against contaminants entering the system, and attic insulation locks in the conditioned air for longer periods. When applied together, they create a tighter, better-insulated, and more efficient home, with benefits that are greater and more durable than any single measure alone.
