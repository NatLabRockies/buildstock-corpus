## Executive Summary

The goal of this work is to develop energy efficiency, demand flexibility, and other retrofit end-use load shapes (electricity, gas, propane, or fuel oil) that cover most high-impact, market-ready (or nearly market-ready) measures. 'Measures' refers to retrofits that can be applied to buildings during modeling.

An 'end-use savings shape' is the difference in energy consumption between a baseline building and a building with an energy efficiency, demand flexibility, or other retrofit measure applied. It results in a time-series profile broken down by end use and fuel (electricity or on-site gas, propane, or fuel oil) at each time step.

ResStock™ is a highly granular, physics-based, bottom-up model that uses multiple data sources, statistical sampling methods, and advanced building energy simulations to estimate the annual subhourly energy consumption of the residential building stock across the United States. The baseline model represents the U.S. residential building stock as it existed in 2018. Technical documentation for the inputs and assumptions in the baseline building stock model is available in Reyna et al. (Reyna, et al., 2025). Calibration and validation of the baseline model results are available in the final technical report of the End-Use Load Profiles project (Wilson, et al., 2022).

This document focuses on a single end-use savings shape measure: Residential Variable-Speed Geothermal Heat Pump (GHP) (4.4 Coefficient of Performance [COP], 30.9 Energy Efficiency Ratio [EER]) With Light Envelope Improvements. This measure combines a variable-speed GHP with light air sealing, attic floor insulation, and duct sealing and insulation as a single package. Because this package is a combination of two other measures, this document focuses on the results associated with this combination of technologies, while individual measure documents for the variable-speed GHP and light envelope improvements package provide methodology and results for those measures.

This upgrade package demonstrates 39% modeled total site energy savings (4.62 quadrillion Btu [quads]) for the U.S. residential building stock. These savings are primarily attributed to:

- 80% stock natural gas heating savings (3.14 quads)
- 64% stock cooling savings (0.85 quads)
- 65% stock fuel oil heating savings (0.40 quads)
- 80% stock propane heating savings (0.36 quads)
- 23% stock electricity heating increase (0.20 quads).
