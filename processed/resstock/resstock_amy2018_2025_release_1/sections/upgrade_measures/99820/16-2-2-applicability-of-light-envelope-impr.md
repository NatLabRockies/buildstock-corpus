## 2.2  Applicability of Light Envelope Improvements Package

The information in this section is repeated here from a previous report (Brossman, White, &amp; Lou, 2026). While envelope improvements are included for any home receiving a variable-speed GHP, the baseline conditions must warrant an improvement for each component of the upgrade to apply.

For the light air sealing upgrades to apply, the infiltration rate of the baseline home must be at least 10 air changes per hour at 50 pascals (ACH50). The light air sealing upgrade is then applied to reach a 25% infiltration reduction. More details on air sealing applicability can be found in the light air sealing measure document (Brossman, Phoung, &amp; White, 2026).

For duct sealing and insulation, the upgrade case installs R-6 duct insulation if existing ducts have an insulation level of R-4 or worse. Ducts are sealed to 5% leakage if there is more than 6% leakage in the baseline. These improvements can be installed independently of each other, or both may apply if both the leakage and insulation criteria are met. More details on duct sealing and insulation applicability can be found in the duct improvement measure document (Lou, Liu, &amp; Parker, 2026).

For attic insulation, this measure upgrades attic floor insulation in unfinished attics where the existing R-value is more than 10 below the maximum level recommended by the IECC for each unit's climate zone. The post-retrofit R-value is set to reflect the maximum value recommended by code. For example, while the IECC recommends an R-value range of R-49 to R-60 for climate zone 2, dwelling units in this zone are upgraded to R-60 to represent the upper bound of achievable performance. This approach ensures that the modeled retrofit captures both the energy savings potential of code-compliant upgrades and the levels typically achieved in field implementations. More details on attic insulation applicability can be found in the attic insulation measure document (Phoung, Lou, &amp; White, 2026).
