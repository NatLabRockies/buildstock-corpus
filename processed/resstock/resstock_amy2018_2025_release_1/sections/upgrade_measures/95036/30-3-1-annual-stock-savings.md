## 3.1  Annual Stock Savings

Figure 25 shows the annual energy consumption of homes with baseline HVAC and three different GHP options (single stage, two stage, and variable speed) for comparison. For the overall housing stock (top) -including homes ineligible for GHP upgrades -variable-speed GHPs could save as much as 35% of the energy consumed by residential buildings, compared to 24% saved by single-stage GHPs. The variable-speed GHP systems represent a substantial efficiency improvement over existing air conditioners and air-source heat pumps used for cooling, such that the energy savings outweigh the addition of cooling in homes that do not have cooling in the baseline, leading to positive cooling energy savings nationwide. When only considering the portion of the housing stock in which GHPs can be installed (see Section 2 for the applicability criteria of this measure), variable-speed GHPs reduce the homes' energy consumption by 46%, compared to 31% for single-stage GHPs.

Figure 26 shows the annual energy consumption by baseline heating fuel for dwellings to which the GHP measures apply. Variable-speed GHPs reduce energy use by 50% for homes currently using natural gas for heating. Substantial savings are also seen in homes using other heating fuels. Variable-speed GHPs are the most efficient GHP option, and moving from two-stage to variable-speed GHPs generally provides lower incremental energy savings for heating but higher savings for cooling than moving from single-stage to two-stage GHPs.

Figure 27 shows the annual energy consumption by Building America climate zone for dwellings to which the GHP measures apply. GHPs can provide substantial savings across all climate zones, with variable-speed GHPs consistently providing the most savings. In cold climates, where most dwelling units are located, variable-speed GHPs reduce energy consumption by 54%.
