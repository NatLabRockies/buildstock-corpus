## 3.2  State Average Energy Savings and GHP Efficiency

Figure 28 shows the annual heating energy savings of variable-speed GHPs compared to the baseline. Heating energy savings are generally slightly larger in heating-dominated climates, but the heating savings overall are generally consistent across climate zones, ranging from 65% to 80% compared to the baseline. Because the Southeast is largely heated by electricity, whereas the rest of the contiguous United States largely uses combustion fuels for heating, heating energy savings are lowest in that region. Some heat losses for combustion heating systems are due to the exhaust of combustion gases, resulting in less efficient heating.

The green scale has five bins from 67.5% to 80.0%. Most states fall between 75% and 77.5%; the Southeast is lightest, with Florida near 70%, and California and Maine are in the darkest bin.

Figure 29 shows the annual cooling energy savings of variable-speed GHPs compared to the baseline. All states experience cooling energy savings, unlike in the single- and two-stage GHP upgrade scenarios, in which some states in colder climate zones experienced negative cooling energy savings due to the higher portion of homes without baseline cooling systems. Vermont has the lowest savings because it has the most homes that lack cooling equipment in the baseline, so although the variable-speed GHP is more efficient than the existing cooling equipment, a large number of homes that previously lacked cooling equipment offset a portion of the overall cooling energy savings. Outside of that outlier, all other states have average cooling energy savings of more than 50%, even when considering that not all homes in the baseline had cooling equipment.

Figure 30 and Figure 31 show the average simulated COP of the variable-speed GHP for heating and cooling, respectively. Heating efficiency is highest in warmer climates, and cooling efficiency is highest in cooler climates, a consistent trend for all GHPs regardless of the compressor type. Even in colder climates, the variable-speed GHP can meet the heating load with an average COP of more than 3. The variable-speed GHP can achieve very high average cooling efficiencies in the northern states; however, because cooling is rarely used in these states, the absolute energy savings of cooling in states like Montana is modest.
