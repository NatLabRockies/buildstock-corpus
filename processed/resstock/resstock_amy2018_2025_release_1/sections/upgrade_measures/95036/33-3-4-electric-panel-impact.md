## 3.4  Electric Panel Impact

This section details the impact the variable-speed GHP upgrade will have on electrical panels. The modeling assumptions and details about electric panels in ResStock can be found in the single-stage GHP documentation (Maguire et al. 2025a).

First, Figure 36 and Figure 37 show information about the ResStock 2025 Release 1 baseline housing stock electric panel distribution. The first column in Figure 36 , 'All,' shows that approximately 35% of all dwelling units have 0 or only 1 breaker space available. The first column in Figure 37 shows that 97.7% of electric panels have no existing capacity constraint for the baseline housing stock, but the other 2.3% of electric panels already have an existing capacity constraint.

Capacity constraints in the existing baseline stock are due in part to the uncertainty associated with the modeling process, where the prediction of the panel size is not based on the presence of all major electric appliances. But it is important to recognize that this level of uncertainty applies to all panel results, including those that look reasonable; hence, the capacity-constrained homes in the baseline can be thought of as over-capacity homes that experience frequent flickering of lights or tripping of breakers if/when large appliances are turned on.

Before this variable-speed GHP retrofit would take place, the panels experiencing a capacity constraint would likely need to address this issue first. Users are encouraged to alternatively analyze the constraint outcomes by excluding homes whose preexisting capacity constraint is not worsened by the upgrade.

Figure 38 shows the distribution of panel constraints overall (first column) and then by heating fuel for dwelling units that receive the variable-speed GHP. It is estimated that approximately 38% of the dwelling units upgrading to a variable-speed GHP will experience either a capacity and/or space constraint, and the other 62% will experience no constraint. Looking more closely at the results, and understanding that natural gas is the predominant heating fuel across the United States, as shown in Figure 26, these results are heavily weighted by dwelling units that have natural gas as their original heating fuel.

The next three figures show percentages of dwelling units for each state that would have some type of electric panel constraint after the variable-speed GHP upgrade. Figure 39 shows a range from 11% to 70% of dwelling units that would have either a capacity and/or a space constraint after the variable-speed GHP. Specifically looking at the space constraints, Figure 40 shows a smaller range, from approximately 4% to 53% of dwelling units that would have a space constraint. Finally, Figure 41 shows a range from roughly 7% to 48% of dwelling units that would have a capacity constraint.
