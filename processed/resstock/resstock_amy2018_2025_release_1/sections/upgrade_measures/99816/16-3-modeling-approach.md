## 3  Modeling Approach

Previous air sealing modeling in ResStock covers how residential air infiltration is modeled (Phoung, Lou and White 2026). ResStock models residential air infiltration values expressed as ACH50. Each dwelling unit in the ResStock dataset is assigned an existing ACH50 value based on housing stock characteristics, informed by the Residential Diagnostics Database (ResDB). ResStock integrates this information using probabilistic sampling logic to represent the diversity of the U.S. housing stock. These infiltration rates are dependent on climate zone, floor area, and vintage. In ResStock, the current infiltration values are based on single-family detached blower door tests in units of ACH50. For single-family detached homes, the ACH50 values reflect total leakage to the outdoors, and buffer zones such as unconditioned attics, crawlspaces, and garages. However, for multifamily and single-family attached units, ResStock models leakage as total unit air leakage, which includes airflow to both the exterior and adjacent units. To estimate only the leakage to the exterior, the model scales the total sampled ACH50 by the ratio of the exterior-facing envelope area to the total envelope area. This adjusted exterior-only infiltration rate is then used in the simulation outputs. In this work, dwelling units with infiltration rates ≥ 10 ACH50 are eligible for a 25% reduction in infiltration rates.

Electrical panel constraint modeling was added to ResStock via OpenStudio-HPXML measures in 2025 to assess whether the existing panel has enough capacity or breaker space to accommodate new loads from an energy retrofit. For details on panel constraint modeling, refer to the ResStock Technical Reference Guide (Reyna, et al. 2025). This measure does not introduce new electrical loads or circuits, so it has no impact on the electrical panel. Any panel constraints appearing in the upgrade results reflect pre-existing conditions, as this measure does not worsen them.
