## 4.2  Example Time-Series Individual Building Results

Three individual cooling and heating load profiles are show in Figure 1 and Figure 2. July 11 was chosen as a representative summer day, and January 3 was chosen as a representative winter day.

![Three line charts of winter heating energy over one day for three example models, baseline against upgrade](99816_images/image_000003_e9acb63d329331a667a46cd2bd07dec7786110ac2015104f632df51af56440af.png)

Figure 2: three stacked line charts titled 'Winter Heating Energy: January 3-4, 2018', one each for the Connecticut, Florida and Texas models. X-axis is time over one day; y-axis is heating in kWh. Series are Baseline and Upgrade. The upgrade line runs below the baseline throughout: about 2.5 kWh against 2.7 overnight in Connecticut, about 3.7 against 4.2 in Florida, and about 0.29 against 0.32 in Texas.

Across all three climates, the upgraded dwelling units show lower cooling and heating load profiles. The cooling energy savings in the Texas model are most prominent in the early afternoon (12:00-15:00) and evening (18:00-21:00). The Florida model shows consistently lower cooling energy use compared to the baseline throughout the whole day. The Connecticut model shows lower savings compared to the baseline with respect to the other dwellings because of its lower initial baseline infiltration level. Thus, the absolute infiltration reduction is less between this dwelling's baseline and its upgrade.

The heading energy impact is reduced for each of the three example dwelling units in the upgraded scenario. All three models show similar load profiles, with consistent heating energy savings throughout the day compared to the baseline.
