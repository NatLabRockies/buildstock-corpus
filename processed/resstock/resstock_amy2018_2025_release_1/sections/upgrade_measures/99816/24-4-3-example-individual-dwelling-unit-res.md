## 4.3  Example Individual Dwelling Unit Results

This section analyzes three different dwelling unit models that received the light air sealing measure. All three individual dwelling unit models show decreases in annual site energy and peak heating and cooling loads as seen in Table 5.

Table 5. Individual Building Model Annual Results

|                                         |                   | Before   |   After | Percent Decrease   |
|-----------------------------------------|-------------------|----------|---------|--------------------|
| Annual Site Energy for All Fuels [kWh]  | Connecticut model | 29,400   |  27,673 | 5.9%               |
|                                         | Florida model     | 31,972   |  29,667 | 7.2%               |
|                                         | Texas model       | 3,060    |   2,834 | 7.4%               |
| Annual Cooling Energy Consumption [kWh] | Connecticut model | 1,411    |   1,188 | 15.8%              |
|                                         | Florida model     | 16,260   |  15,150 | 6.8%               |
|                                         | Texas model       | 1,836    |   1,676 | 8.7%               |
| Annual Heating Energy                   | Connecticut model | 17, 960  |  16,246 | 9.5%               |
| Consumption                             | Florida model     | 7,328    |   6,212 | 15.2%              |
| [kWh]                                   | Texas model       | 174      |     141 | 19.0%              |
| Cooling Hourly Peak Power [kBtu/h]      | Connecticut model | 12.54    |   12.36 | 1.4%               |
|                                         | Florida model     | 43.44    |   41.50 | 4.5%               |
|                                         | Texas model       | 8.78     |    8.70 | 0.9%               |
| Heating Hourly                          | Connecticut model | 52.14    |   51.21 | 1.8%               |
| Peak Power [kBtu/h]                     | Florida model     | 77.98    |   69.93 | 10.3%              |
| Peak Power [kBtu/h]                     | Texas model       | 4.42     |    3.94 | 10.9%              |

The Connecticut model shows an annual site energy reduction of 5.9% from the light air sealing measure. This model shows higher annual cooling savings (15.8%) compared to annual heating savings (9.5%). The cooling and heating hourly peak power shows minimal decrease, at 1.4% and 1.8%, respectively.

The Florida model shows a 7.2% decrease in annual site energy, with annual cooling and heating energy consumption decreasing by 6.8% and 15.2%, respectively. The cooling peak power is reduced by 4.5%, and heating hourly peak power is reduced by 10.3%.

The Texas model shows an annual site energy decrease of 7.4%. Annual cooling and heating consumptions both decrease, with cooling savings of 8.7% and heating savings of 19%. Annual hourly peak cooling and heating power both decrease, by 0.9% and 10.9%, respectively. In both the Texas and Florida models, the annual heating energy shows a higher percent decrease than annual cooling energy. These models have minimal heating requirements, and therefore the percent change in heating energy is more sensitive. By magnitude, the annual heating savings is comparable or smaller than the annual cooling savings.
