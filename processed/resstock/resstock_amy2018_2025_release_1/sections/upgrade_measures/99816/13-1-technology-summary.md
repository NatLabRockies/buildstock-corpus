## 1  Technology Summary

Air sealing involves identifying and closing large and small gaps, cracks, and penetrations in the home's primary or secondary pressure boundaries, such as around windows, doors, attic hatches, and plumbing or wiring holes (U.S. Department of Energy 2025). These leaks, whether large or small, accumulate and can allow a substantial amount of heated or cooled air to escape. By reducing air leaks, air sealing improves indoor comfort, maintains more stable temperatures, and reduces the workload on the heating and cooling system. Air sealing is especially important in older homes, which typically have more leaks (Green Building Alliance 2025). For a comprehensive overview of air sealing and the technology in general, see ResStock Measure Documentation: Advanced Air Sealing (Brossman, White and Phoung 2026).
