## 5.1  Annual Changes

Figure 7 presents the distributions of annual ResStock energy usage for the baseline and the measure at the national level. With broad adoption of EVs and L2 chargers (86.8% of the housing stock), the national site energy usage increases by 7.2%, which is entirely driven by increases in EV charging electricity demand. This increase is a result of EV and EV charger adoption, as the demand flexibility schedules typically have little to no impact on total energy usage.

Figure 8 shows the annual stock energy consumption by Building America climate zone for this measure and the baseline. Stock energy consumption expectedly increases with the 'EV Adoption With L2 Charging' measure package in all climate zones compared to the baseline. This increase is entirely due to additional charging loads, while demand flexibility has little effect on annual energy use since suspended charging generally only delays consumption rather than reduces it. However, in a small number of cases, it is not always possible to fully make up for the peak-period interruption (see Section 4.2); when this occurs, the number of unmet driving hours increases and charging consumption decreases.

Figure 9 displays the average unmet driving hours for each VMT value and percentage charged at home for this measure and a reference case without DF. Unmet driving hours occur when battery charging is not sufficient for the EV's energy demand and are primarily a result of modeling limitations. The most important factors determining unmet driving hours are charger level, VMT, and percent charged at home. Charger level influences the rate of charging possible, while VMT and percent charged at home determine the amount of charging needed. Figure 9 highlights this trend, where only vehicles with high VMT and a high rate of home charging incur unmet driving hours. While both measures shown include L2 charging, which significantly reduces average unmet driving hours compared to the baseline, the measure with demand flexibility shows slightly higher unmet driving hours. This occurs in cases where shifting the charging schedule leaves insufficient time at home to fully meet the vehicle's charging demand. Vehicles with high VMT are relatively uncommon, and most vehicles in the stock experience no unmet driving hours. Across the entire stock, this measure results in an average of 0.41 unmet driving hours, affecting 2.42% of homes. In comparison, the reference measure without DF results in an average of 0.35 unmet driving hours.
