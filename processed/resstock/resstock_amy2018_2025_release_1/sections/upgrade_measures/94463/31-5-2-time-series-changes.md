## 5.2  Time Series Changes

Figure 10 shows the electricity demand for the state of Colorado over 3 days in the winter (left) and 3 days in the summer (right). The dashed line is the demand from the baseline scenario. The full adoption of EVs with L2 chargers increases the demand, however, during the peak hours, the demand is reduced below the baseline value as EV charging is suspended.

Figure 10. Residential Electricity demand in Colorado for the EV Adoption with L2 Charging and DF measure over 3 days in the winter (left) and summer (right)

Figure 10: two stacked area charts of electricity usage in GW in Colorado by end use, January 15 to 17 and July 15 to 17, with the baseline total as a dashed line, EV charging as the top layer and each day's peak period shaded. EV charging is absent during the shaded periods and spikes just after each ends, rebounding to about 2.2 GW on one winter evening, against a baseline peak of about 1.45 GW.

GW = gigawatt
