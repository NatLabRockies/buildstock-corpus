## 1.2  Drill-and-Fill Considerations

The thermal resistance of drill-and-fill insulation is measured by its R-value, which represents the material 's ability to reduce heat flow (U.S. Department of Energy 2010). A higher R-value results in less heat transfer. As a retrofit, drill-and-fill wall insulation can achieve up to R-13 cavity insulation in 2x4 exterior stud cavities (3.5 inches at R-3.7 per inch), which is the maximum insulation level modeled in this measure (Holladay 2013).

Wall cavities that are insulated to levels less than R-13 (e.g., R-11, R-7) did not receive the drill-and-fill upgrade in this ResStock measure. An experienced drill-and-fill practitioner noted that installing additional drill-and-fill insulation into an already insulated wall could cause the insulation batt to bunch, create uneven insulation levels, and lead to other issues. This is supported by literature on retrofit projects that choose not to perform drill-and-fill insulation on walls with existing insulation below R-13 (Frontier Energy, Inc., Misti Bruceri &amp; Associates, LLC 2022) (Antonopoulos, et al. 2022). However, there is some evidence that drill-and-fill can be applied to walls with existing insulation, but this is not included in this measure (Holladay 2013).

Manufactured housing or mobile homes receive drill-and-fill insulation in this measure only if the walls are uninsulated and wood stud. In practice, this can be extremely difficult and requires caution (Carlson and Bemis 1979) (Judkoff, Hancock and Franconi 1990). As with all ResStock results, this upgrade measure represents theoretical technical potential, and dataset users should independently assess whether this measure is appropriate for their building segment of interest.

Studies often pair air sealing and drill-and-fill insulation and report combined energy savings. One study modeled energy savings from drill-and-fill insulation across different climate zones and found whole-home site energy savings ranging from 8.8% to 13.5% (Polly, et al. 2011). Across the literature, drill-and-fill insulation upgrades are frequently paired with air sealing measures (U.S. Department of Energy 2025) (Brozyna and Badger 2013) (Roberts and Stephenson 2012). More information on air sealing modeling can be found in the advanced air sealing documentation (Phoung, Lou and White 2025).

For modeling purposes, drill-and-fill insulation is assumed to be installed properly to a Grade 1 insulation level. According to the RESNET Grading system, Grade 1 assumes installation follows the manufacturer's instructions and fills the entire wall cavity ( Construction Instruction Experience Center n.d.). However, a field study found that only half of all homes had Grade 1 insulation (North American Insulation Manufacturers Association 2017). In practice, a Grade 1 assumption may be optimistic, and insulation inspection should be conducted carefully (North American Insulation Manufacturers Association 2017). This optimistic assumption about proper insulation installation is a limitation of the work, and may cause modeled results to be different than actual building retrofit results.

In summary, this upgrade measure assumes:

- Advanced air sealing is applied to dwelling units with ACH50 infiltration rates of 6 or greater. This applies to 92% of all dwelling units and heavily influences the results.
- Drill-and-fill insulation is applied to about 30% of dwelling units only
- Drill-and-fill insulation uses cellulose up to R-13 on exterior above-grade walls that separate conditioned from unconditioned spaces only
- Existing wall cavities with no insulation will receive the insulation, and walls with insulation less than R-13 will not
- Proper installation is assumed, similar to Grade 1 RESNET installation.
