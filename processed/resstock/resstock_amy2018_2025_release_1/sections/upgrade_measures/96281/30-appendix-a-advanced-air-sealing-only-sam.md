## Appendix A. Advanced Air Sealing Only Sample Results

This section includes sample results comparing advanced air sealing only to advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill insulation.

Figure A-1: horizontal grouped bar chart. Rows are end uses by fuel plus total site energy; x-axis is all-fuels end uses in kWh, 0 to 20k. Series are Air Sealing and 'Air, Wall Sealing/Insulation', both for applicable dwelling units only. Total site energy is about 20.4k kWh with air sealing alone and about 19.2k with wall insulation added; natural gas heating is about 5.8k against about 5.0k.

![Two bar charts: percentage savings by wall type, and site energy by vintage, for two upgrade scenarios](96281_images/image_000016_679b6e951f47c24787642cc44e5f11bac3cab627311b5a11e4f6a16d05a68e86.png)

Figures A-2 and A-3, extracted as one bitmap that also holds Figure A-2's caption: 'Average annual dwelling unit percentage site energy savings per applicable dwelling unit for advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation (air, wall sealing/insulation) and advanced air sealing only'. In the upper chart (A-2) the two scenarios save the same for every wall type except uninsulated wood stud: about 18% with air sealing alone and about 32% with wall insulation. The lower chart (A-3) shows site energy by vintage; homes built before 1940 use about 26k kWh against about 22.8k.

Figure A-4: horizontal grouped bar chart. Rows are nineteen climate zone groups from 1A - FL to 8AK; x-axis is site energy total consumption in kWh, 0 to above 30k. Series are Air Sealing and 'Air, Wall Sealing/Insulation'. Adding wall insulation lowers consumption a little further in most zones: about 30.5k against 29.7k in 7A and about 26.8k against 24.7k in 5A; the hottest zones barely differ.
