## Executive Summary

The goal of this work is to develop energy efficiency, demand flexibility, and other retrofit end-use load shapes (electricity, gas, propane, or fuel oil).

An ' end-use savings shape ' is the difference in energy consumption between a baseline building and a building with an energy efficiency, demand flexibility, or other retrofit measure applied. This results in a time-series profile broken down by end use and fuel (electricity or on-site gas, propane, or fuel oil use) at each time step.

ResStock ™ is a highly granular, physics-based, bottom-up model that uses multiple data sources, statistical sampling methods, and advanced building energy simulations to estimate the annual subhourly energy consumption of the residential building stock across the United States. The baseline model is intended to represent the U.S. residential building stock as it existed in 2018. The methodology and results of the baseline model are discussed in the final technical report of the End-Use Load Profiles project 1  (Wilson, et al. 2022).

This documentation focuses on a single end-use savings shape measure: advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation.

The advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation upgrade demonstrated 22% total site energy savings for the U.S. residential building stock (see Figure 3 for more details). These savings are primarily attributed to:

- 32% stock electricity heating savings
- 37% stock natural gas heating savings
- 39% stock fuel oil heating savings
- 32% stock propane heating savings
- 11% stock electric cooling savings.
