## 4.1.2 Example Annual Individual Building Results

In general, the three individual buildings each show savings in total annual site energy. Although the set of measures modeled here differ from those in other studies, the amounts are generally consistent with the magnitude of savings from typical envelope upgrades (Baechler, et al. 2021) (Polly, et al. 2011) (ENERGY STAR n.d.). In all three building models, both cooling and heating energy consumption show annual savings. Cooling and heating peak loads also show reductions ranging from slight to considerable. Individual model results are presented in Table 7.

The Colorado model shows a total site energy reduction of 26.5% from the advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation package. There is a moderate reduction in cooling energy consumption and a greater reduction in heating energy consumption. In this building model, both unmet heating and cooling hours decrease by over 80%. The heating and cooling hourly power peaks show minimal decreases, at 4.8% and 2.3%, respectively.

The North Carolina model has a 23.5% decrease in annual site energy, with annual cooling and heating energy consumption decreasing by 23.7% and 29.5%, respectively. The annual unmet cooling hours drop from 1.5 to 0, while annual unmet heating hours remain at 0. The cooling and heating hourly peak power are both reduced by about 25%.

The Arizona model shows an annual site energy decrease of 18.5%. Annual cooling and heating consumption both decrease, with heating energy consumption showing annual savings of over 50%. This is likely due to the dwelling unit's location in Maricopa County, Arizona. The heating load was already minimal, and the advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation further decreased it significantly. Unmet cooling hours were originally 0 and remained at 0. Annual hourly peak cooling and heating power both decrease, by 24.4% and 1.4%, respectively.
