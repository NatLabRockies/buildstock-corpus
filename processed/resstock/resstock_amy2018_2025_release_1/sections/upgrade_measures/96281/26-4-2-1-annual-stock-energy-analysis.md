## 4.2.1  Annual Stock Energy Analysis

The average baseline applicable dwelling unit consumed 24.51 MWh of site energy per year. After air sealing and drill-and-fill wall insulation were applied to applicable units, the average dwelling unit consumed 19.20 MWh of site energy per year. This represents an average 21.7% decrease.

Figure 3 shows the average energy consumption by end use per dwelling unit before and after the upgrade.

Figure 3. Average energy consumption by end use group per applicable dwelling unit

![Image](96281_images/image_000004_34347d7836123add5a60a69e20342925aab67b0d013ea3a83a25053f2b797833.png)

Before the upgrade, the largest two end uses in the average dwelling unit were natural gas heating and electric cooling. Other notable end uses included miscellaneous electric loads, electric appliances, and natural gas hot water heating. Although these patterns remain in the upgrade scenario, total site energy use is 22% lower. Significant savings are seen in heating and cooling end uses, particularly electric heating (35%), natural gas heating (40%), fuel oil heating (41%), propane heating (36%), electric cooling (11%), electric heating fan pumps (37%), and electric heat pump backup (54%). Slight savings are observed in the electric cooling fans and pumps (8%). End uses like water heating, lighting consumption, and miscellaneous electricity remain unchanged.

Figure 4 shows the annual average percent savings per upgraded dwelling unit broken down by original wall type and insulation level.

Figure 4. Average annual dwelling unit percentage site energy savings per applicable dwelling unit wall type and insulation level

![Image](96281_images/image_000005_a9975d58cdf6a4834ab1e7c26924121d3ed2187873caca333f68df3a4b6f0bd6.png)

The applicability logic states that only uninsulated wood stud walls are eligible for drill-and-fill insulation, whereas all dwelling units with an infiltration rate of 6 ACH50 or higher are eligible for advanced air sealing. This logic is clearly reflected in the results: uninsulated wood stud walls show an average site energy savings of 32%. All other wall type and insulation combinations show site energy savings ranging from 8% to 20%. Note that these results are more heavily influenced by advanced air sealing, as its applicability exceeds 90%, whereas drill-and-fill insulation applies to only about 30% of dwelling units.

Figure 5. Stock-level applicable buildings only total annual site energy consumption by infiltration level

![Image](96281_images/image_000006_9483f9b84ddc19c186d1247f9db9e2679b265ff1d48b2ac14a0ff3b7f6852aa8.png)

Figure 5 shows total annual site energy consumption for the stock level before and after the upgrade. In general, dwellings with higher baseline infiltration rates achieve greater energy savings than those with lower infiltration rates. Figure 5 also shows that dwellings with low infiltration levels have relatively low stock level energy consumption. Figure 6 shows the average percentage of site energy savings per dwelling unit at different infiltration rates.

Figure 6. Average applicable only annual dwelling unit percentage site energy savings by baseline infiltration rate

![Image](96281_images/image_000007_6cee5967ff3b230472cbaaf860ca80611294f363be3fe84052f02cdba2408c4e.png)

Again, Figure 6 shows that dwellings with higher baseline infiltration rates achieve greater energy savings, exceeding 40% when the baseline infiltration is 50 ACH50. This trend continues down to 10 ACH50 but changes when infiltration rates fall below 10 ACH50. Infiltration rates of 2 -5 ACH50 have a higher percentage of savings than rates of 6 -8 ACH50. This is likely due to 2-5 ACH50 dwelling units having a lower annual site energy than other buildings, as seen in Figure 5, so any change to the site energy consumption results in a higher percentage change even if the absolute change is small.

Figure 7 shows the average annual site energy consumption per dwelling unit by vintage. Infiltration rates depend on vintage, so there is some correlation in the ResStock 2025 Release 1 baseline samples: older homes generally have higher infiltration rates and are more likely to have uninsulated walls.

Figure 7. Average annual site energy consumption per dwelling unit by vintage for applicable dwelling units only

![Image](96281_images/image_000008_9c74e86c9c1c756c55e84b8c64382b43549ca9e74bc704a60cf1b588e30ac007.png)

In general, older homes use more energy and achieve greater energy savings. This trend appears in the following figures as well. The oldest homes, built before 1940, have an average energy savings of 32%, while the newest homes have an average energy savings of only 9%.

Figure 8. Average annual percent site energy savings by vintage for applicable units only

![Image](96281_images/image_000009_c5718aeedc65f82ad45d27e0d45fded267034d0345cefa8e1dbce545f5f03706.png)

Figure 9 shows energy savings by climate zone.

Figure 9. Average annual per dwelling unit percentage site energy savings by climate ASHRAE 2004 climate zone for applicable dwelling units only

![Image](96281_images/image_000010_3327c93c4b8815e775d0a7aaec0807570894f9eaa068811cce34d2b3ea56c911.png)

In general, climate zones 4 -7 experience a higher percentage of energy savings than other climate zones. This is expected given the significant reduction in heating energy shown in the end use breakdown. Climate zones 4A, 5A, 6B, and 7A show greater percentage energy savings than the dwelling units in their respective climate zones. For example, climate zone 4A shows greater savings than 4B and 4C because the average baseline dwelling unit energy consumption in 4A is higher than in the other zones, as shown in Figure 10. This is because climate zone 4A is a more extreme climate than climate zones 4B and 4C.

Climate zone 7B shows a larger site energy percentage savings than climate zones 7AK and 8AK because climate zone 7B 's results are driven by natural gas heating savings, which are on average more than three times as large as climate zones 7AK and 8AK savings. In addition, climate zone 7AK shows heat pump fan and cooling electricity increasing after the upgrade. These end use increases further add to the difference in total site energy savings experienced between climate zones 7AK and 7B. The difference among the climate zones savings are also influenced by the electricity consumption from the heat pump heating backup. Climate zone 7B shows a 38% savings on average for this end use, while climate zone 7AK only shows a 23% site energy savings, and this end use is not applicable for climate zone 8AK. This is another reason for the large difference in site energy savings between the three climate zones.

Figure 10. Average site energy consumption for applicable dwelling units only by ASHRAE climate zone

![Image](96281_images/image_000011_54f7e8de384993f58882e7ad1a8225311a689c2ef180b731b86d34151e881bd1.png)

Energy consumption is only one way to display impact. On average, unmet heating and cooling hours also decrease. In the baseline, the average applicable dwelling unit had 130 unmet cooling hours and 22 unmet heating hours. After applying advanced air sealing with drill-and-fill wall insulation, these values decrease to 81 and 17, representing reductions of 46% and 27%, respectively.
