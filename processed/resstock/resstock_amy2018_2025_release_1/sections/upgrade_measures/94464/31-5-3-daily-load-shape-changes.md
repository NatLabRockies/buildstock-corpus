## 5.3  Daily Load Shape Changes

Figure 11 shows the daily average electricity demand in Colorado during summer and winter seasons under three scenarios: the baseline, EV adoption with L2 charging, and efficient EV adoption with L2 charging and DF (this measure). The shaded red regions denote the peak demand hours.

The DF measure successfully reduces demand during the peak period in both seasons compared to the L2-only scenario. However, this reduction leads to a rebound in demand immediately following the peak window, and a change in peak timing. The new peak timing in the DF scenario is closely tied to the peak window, which reflects both residential and commercial sector peaks and may not align with the baseline peak for residential loads. As a result, the DF scenario does not always shift the modeled peak later in the day. In the winter, the peak window aligns with the baseline residential peak, so the shifted load creates a new, later peak time due to the rebound. In summer, the baseline peak occurs before the peak window begins, so the demand shift does not change the peak timing. However, demand shifting still reduces peak magnitude compared to the L2-only scenario.

While the total daily electricity consumption with and without DF will remain the same, the DF measure shifts load away from high-demand hours in both seasons. Further, the introduction of more efficient EVs reduces the overall charging energy demand, but does not influence the peak, as the charging power remains constant in each scenario. This strategy helps mitigate peak demand but also illustrates the potential for rebound effects and new peaks, which can inform design and evaluation of EV demand flexibility strategies.

Figure 11. Daily residential electricity load shape for Colorado in winter (left) and summer (right). This measure ('Eff. EV + L2 Charger + DF') and two reference cases ('Baseline' and 'EV + L2 Charger') are represented.
