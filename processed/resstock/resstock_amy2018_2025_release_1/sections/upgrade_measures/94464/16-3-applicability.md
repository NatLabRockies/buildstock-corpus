## 3  Applicability

The 'Efficient EV Adoption With Level 2 Charging and DF' measure assigns an efficient EV, an L2 charger, and a flexible charging schedule to all occupied dwelling units. This logic corresponds to 87.9% of the existing housing stock. Dwelling units with an EV in the baseline are upgraded to a more efficient EV, and units with an L1 charger are upgraded to an L2 charger. Other inputs that influence charging demand, such as annual miles driven or fraction charged at home, remain unchanged from the baseline. Only a single EV is adopted per dwelling unit, as modeling multiple vehicles is not currently supported. An EV demand flexibility measure is applied to all units so that EV charging energy is avoided during peak periods. This measure design allows flexibility when analyzing scenarios of adoption. For example, a user can select a percentage of a specific segment to adopt this measure while using the results from the baseline simulation for the remaining models. Units in multi-family buildings are included in the measure. Although multi-family households may not own the charging infrastructure or see its impact on their utility bills in practice, we model them consistently with other housing types, treating EV energy as a direct household energy cost. We also assume that multi-family chargers are always available, which may not be true in practice, and therefore the charging event durations may not reflect actual events.
