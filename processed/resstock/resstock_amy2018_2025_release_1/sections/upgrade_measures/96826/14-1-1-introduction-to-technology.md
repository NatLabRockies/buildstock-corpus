## 1.1  Introduction to Technology

A HPWH is a high-efficiency electric storage water heater that uses a refrigerant cycle to extract heat from the surrounding air to produce hot water. A 240V HPWH, also known as a hybrid water heater, has backup electric resistance elements inside the tank to allow several operation modes: heat pump only, electric resistance only, or hybrid (i.e., the high-power electric resistance only turns on to supplement the heat pump in high demand situations). The refrigerant cycle leverages phase-changing fluid to transfer more heat than the electricity it consumes, resulting in an efficiency level several times greater than that of electric resistance heating, which has nearly 100% efficiency (IEA, 2022).

For electric water heaters, HPWHs are the only technology type that can meet ENERGY STAR ® Version 7.0 efficiency requirement, which is set to an FHR of 45 gal/h and a UEF of 3.3 for 240V models (ENERGY STAR, 2025c). Note that 120V HPWHs, characterized by no or low-power electric backup elements, are also ENERGY STAR qualified, but they are not considered in this measure.

The FHR indicates how much hot water a water heater can deliver in an hour of operation and is often used when sizing for an application. UEF is a metric used to quantify the energy efficiency of water heaters under standard test conditions (Jutras, 2024). The UEF test procedure includes a draw profile that is intended to reflect realistic hot water usage for that water heater and tank size. The FHR for a water heater determines which of four usage levels are used in the UEF test procedure. Thus, a UEF has a companion usage bin label and should only be compared with other UEF of the same bin. A higher UEF indicates that the water heater is more efficient in converting energy into hot water within its specific usage bin. HPWHs generally have an FHR between 45 and 87 gal/h and a UEF rating between 3.34 and 4.14. In comparison, an electric resistance water heater has a UEF of 0.9 to 0.95. HPWHs account for only 2% of all water heaters sold in 2024, but are gaining popularity due to a growing awareness of their efficiency and cost savings potential. HPWH sales grew by 35% from 2022 to 2023 (Wachunas, 2024).
