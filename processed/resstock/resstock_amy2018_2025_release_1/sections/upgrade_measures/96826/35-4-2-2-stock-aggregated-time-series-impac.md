## 4.2.2  Stock Aggregated Time-Series Impacts

To illustrate the impact of the HPWH measure on the aggregated load profile, Figure 12 presents the modeled total residential stock electricity profile before and after the measure for HI, CA, and AK during their respective peak day in 2018. The impact varies significantly in each of the three states, which are in very different climates. The upgrade reduced HI's summer electric peak on 2018-09-30 16:30:00 local time from 0.78 GW by 8.5%, mostly because the bulk of the water heating load is already electric and benefits from the efficiency gain. For CA, which is also summer peaking, the upgrade reduced its electric peak on 2018-07-07 17:00:00 local time from 32.17 GW by only 0.5%. This is due to a higher share of fuel water heaters in CA, which adds new electric demand when replaced. This added demand offsets the efficiency gains from replacing electric water heaters with HPWH, resulting in a lower overall peak demand reduction. On the other hand, AK is winter-peaking, and instead of a peak load reduction like CA and HI, its electric peak on 2018-02-03 19:45:00 local time is increased by the upgrade from 0.43 GW by 9.2%, a relatively large increase. This is because in addition to new electric loads from replacing many fuel water heaters, similarly to CA, the measure increases AK's electric space heating by making the electric heaters, generally lower efficiency than the HPWH, run longer to make up the heat lost to the HPWH, thereby increasing the electric system peak. As illustrated by the examples, HPWH can significantly impact the electric grid load shape depending on the saturation of fuel water heaters and the interaction between HPWH and HVAC.
