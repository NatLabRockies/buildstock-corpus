## 4.3  Stock Energy Utility Bill Impacts

The modeled overnight energy utility bill savings potential is $19 billion from the high-efficiency HPWH upgrade, with 53% of the savings coming from natural gas and 36% from electricity, as shown in Figure 13. This is calculated using 2024 state average utility rates and by applying the HPWH to all homes eligible for such an upgrade, which is estimated to be 99.4% of the housing stock. When down-selected to occupied and upgraded dwellings (90% of the stock), the average utility cost savings from the HPWH upgrade is $151 per year or 5.3% of the total annual average energy bills.

On average, 52% of the energy bill savings comes from natural gas, which is attributed to 73% of the total energy savings. In comparison, electricity represents 37% of the bill savings but just

20% of the energy reduction. This indicates that natural gas on average is cheaper than electricity on a site energy basis, reducing the relative savings in bills compared to energy. As a result, the range of modeled energy bill savings is lower from the HPWH measure, with more dwellings expected to see a bill increase, when switching from natural gas water heating than any other water heating fuel types, as shown in Figure 14. Across U.S. states, the utility bill impact from the HPWH upgrade will depend on how the cost electricity compares to the cost of other fuels. Overall, 5.6% of applicable dwelling units are estimated to see a net bill increase from the HPWH upgrade, while 1.5% are expected to see a net increase in site energy consumption.
