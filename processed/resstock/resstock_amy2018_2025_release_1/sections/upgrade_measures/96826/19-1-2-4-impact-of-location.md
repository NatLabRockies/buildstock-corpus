## 1.2.4 Impact of Location

The location of a HPWH can be a major consideration when it comes to its performance and impact on occupant comfort, and manufacturer guidance varies by climate region (ENERGY STAR, 2025b). The HPWH draws heat from the ambient air and exhausts air that is cooler and drier to the same space unless ducted. HPWH performance can be boosted in a warm environment (e.g., a utility room that offers waste heat from other appliances). In colder climates, installation in cold, unconditioned spaces, such as garages and outside, should be limited. Unvented small spaces, such as a closet, can also present a challenge, but can be mitigated with passive venting or short duct runs to another conditioned location (Larson &amp; Larson, 2022). While ResStock models water heater location diversity, it does not model the impact of tight closet spaces or ducting options for HPWHs. HPWHs can be slightly larger and taller than conventional storage water heaters due to the heat pump and larger tank to account for slower recovery, which can limit where they can be installed. They also require a drain for condensate or the addition of a condensate pump if a drain is not available. ResStock does not account for these limitations and assumes the existing water heaters can be swapped out for the HPWH without changing the installation location within the home.

When located within the conditioned space, the cooling from HPWH can lead to an energy increase for space heating and decrease for space cooling. This interactive effect is captured in ResStock and its net impact on space heating and cooling can vary by climate region. The cooling and dehumidification can also impact occupant comfort. Lower humidity makes it feel cooler, potentially allowing for a higher space cooling setpoint to achieve the same comfort. ResStock does not currently model indirect humidity impacts, such as that on setpoint, or the use of humidifiers or dehumidifiers in the housing stock. ResStock also assumes an 'interaction factor' of 1, where the HVAC responds to every unit of heat taken from the space and adjusts its operation accordingly to maintain setpoint. Actual data from homes shows this interaction can vary widely and is often less than 1 depending on where in the home the water heater is installed.
