## 1.2.1 Efficiency

In an electric HPWH, cold water comes in from the bottom of the tank and is heated by the heat pump coil or the backup elements. As hot water rises and goes out from the top, it creates a temperature gradient or a stratified tank. A tempering valve can be added to the outlet to mix the hot water with cold water to prevent scalding at the point of use, typically by keeping the temperature of water drawn after the tempering valve below 125°F. This also lets the tank temperature to be set higher, allowing for more stored energy and reducing the risk of legionella growth in the tank. Table 1 summarizes the range of performance of ENERGY STAR rated HPWHs available in the U.S. market according to EPA data retrieved in May 2025 (ENERGY STAR, 2025a).

Table 1. ENERGY STAR 240V HPWH Performance Ratings

| Volume (gal)   | FHR (gal/h)   | UEF       |
|----------------|---------------|-----------|
| <45            | 46-66         | 3.4-3.88  |
| 45-55          | 65-76         | 3.35-3.93 |
| 56+            | 70-87         | 3.34-4.14 |
