## 1.2.2 Sizing

Storage water heater sizing depends on the first hour rating (FHR) per Uniform Plumbing Code. Right-sizing ensures the water heater is delivering the required hot water demand without increasing the equipment upfront cost or operational inefficiencies. Hot water demand is primarily driven by the number of occupants but can be influenced by many other factors, including the type and behavior of occupants, number and type of hot water appliances and fixtures, and length of distribution. Because number of occupants can change over time while physical characteristics like number of bedrooms and bathrooms are typically fixed, sizing is sometimes done in terms of these characteristics instead. Generally, the larger the household, the larger the house, and the larger the water heater. Additionally, because HPWHs recover slower than other storage water heaters when operating in heat pump mode, EnergyStar recommends to upsize for more storage capacity (ENERGY STAR, 2025b). Table 2 shows the ENERGY STAR

NextGen program's minimum sizing requirement, which depends on the number of bedrooms and bathrooms (ENERGY STAR, 2025b).

For modeling purposes, we simplify the efficiency and size of ENERGY STAR HPWH data down to the following in Table 3. Note the sizing is slightly larger than the minimum sizing requirement in Table 2 and consistent with the recommendation to upsize HPWH to reduce the chance of unmet hot water. The efficiency values were averages of models from two major U.S. HPWH manufacturers. They were higher than those shown in Table 1 because the ENERGY STAR database typically includes older generations of HPWH.
