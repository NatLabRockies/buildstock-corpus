## 4.1.1 Single Dwelling Annual

Based on the applicability logic, the windows in all four individual dwellings were upgraded. In addition to the updated window performance characteristics, all four dwellings received a 30% infiltration reduction because they all started with single pane windows.

Looking at the results on an annual basis, the end uses and fuel types that changed significantly because of the upgrade were HVAC-related heating and cooling energy for dwellings with HVAC systems. This is expected, because upgrading the windows changes the overall heat transfer through the building envelope. Dwelling 1, located in Hawai'i, had no HVAC, which is common in Hawai'i. In this dwelling, the only impact of upgrading the windows was a tiny (1%) reduction in electricity consumption by the refrigerator, presumably because of slightly decreased indoor temperatures during parts of the year. The water heater was not impacted because it is outside of the living space in a separately conditioned mechanical space.

The annual savings for the affected end uses and fuels for each of the individual dwellings are shown in Table 12. As expected, there are no savings in HVAC energy for Dwelling 1 because it has no heating or cooling. Also as expected, Dwelling 2 shows heating electricity savings, and Dwellings 3 and 4 show heating natural gas savings based on their heating system fuels. These buildings show savings that are on the higher end of the expected range given their single pane baseline windows.
