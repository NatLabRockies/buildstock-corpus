## 1.1  Introduction to Technology

ASHPs provide space heating and cooling in buildings. According to the 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey from the U.S. Energy Information Administration, only ~16.5% of dwellings in the contiguous United States have ASHP systems, with most ASHPs residing in warmer climate regions like the southern United States (U.S. Energy Information Administration 2022).

ASHPs transfer heat from outdoor air to indoor air during heating operation and from indoor to outdoor during cooling operation. During heating, an ASHP's compressor powers a vapor compression cycle that sends heat to the conditioned space of the dwelling. Unlike fossil fuel combustion or electric resistance heating, ASHPs can heat dwellings at an efficiency level over 100%. For example, average heating coefficients of performance (COPs)-the ratio of heating delivered divided by energy consumed-vary from ~2.0 in colder climates to ~3.0 in hotter climates for the ducted ENERGY STAR ®  air-to-air heat pump upgrade (Praprost, et al. 2026). For reference, an electric resistance heater heats at a COP of 1.0.

When an ASHP's compressor cannot meet the heating load of a dwelling, its backup heating system will turn on, if one exists, and aid in meeting the load. Backup heating systems can be either integrated into the ASHP or separate. Integrated backup heating systems use electric resistance heating or ducted fossil fuel furnaces. Separate backup heating systems can use either fossil fuel or electricity to help meet the load and can include a wide range of heating technologies.

The intent of this ASHP measure upgrade is to reflect the specifications of a typical cold climate air-source heat pump (termed 'ccASHP' by the Northeast Energy Efficiency Partnerships [NEEP]) based on the median metrics for ccASHPs in the NEEP cold climate ASHP database (Northeast Energy Efficiency Partnerships, Inc. 2023). NEEP requires heat pumps to have a variable-speed compressor to be noted as a ccASHP. More information on the cold climate designation can be found in NEEP's product list documentation. Section 1.2. describes this selection process in more detail (Northeast Energy Efficiency Partnerships, Inc. 2023).
