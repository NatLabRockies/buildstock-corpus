## Executive Summary

The goal of this work is to develop energy efficiency, demand flexibility, and other retrofit end-use load shapes (electricity, gas, propane, or fuel oil) that cover a majority of the high-impact, market-ready (or nearly market-ready) measures. 'Measures' refers to retrofits that can be applied to buildings during modeling.

An 'end -use savings shape' is the difference in energy consumption between a baseline building and a building with an energy efficiency, demand flexibility, or other retrofit measure applied. It results in a time-series profile that is broken down by end use and fuel (electricity or on-site gas, propane, or fuel oil use) at each time step.

ResStock ™ is a highly granular, physics-based, bottom-up model that uses multiple data sources, statistical sampling methods, and advanced building energy simulations to estimate the annual subhourly energy consumption of the residential building stock across the United States. The baseline model intends to represent the U.S. residential building stock as it existed in 2018. Technical documentation for the inputs and assumptions in the baseline building stock model is available in Reyna et al. (2025). Calibration and validation of the baseline model results are available in the final technical report of the End-Use Load Profiles project (Wilson et al. 2022).

This documentation focuses on a single end-use savings shape measure: Heating, Ventilation, and Air-Conditioning (HVAC) Load Flexibility.

The HVAC load flexibility measure modifies the heating and cooling thermostat set points before peak periods to preheat or precool the building or during peak periods to shift load away from peak periods. In real deployments of demand flexibility via smart thermostats, the dispatch is typically limited to a handful of days per year to avoid customer fatigue. But the goal of this modeling is to create a dataset that can be analyzed to determine the maximum technical potential load profile impact at any given point in the year, so the demand flexibility is dispatched nearly every day in the modeled dataset . Although the profile on any given day is realistic, the full annual load profiles do not reflect the realistic dispatch of HVAC load flexibility . Details on the appropriate use of the load profiles can be found in this document.

To keep the logic simple and to generate the most comprehensive result that can support different future analysis, this measure applies to all dwelling units with either a heating or a cooling system (approximately 99.7% of the dwelling units modeled in ResStock); however, the heating load analysis in this document is done on only buildings with electric heating. With a 4°F offset during the 4-hour peak periods across all 51 states, this resulted in an average reduction of 57% in peak period heating electricity consumption during the winter months and a 54% reduction in peak period cooling electricity during the summer months.
