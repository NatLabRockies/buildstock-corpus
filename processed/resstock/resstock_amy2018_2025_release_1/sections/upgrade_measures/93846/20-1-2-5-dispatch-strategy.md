## 1.2.5 Dispatch Strategy

In historic deployments, thermostat-based demand response is typically only dispatched 15 -25 days per year to avoid customer fatigue (Xcel, 2021) (Austin Energy, 2024). But the goal of this modeling is to create a dataset that can be analyzed to determine the maximum technical potential load profile impact at any given point in the year, so the demand flexibility is dispatched every day in the modeled dataset . The dispatch strategy is modified for shoulderseason days where heating or cooling load is assumed to be minimal, defined by an average daily temperature greater than 50°F and less than 68°F. On those days, load shifting (done via precooling or preheating) is not dispatched, but load-shedding events are dispatched. The method for choosing this temperature range is described in section 4.2 Initial Sensitivity Analysis. Because of the unrealistically high number of days that demand flexibility is dispatched in this upgrade, the results must be used thoughtfully. One approach is to use daily profiles from the baseline for most days of the year and to splice in daily profiles from the upgrade for days when the intent of the analysis is to dispatch demand flexibility. Similarly, annualized energy consumption or energy savings metrics should not be used because of the overrepresentation of dispatch .
