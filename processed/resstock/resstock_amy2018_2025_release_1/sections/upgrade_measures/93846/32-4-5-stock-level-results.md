## 4.5  Stock-Level Results

A test run with 30,000 samples was conducted. The state-level D3 metrics are calculated in the following five steps:

1. Filter out samples that have electric heating and samples that have cooling separately.

2. Aggregate state-level heating (or cooling) time series for both the baseline and savings from upgrades.
3. Calculate the percentage reduction in heating or cooling during the peak periods for each day.
4. Remove days where heating (or cooling) savings during peak periods are zero, indicating that the thermostat demand flexibility measure is not applicable on those days.
5. Compute the mean, along with the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles for the remaining days.

The results for the single-family detached across the five upgrades are shown in Figure 15 -Figure 19. States not shown in the figures either lack relevant data due to an insufficient sample size or do not have the specific end use (heating or cooling) savings.

Figure 15 shows an approximate 40% winter and 30% summer reduction in peak load from load shedding for 4 hours with a 2°F offset. As expected, Figure 16 shows an approximate 15% winter and 10% summer reduction from load shifting (preconditioning) for 4 hours with a 2°F offset. This lower reduction relative to Figure 15 is expected because the thermal inertia does not carry through the entire 4-hour peak window. Figure 17 shows an approximate 60% winter and 50% summer reduction in peak load from load shedding for 4 hours with a 4°F offset. This deep reduction is expected because of the significant depth of the set point change. Figure 18 shows an approximate 30% winter and 20% summer reduction from load shifting (preconditioning) for 4 hours with a 4°F offset. As expected, this reduction is larger than the reduction shown from load shifting with a 2°F offset because the deeper set point change for 4 hours allows more charging of the building thermal mass, allowing reduced space conditioning during more of the 4-hour peak window. Last, Figure 19 shows an approximate 10% winter and 5% summer reduction from load shifting (preconditioning) for 1 hour with a 2°F offset. This minimal impact is expected because the short duration of the preconditioning coupled with the small set point offset does not allow much charging of the building thermal mass, leading to a minimal impact during the peak window.
