## Executive Summary

The goal of this work is to develop energy efficiency, demand flexibility, and other retrofit end-use load shapes (electricity, gas, propane, or fuel oil) that cover most high-impact, market-ready (or nearly market-ready) measures. 'Measures' refers to retrofits that can be applied to buildings during modeling.

An 'end-use savings shape' is the difference in energy consumption between a baseline building and a building with an energy efficiency, demand flexibility, or other retrofit measure applied. This results in a time-series profile broken down by end use and fuel (electricity or on-site gas, propane, or fuel oil) at each time step.

ResStock™ is a highly granular, physics-based, bottom-up model that uses multiple data sources, statistical sampling methods, and advanced building energy simulations to estimate the annual subhourly energy consumption of the residential building stock across the United States. The baseline model represents the U.S. residential building stock as it existed in 2018. Technical documentation for the inputs and assumptions in the baseline building stock model is available in (Reyna, et al., 2025). Calibration and validation of the baseline model results are provided in the final technical report of the End-Use Load Profiles project (NREL, 2025).

This documentation focuses on a single end-use savings shape package: advanced air sealing, attic floor insulation, and duct sealing upgrade.

The advanced air sealing, attic floor insulation, and duct sealing upgrade achieved 22.6% total site energy savings (780 TWh) for the U.S. residential building stock (Figure 5). These savings are primarily attributed to:

- 40% stock natural gas heating savings (458 TWh)
- 36% stock electricity heating savings (92 TWh)
- 21% stock electricity cooling savings (80 TWh).
