## 4.2.1  Annual Stock Energy Analysis

Figure 5 shows the annual site energy consumption by end use before and after the envelope package upgrades for all homes. The annual stock energy savings from the envelope package upgrade is 780 TWh, compared to a pre-upgrade average consumption of 3,454 TWhrepresenting a 22.6% reduction. Most savings occurred in heating and cooling end uses. These include 36% (92 TWh) electricity heating savings, 40% (458 TWh) natural gas heating savings, 37% (62 TWh) fuel oil heating savings, 36% (47 TWh) propane heating savings, 68% (15 TWh) electricity heating heat pump backup savings, 21% (80 TWh) electricity cooling savings, 39% (14 TWh) electricity heating fans and pumps savings, and 20% (13 TWh) electricity cooling fans and pumps savings.

Table 5 shows the annual energy consumption savings percentage per upgraded dwelling unit. As discussed earlier, most savings were observed in heating and cooling end uses. However, there is only a small change in hot water-related end uses. When both the water heater and ducts are in the same place (e.g., unheated basement or attic), hot water energy consumption can be affected slightly due to reduced heat transfer from the ducts. If the water heater is in the attic, it can also be affected by improved attic floor insulation. Additional details are available in the duct sealing and attic floor insulation documentation (Lou &amp; Liu, 2025) (Phoung, Lou, &amp; White, 2025b). Furthermore, indoor temperature changes also influence hot water energy use. Overall, the impact on hot water energy consumption is less than 0.2%.

There is also a very small change in appliance electricity use, primarily for refrigerators. Refrigerators work by transferring heat from the interior to the surrounding air. When the surrounding air is cooler, a refrigerator operates more efficiently and uses less energy. The average indoor temperature over the year increased slightly from 71.28°F to 71.46°F. This slight increase in temperature results in a minor increase in refrigerator energy use. However, the impact is negligible-the change in appliance electricity consumption is 0.21%.

Figure 9 shows the annual energy consumption savings from individual and combined envelope measures, based on 27.8% of dwelling units where all three individual measures apply. When all measures are implemented together, 91.1% of the units experience total savings that are slightly lower than the sum of the individual savings, while 8.9% see total savings that slightly exceed the sum of the individual impacts. The slightly lower savings observed in most units result from overlapping effects among the measures. For example, both attic insulation and advanced air sealing reduce heat loss, but their impacts partially overlap, so the combined effect is not fully additive.

For the 8.9% of dwelling units that achieve higher savings from the envelope package, the benefit comes from the HVAC performance curve, particularly for cooling. The envelope package yields greater improvements in cooling energy consumption per unit of delivered cooling than the sum of the individual measures. Although it reduces delivered cooling less than the sum of the individual measures, the efficiency gains result in higher overall energy savings.
