## Air Sealing

Air sealing involves identifying and closing large and small gaps, cracks, and penetrations in the home's primary or secondary pressure boundaries, such as around windows, doors, attic hatches, and plumbing or wiring holes (DOE, 2025). These leaks, whether large or small, accumulate and can allow a substantial amount of heated or cooled air escape. By reducing air leaks, air sealing improves indoor comfort, maintains more stable temperatures, and reduces the workload on the heating and cooling system. Air sealing is especially important in older homes, which typically have more leaks (Alliance, 2025). Refer to the air sealing documentation for more information on common leakage points, and best practices for sealing, and prescriptive air leakage rates based on climate zones (Phoung, Lou, &amp; White, 2025a).
