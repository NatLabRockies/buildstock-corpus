## 4.1.1 Individual Building Annual Results

Figure 1 shows the annual energy consumption of the example dwelling unit under the full envelope package upgrade and under each of the three individual measures applied separately. Among these four scenarios, heating energy use shows the largest reduction, dropping from 105.5 MBtu in the baseline to 65.3 MBtu with the full upgrade, a 38% decrease. Cooling energy use also decreases slightly, while hot water energy use remains nearly unchanged. All other end uses are unaffected.

The envelope upgrade reduced heat loss from the dwelling, which led to a slight increase in hot water energy consumption. In this example, the water heater is in an unheated basement. By preventing heat loss during winter and retaining cool air during summer, the upgrade improves overall thermal efficiency. However, because the unit is heating-dominated, the reduction in heat loss during winter is more significant than the benefit of retaining cool air in summer. As a result, less ambient heat is released into the unheated basement, causing the water heater to work harder and use slightly more energy to maintain the desired water temperature.

The comprehensive envelope upgrade package reduces the dwelling unit's annual total energy use from 165 MBtu to 122 MBtu, a 26% reduction. In contrast, advanced air sealing, duct sealing and insulation, and attic floor insulation individually reduced energy consumption by 18%, 8%, and 1%, respectively. This demonstrates that the full envelope package maximizes energy efficiency, highlighting the synergistic benefits of combining all three measures.

Figure 2 further breaks down the annual energy savings for the dwelling unit, showing the impact of each individual measure and the combined effect of the full envelope upgrade package. Advanced air sealing provides the largest savings at 30.2 MBtu, followed by duct sealing and insulation (12.8 MBtu). Attic floor insulation alone contributes a modest 2.4 MBtu. When all measures are implemented together, total savings reach 42.5 MBtu, which is slightly lower than the sum of the individual impacts. However, with only one example dwelling unit, it is difficult to determine whether combined measures consistently result in higher or lower savings than the sum of individual impacts. A broader assessment will be conducted in the stock-level analysis in Section 4.2.

As shown in Table 4, unmet heating hours either remained at 0.25 or were reduced to zero after the envelope upgrade, while unmet cooling hours consistently decreased across all scenarios. The full envelope package provides the most significant improvement, reducing unmet cooling hours from 291.5 to 31.5, an 89% reduction.

Table 4. Heating and Cooling Unmet Hours Before and After the Envelope Upgrades

| Upgrades                                                                    |   Heating Unmet Hours (hr) |   Cooling Unmet Hours (hr) |

| Advanced Air Sealing                                                        |                       0.00 |                     193.00 |
| Attic Floor Insulation                                                      |                       0.25 |                     240.25 |
| Duct Sealing and Insulation                                                 |                       0.00 |                      71.71 |
| Advanced Air Sealing + Attic Floor Insulation + Duct Sealing and Insulation |                       0.00 |                      31.50 |
