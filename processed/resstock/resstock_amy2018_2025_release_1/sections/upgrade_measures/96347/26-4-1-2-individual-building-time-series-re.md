## 4.1.2 Individual Building Time-Series Results

Section 4.1.1 shows that only heating- and cooling-related energy consumption changed significantly due to the envelope upgrades. Therefore, this section analyzes 15-minute time-series data for these end uses under the full envelope package upgrade and each individual measures applied separately. Heating end uses include heating and heating fans/pumps. Cooling end uses include cooling and cooling fans/pumps. January 2 is selected to examine the heating time-series data, while July 2 is selected to examine the cooling time-series data.

Figure 3 shows that each envelope upgrade reduces heating energy consumption compared to the baseline. The combined upgrade package (dark blue line) consistently shows the lowest heating energy consumption throughout the day, indicating cumulative savings from all measures. The sudden drop in heating energy consumption at 2:15 and the rise at 11:15 are caused by changes in the heating setpoint temperature. At 2:00, the setpoint drops from 70°F to 67°F, eliminating the need for heating and resulting in zero energy use from 2:00 to 2:15. At 11:00, the setpoint increases from 67°F to 70°F, triggering a spike in heating energy use from 11:00 to 11:15. After that, heating energy consumption returns to normal.

The baseline runs at about 2.5 to 3 kWh and the package at about 1.7 to 1.9.

Figure 4 shows that each envelope upgrade reduces cooling energy consumption compared to the baseline. The combined upgrade package (dark blue line) consistently results in the lowest cooling energy use from 12:45 to 22:00, when cooling demand is high, indicating cumulative savings from all measures. During 0:00-12:15 and 23:30-24:00 when cooling demand is minimal or absent in the baseline model, cooling energy consumption increases after the envelope package upgrade. This increase is due to the reduced air exchange and heat loss resulting from the improved building envelope. In the baseline model, natural cooling occurred during these hours through air leakage and lower outdoor temperatures. After the upgrade, the dwelling unit becomes more airtight and better insulated, reducing passive cooling and resulting in small cooling loads in the upgraded model to maintain indoor comfort.

The sudden rise in cooling energy consumption at 0:15 is driven by changes in the cooling setpoint temperature of this example dwelling unit. At 0:00, the setpoint drops from 72°F to 70°F, triggering a spike in cooling energy use from 0:00 to 0:15. Afterward, cooling energy consumption returns to normal.
