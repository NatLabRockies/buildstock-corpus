## Executive Summary

This report documents the development and simulation of the Electric Vehicle (EV) Adoption with Level 1 (L1) Charging measure within ResStock™. ResStock is a physics-based, bottom-up modeling tool designed to represent the diversity of the U.S. housing stock and estimate building energy use and bills for scenarios at both regional and national scales.

The EV Adoption with L1 Charging measure adds one EV and one L1 charger to every occupied dwelling unit. This approach models a future scenario to capture the effects of widespread EV adoption and L1 charging on the residential energy sector. Results show that full adoption increases national residential site energy use by approximately 7.2%, driven entirely by additional EV charging demand. The broad applicability of the measure allows users to combine results with the baseline and other EV scenarios to simulate different levels of adoption and participation. These custom scenarios can be designed to model EV technology penetration and behavior, and leverage ResStock granularity to construct adoption scenarios based on housing attributes or occupant demographics.

This measure provides a method for analyzing the interactions between residential energy demand and transportation electrification. By aligning charging behavior with other occupantdriven loads, users can evaluate strategies to balance household energy needs with grid reliability. The results highlight the growing influence of EV adoption on residential energy consumption and the importance of planning for increased electricity demand as the transportation sector electrifies. As EV adoption expands and charging technologies advance, this work supports data-driven decisions that promote a more efficient, resilient, and affordable energy future.
