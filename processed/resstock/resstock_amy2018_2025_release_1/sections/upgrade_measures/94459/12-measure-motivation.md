## Measure Motivation

Utilities, auto manufacturers, charging providers, and EV supply equipment manufacturers are working together to develop technologies (such as smart chargers) and services (such as time-of use rates) that enable efficient EV-grid integration. With potential continued EV adoption (over 10% of sales in the United States in 2024 [IEA 2025] and 25.3% of sales in California [California Energy Commission 2025] in 2024), coordination between the residential energy sector, transportation sector, and electric grid will play a key role in maintaining affordability and reliability. Without such coordination, increases in peak demand may require costly grid upgrades, increase electricity prices, and increase the risk of outages or degraded service.

This ResStock ™ (Reyna et al. 2025) measure assigns one EV and an L1 charger to each occupied dwelling unit model that does not own an EV in the baseline scenario. We apply only to units without an existing EV so that Level 2 (L2) chargers are not downgraded to L1 and households with L1 chargers in the baseline result in no change. By modeling widespread adoption of EVs and chargers, ResStock users can combine models from both the baseline and the measure to create their own unique EV saturation scenarios. This allows nuanced scenarios in which only segments of households adopt EVs or shift their charging load. Such a method can be used to model EV and charger adoption scenarios and examine the resulting energy and power impacts on the residential building sector. The simultaneous charging of EVs at multiple dwelling units in this measure can also quantify the potential stress that a local electric grid may incur during peak charging hours.
