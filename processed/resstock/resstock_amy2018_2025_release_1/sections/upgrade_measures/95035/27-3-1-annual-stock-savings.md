## 3.1 Annual Stock Savings

Figure 1 shows the annual energy consumption for baseline, two-stage GHP, and two-stage GHP plus envelope improvements. The top figure shows the results for all dwellings, regardless of whether the measure applies, while the bottom figure only shows results for applicable portions of the housing stock. Results for the whole stock are relevant for some groups, such as utilities, who might care how the total load changes in their service territory with the understanding that this measure will not improve all houses. Results that focus only on the applicable portion of the stock make it easier to understand how this measure affects energy use directly. Note that for this measure about 18% of the stock does not have a GHP measure apply, largely due to a lack of ducts in the baseline building. Adding envelope improvements increases the amount of energy savings over just installing a two-stage GHP by about 10% for the entire housing stock and about 13% for the portion of the stock that can receive a GHP upgrade. While both heating and cooling energy consumption are reduced, most of the savings come from reducing heating energy use as the U.S. housing stock overall is heating dominated.

The top figure shows all dwellings, including those the GHP upgrade may not apply to, while the bottom figure is filtered to only show applicable buildings in both the baseline and upgrade scenario.

Figure 2 shows the annual energy consumption for the portion of the housing stock this upgrade applies to split out by Building America climate zone (Antonopoulos, Gilbride, Margiotta, Cragin, &amp; Kaltreider, 2022). As envelope improvements reduce loads, some savings are seen in almost all climate zones. The one exception is the marine climate zone, which sees a slight increase as the higher infiltration can sometimes reduce HVAC energy consumption during very mild conditions. The largest incremental increase in savings is seen in cold climate zones, which also have the largest portion of the housing stock. The reduction in heating load and associated energy consumption provides most of the savings in these climate zones. Note that for all climate zones installing a two-stage GHP provides most of the savings, while additional envelope improvements further increase the savings.

Figure 2. Annual energy consumption of dwellings in the baseline and with two-stage GHP upgrades and two-stage GHP plus envelope upgrades by climate zone.

Figure 3 shows the annual energy consumption subdivided based on the baseline heating fuel. Cases with existing electric heating are generally replacing electric resistance equipment, such as an electric furnace, with a more efficient GHP. Note that because ducts are required in the baseline for this upgrade to apply, homes with electric baseboards are excluded here. The largest absolute savings occurs in homes that use natural gas for heating, which also make up most of the applicable housing stock. Adding envelope improvements on top of installing a two-stage GHP can further increase energy savings by 10-15% depending on the baseline heating fuel.
