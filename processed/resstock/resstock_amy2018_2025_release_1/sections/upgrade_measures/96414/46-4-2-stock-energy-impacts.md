## 4.2  Stock Energy Impacts

Across the entire stock, including dwellings that are not applicable, this measure upgrade yields a modeled total energy consumption decrease of 9.3%, as shown in Figure 5. Figure 6 presents total energy consumption for only the dwellings applicable to the upgrade. When considering only these dwellings, total savings from the baseline are 16.5%. These savings are driven by a 27.9% decrease in heating energy (including backup heating energy) and a 9.7% decrease in cooling energy from the baseline to the measure upgrade scenario.

Figure 7 shows the same consumption as Figure 6 but sorted by baseline heating fuel. Most absolute savings occur in dwellings with natural gas as the original fuel, where annual energy decreases by 17.4%. Households using fuel oil see their total energy decrease by 22.2%, while those using propane see a total energy decrease of 1.5%.

Figure 7. Total energy consumption by dwellings that are applicable to the upgrade, sorted by baseline heating fuel

![Image](96414_images/image_000008_5327cce2aff3415e86bcd096f8e4a227a20c5e31542e74b31ed20a2165e96af7.png)

Figure 8 presents annual total energy consumption sorted by Building America climate zone. The greatest savings occurs in the Cold and Mixed-Humid climates, with 495 TBtu (15.1%) and 364 TBtu (19.6%) savings, respectively, from the dual fuel system upgrade. Percent savings decrease in warmer climate zones because homes require less heating energy. Savings are also minimal in the Very Cold climate zone, as fewer heating hours occur above the switchover temperature of 35°F, resulting in less use of the efficient electric ASHP.
