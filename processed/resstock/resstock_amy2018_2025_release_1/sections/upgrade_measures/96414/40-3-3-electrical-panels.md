## 3.3  Electrical Panels

Electrical panel constraint modeling was added to ResStock via OpenStudio-HPXML measures in 2025. As new electric loads are added into the home during an improvement project, the electrical panel may not have enough capacity or space headroom to accommodate these new circuits. Thus, the panel may become constrained, requiring an electrical upgrade that may include upsizing the service entrance. Panel constraints can also be mitigated in other ways without the panel or service being upsized, by using load shedding devices, circuit sharing devices, or a smart panel to allow more loads to be connected; low-power, combination, or smart appliances to reduce electric loads at the source; slim tandem breakers or subpanels for more space; and more (Armstrong, et al. 2022). The panel constraint estimate from the upgrade measure allows for the costing of any potential electrical upgrade or mitigation, thus helping to capture the total cost of upgrade more holistically across the housing stock.

Panel capacity and space constraints are modeled using a service load and a breaker space calculation, respectively. The load calculation is based on 2023 National Electrical Code's loadbased method in Section 220 (National Fire Protection Association 2023), which guides how the load diversity should be estimated and summed to estimate the total load connected to the panel. When this total load converted to ampacity exceeds the service rating of the panel, the panel is considered capacity-constrained in ResStock. Similarly, the panel is considered spaceconstrained when the total occupied breaker space counted from the bottom-up exceeds the rated space count of the panel. The panel's capacity and space ratings are probabilistically sampled based on other housing characteristics such as heating fuel and the presence of certain appliances. Users are encouraged to reevaluate these constraint metrics according to other considerations, such as a margin of safety to account for uncertainty in the modeling process. For more details on panel constraint modeling, refer to the ResStock Technical Reference Guide (Reyna, et al. 2025).
