## 4.1.2 Single Dwelling Time-Series Results

Time-series results from summer and winter days for both dwelling test models are shown below. Figure 1 shows a slight decrease in energy consumption for Dwelling 1 during most of the summer day. The cooling mode of the ASHP is only slightly more efficient than the baseline AC in Dwelling 1, and the cooling fan in the upgrade consumes more energy than the baseline because of a slightly larger cooling unit. Additionally, the cooling loads for this day are quite low due to the low temperatures in WI on this day, so there is less potential for savings. In Figure 2 a slight but more significant decrease in total energy is seen for Dwelling 2 during a summer day. This slight decrease in energy consumption is due to the more efficient cooling mode of the ASHP. Unlike Dwelling 1, the size of the cooling unit is the same as baseline.

During winter, both Dwelling 1 and Dwelling 2 use natural gas backup systems because outdoor temperatures are below the switchover temperature of 35°F. Figure 3 shows that Dwelling 1 uses natural gas backup until about 16:00, when the temperature rises above 35°F and the ASHP meets the heating load. Some energy appears as ASHP backup in the figure because the fan used during natural gas backup operation requires electricity. Dwelling displays a similar pattern in Figure 4, with the natural gas backup switching on and off as the outdoor temperature fluctuates above and below the switchover temperature of 35°F.
