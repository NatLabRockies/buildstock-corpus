## 1.1  Introduction to Technology

A dual fuel heating system combines two technologies: an ASHP and a natural gas furnace. These systems operate together using the same indoor air handler (fan blower) and ductwork to distribute conditioned air throughout the home. The system is designed to alternate between the ASHP and the furnace depending on outdoor temperature.

In this configuration, the ASHP coil is typically installed in line with the furnace, positioned so that air flows through it after passing through the furnace's heat exchanger. During mild to moderately cold weather, the ASHP serves as the primary heating source, transferring heat from the outdoor air into the home using a refrigerant and a compressor.

As outdoor temperatures fall, the heating capacity of the ASHP decreases. At a specific outdoor temperature, known as the switchover temperature or balance point, the system stops the ASHP and starts the natural gas furnace. From this temperature and below, the furnace provides all heating to the home and operates independently when the ASHP is inactive.

The switchover temperature can be set manually or managed by a thermostat that monitors outdoor conditions and determines which system should be active. This arrangement allows for coordinated operation of the two systems without overlap in heating delivery.

This type of system is commonly used in regions with a wide range of outdoor temperatures and where natural gas is available. More detailed information about the individual ASHP and furnace components is provided below.
