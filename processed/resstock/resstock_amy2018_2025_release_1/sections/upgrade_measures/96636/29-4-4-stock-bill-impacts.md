## 4.4  Stock Bill Impacts

The upgrade can result in a total of $8.49 billion in energy bill savings when applied across the housing stock. Most savings would occur from electricity bills, as shown in Figure 12.

Figure 12. Total annual housing stock energy bill savings by home heating fuel type

![Image](96636_images/image_000013_f55150d53ff544dc9aeddf77adf1e59342cf2d5dc5f5b4291da0eaf76fc9d01f.png)

In dwellings heated with electricity, a total of $4.37 billion could be saved annually with the upgrade, as shown in Figure 12. For dwellings heated with natural gas, 93% of total annual housing stock energy bill savings are derived from electricity, indicating most housing stock annual energy bill savings for this upgrade are sourced from cooling efficiency improvements. Figure 13 shows how the upgrade impacts bill savings across HVAC cooling efficiency.

Figure 13. Annual bill savings by HVAC cooling efficiency

![Image](96636_images/image_000014_163bb4e76ad8aa7b6cc1a374790e36ba8623fdd0dd5951c2dfd6a835ceda195d.png)

Like the findings from energy savings, lower efficiency, fully conditioned air conditioning systems have higher energy bill savings than higher efficiency or room air conditioning systems, as shown in Figure 13 and detailed in Table 10.

Savings for HVAC cooling efficiencies with ' AC, SEER 15 ' show the lowest absolute bill savings and percent bill savings, in large part because a cooling upgrade was not applicable but a furnace upgrade was applicable.
