## 4.2  Aggregated Building Time Series

Colorado is selected as a case study to analyze the aggregated time-series data. Out of all national dwelling units, 1.7% are in Colorado. Figure 4 presents the aggregated average daily baseline energy consumption in Colorado, with February 20 identified as the day with the highest energy use. To further explore this peak, Figure 5 examines the aggregated average power on February 20 in Colorado, comparing conditions before and after the upgrade.

Figure 4: line chart of daily aggregated average energy consumption (kBtu, 100 to 800) over January to December 2018 for the baseline in Colorado. Consumption is highest and most variable in winter, peaking near 780 kBtu in late February, falls through spring to a flat summer floor of roughly 110 kBtu from June to September, then rises again from October to about 700 kBtu by late December.

Figure 5 shows that the upgrade scenario results in slightly lower power demand throughout the day compared to the baseline.

Figure 5: line chart of aggregated average power (kW, about 7 to 13) over the 24 hours of February 20, 2018, the peak day in Colorado, with one line each for Baseline and Upgrade. Both follow the same shape: a morning peak of nearly 13 kW around 07:30, a trough of about 7 kW near 15:30 and an evening plateau near 9 kW. The upgrade line runs slightly below the baseline for most of the day.
