## 1.3.4  Heating Seasonal Performance Factor, Version 2

HSPF2 is a rating representing the heating efficiency of heat pumps as they perform over an entire heating season. It is defined as the total heating output (in Btu) during a typical heating season divided by the total electrical energy input, including the energy consumed by the indoor blower fan (in watt-hours) and outdoor unit over that same period. Higher HSPF2 indicates more efficient systems, since they deliver more heating per unit of electricity. Factors such as on-off cycling, coil defrost cycles, and standby power consumption can reduce HSPF2.

HSPF2 is determined under standardized test conditions, which simulate a range of outdoor temperature and part-load conditions to approximate real-world performance throughout the heating season. Residential central air conditioning heat pumps manufactured and distributed in commerce, as defined by 42 U.S.C. 6291(16), must meet the energy conservation standards specified in the Code of Federal Regulations at 10 CFR 420.32(c)(5) (DOE 2025a).

In this study, dwellings with existing systems below the regional minimums are modeled with ducted heat pumps rated at 7.5 HSPF2. For dwellings whose current heating equipment already meets or exceeds these thresholds, we simulate a like-for-like replacement at the higher efficiency.
