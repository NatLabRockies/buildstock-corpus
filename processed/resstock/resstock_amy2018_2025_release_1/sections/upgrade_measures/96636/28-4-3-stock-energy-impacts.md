## 4.3  Stock Energy Impacts

Out of 550,000 model building simulation (i.e., models), 413,611 models are applicable for the reference space heating and air conditioning upgrade (75.2%). For the 413,611 models, the total baseline consumption is 2.87 trillion kWh annually, and after the upgrade is 2.80 trillion kWh, a 2% savings. Figure 6 shows the total site energy consumption between the baseline and upgrade.

Figure 7: horizontal stacked bar chart of all-fuels end-use consumption (kWh, 0 to about 2.4T) for three HVAC heating types, Ducted Heat Pump, Ducted Heating and Non-Ducted Heating, each with a Baseline bar and an upgrade bar (labelled Minimum Efficiency Furnace). Segments are end uses by fuel. Ducted Heating dominates at roughly 2.3T kWh, against about 0.35T for Ducted Heat Pump and 0.15T for Non-Ducted Heating; the upgrade bars are only slightly shorter.

Cooling from electricity in ducted heating systems accounts for 42,000 GWh of energy reduction, the greatest energy reduction after the upgrade; in comparison, heating from natural gas in ducted heating systems was reduced by 6,000 GWh. Cooling fans and pumps (i.e., air handler fan energy) also yielded 6,400 GWh in energy savings, whereas heating fans and pumps yielded 1,300 GWh in energy savings. Energy savings from fans and pumps are primarily a result of more advanced motors and controls.

Given that this is a reference case and relatively few HVAC systems are below minimum efficiency, only modest energy savings are expected: 4% for electricity-heated homes, 2% for natural gas, 3% for fuel oil, and 1% for propane.

Figure 8: horizontal stacked bar chart of average all-fuels end-use consumption per dwelling (kWh, 0 to 35k) for ten HVAC cooling efficiency classes, each with a Baseline bar and an upgrade bar (labelled Minimum Efficiency Furnace). Segments are end uses by fuel. AC, SEER 8 is the longest pair, about 34k kWh in the baseline and visibly shorter after the upgrade; Ducted Heat Pump is the shortest, near 16k kWh, and most pairs barely differ.

On average, dwellings upgraded from an ' AC, SEER 8 ' cooling system to the minimum efficiency resulted in 2,900 kWh in energy savings, the largest average savings when segmented by HVAC cooling efficiency. In contrast, ' AC, SEER 15 ' shows 107 kWh in average per-dwelling annual energy savings -all sourced from heating end uses and indicating efficiency gains in heating systems rather than cooling systems. Please note that the central AC cooling efficiencies are measured in SEER1 as the baseline condition. Conversion to SEER2 in the baseline condition may be a feature in future versions of ResStock outputs.

Upgrading dwellings from a lower to a higher efficiency air conditioning system can be effective for reducing energy consumption, as shown in Figure 11. For example, ' AC, SEER 8 ' and ' AC, SEER 10 ' can result in average annual savings of 2,892 kWh and 1,749 kWh, respectively.
