## 1.3.1 Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency

AFUE is the standard rated efficiency for how efficiently a furnace converts fuel into heat at rated conditions. The higher the AFUE rating, the more efficient the system is at converting fuel into usable heat. Energy lost through exhaust gases, radiation, and other components decreases efficiency.

AFUE is the total annual heat output divided by the total annual fuel input -on a scale of 0% to 100%. Note that unlike SEER2 and HSPF2, AFUE does not include the electricity used by the air handler blower, combustion blower, ignition system, or controls. ANSI/ASHRAE 103-2022 provides a procedure for determining AFUE of residential central furnaces and boilers, which is a test cycle structured to simulate ordinary heating conditions throughout the year -including seasons (ANSI/ASHRAE 2022).

' Residential furnaces manufactured and distributed in commerce, as defined by 42 U.S.C. 6291(16) must meet the energy conservation standards specified in the Code of Federal Regulations at 10 CFR 430.32 ' (DOE 2025b). 80% AFUE furnaces are the minimum for non-weatherized gas furnaces (i.e., natural gas and propane) manufactured from 2015 through Dec. 18, 2028. After Dec. 18, 2028, the minimum is 95% AFUE. If an investigator would like to understand equipment standard changes over time, a useful approach could blend data from this study with data from the Propane and Fuel Oil Furnaces upgrade (Stenger et al. 2026b) as well as the Natural Gas Furnaces upgrade (Stenger et al. 2026a), both of which model 95% AFUE for propane and natural gas furnaces, respectively. In this study, we model an 80% AFUE as minimum efficiency natural gas furnace when existing efficiency is rated lower than 80% AFUE, and a like-for-like replacement when the existing natural gas furnace is rated above 80% AFUE.
