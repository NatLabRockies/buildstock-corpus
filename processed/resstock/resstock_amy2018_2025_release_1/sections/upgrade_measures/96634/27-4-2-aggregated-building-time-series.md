## 4.2  Aggregated Building Time Series

Colorado is selected as a case study to analyze the aggregated time-series data. Out of all national dwelling units, 1.7% are in Colorado. Figure 4 presents the aggregated average daily baseline energy consumption in Colorado, with February 20 identified as the day with the highest energy use. To further explore this peak, Figure 5 examines the aggregated average power on February 20 in Colorado, comparing conditions before and after the upgrade.

Figure 4: line chart of daily aggregated average energy consumption (kBtu, 0 to 800) over January to December 2018 for the baseline in Colorado. Consumption is highest and most variable in winter, peaking near 780 kBtu in late February, falls through spring to a flat summer floor of roughly 110 kBtu from June to September, then rises again from October to about 700 kBtu by late December.

Figure 5 shows that the upgrade scenario consistently results in lower power demand throughout the day compared to the baseline.

Figure 5: line chart of aggregated average power (kW, about 6 to 13) over the 24 hours of February 20, 2018, the peak day in Colorado, with one line each for Baseline and Upgrade. Both follow the same shape: a morning peak near 07:00 (about 12.9 kW baseline, 11.9 kW upgrade), a trough near 15:00 (about 7.0 and 6.5 kW) and an evening plateau near 9 kW. The upgrade line stays below the baseline all day.
