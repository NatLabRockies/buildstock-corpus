## 4.3  Stock Energy Impacts

Out of 550,000 model building simulations (i.e., models), 408,800 models are applicable for the natural gas fuel furnace upgrade (74.3%), representing the 139,647,020 dwellings from American Community Survey's 2021, 5 -year estimate of U.S. households (not including territories).

For the applicable models, the total baseline consumption is 2.85 trillion kWh per year, and after the natural gas furnace upgrade, the total consumption is 2.67 trillion kWh per year, a 6.3% savings.

Figure 7 demonstrates a mixed effect for energy savings, where heating for natural gas increases (i.e., negative energy savings) by 157 billion kWh in ducted heat pumps, 185 billion kWh in ducted heating, and 38 billion kWh in non-ducted heating.

The modeling approach also removes cooling for dwellings heated with ducted heat pumps -as shown by the 87.5 billion kWh in energy savings from electricity for cooling. The distribution of annual energy savings per dwelling are shown in Figure 8.

Figure 8: violin plots with box plots of site energy savings per dwelling (kWh, about -40k to 50k), one row per baseline HVAC heating efficiency, each labelled with its model count (n=4075 for ASHP SEER 10 up to n=134906 for Fuel Furnace 80% AFUE). Fuel Furnace 60% AFUE has the highest median, near 10k kWh; Fuel Furnace 92.5% AFUE and the electric resistance systems cluster near zero. Model counts match Table 5.

Upgrading fuel furnace efficiency when lower than 95% AFUE yields energy savings, where dwellings with 60% AFUE fuel furnaces can save up to 30,800 kWh annually (i.e., upper fence of box plot), as shown in Figure 8. In contrast, ASHPs show a mixed effect for energy savings, where more efficient ASHPs -like ASHP, SEER 15, 7.7 HSPF -have a median of 1,178 kWh energy savings and as much as a 9,289 kWh increase in energy (i.e., lower fence of box plot).

In other words, energy use can increase among dwellings with ASHPs, even with the removal of cooling as a service, after the transition from ASHP to natural gas furnace. In addition, boilers and wall/floor furnaces demonstrate a range of mixed effects on annual energy savings per dwelling. Figure 9: horizontal stacked bar chart of average all-fuels end-use savings per dwelling (kWh, about -22k to 20k) by baseline HVAC heating efficiency, with segments for each end use and fuel. Heating segments dominate every row; negative segments are the natural gas heating added by the upgrade. Fuel Boiler 90% AFUE is the widest bar, from about -22k to +20k kWh. The three ASHP rows also show cooling electricity savings.

As shown in Figure 9, natural gas heating results in negative energy savings (i.e., energy consumption increases). Interestingly, boilers, wall/floor furnaces, ASHPs, and electric baseboard heating demonstrate the mixed effect of energy savings after the upgrade -on average, 90% AFUE fuel boilers (1,934 kWh), 100% efficiency electric baseboard (1,633 kWh), 100% AFUE electric boiler (1,142 kWh), and 100% AFUE electric wall furnaces (1,572 kWh) show an average annual increase in energy consumption per dwelling. This average increase can be caused by a decrease in energy efficiency and fuel switching to a less efficient site energy (i.e., from electric to natural gas).
