## 4.2.1  Annual Stock Energy Analysis

Figure 5 shows the annual site energy consumption by end use before and after the envelope package upgrades for all homes. The annual stock energy savings from the envelope package upgrade is 938 TWh, compared to a pre-upgrade average consumption of 3,454 TWh-representing a 27.2% reduction. Most savings occured in heating and cooling end uses. This includes 43% (110 TWh) electricity heating savings, 48% (556 TWh) natural gas heating savings, 48% (79 TWh) fuel oil heating savings, 42% (55 TWh) propane heating savings, 72% (16 TWh) electricity heating heat pump backup savings, 24% (91 TWh) electricity cooling savings, 46% (17 TWh) electricity heating fans and pumps savings, and 23% (15 TWh) electricity cooling fans and pumps savings.

Table 5 shows the annual energy consumption saving percentage per upgraded dwelling unit. As discussed earlier, most savings were observed in heating and cooling end uses. However, there is only a small change in hot water-related end uses. When both the water heater and ducts are in the same place (e.g., unheated basement, attic), hot water energy consumption can be affected slightly due to reduced heat transfer from the ducts. If the water heater is in the attic, it can be affected by improved attic floor insulation. Additional details are available in the duct sealing and attic floor insulation documentation (Lou &amp; Liu, ResStock 2025 Release 1 SDR Duct Sealing and Insulation Upgrade Documentation, 2025) (Phoung, Lou, &amp; White, 2025b). Furthermore, indoor temperature changes also influence hot water energy use. Overall, the impact on hot water energy consumption is less than 0.1%.

There is also a very small change in appliance electricity use, primarily for refrigerators. Refrigerators work by transferring heat from the interior to the surrounding air. When the surrounding air is cooler, a refrigerator operates more efficiently and uses less energy. The average indoor temperature over the year increased slightly from 71.28°F to 71.68°F. This slight increase in temperature results in a minor increase in refrigerator energy use. However, the impact is negligible-the change in appliance electricity consumption is 0.27%.
