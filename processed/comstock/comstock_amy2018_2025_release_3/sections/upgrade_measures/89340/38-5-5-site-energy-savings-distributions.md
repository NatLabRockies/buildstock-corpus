## 5.5  Site Energy Savings Distributions

Figure 11 through Figure 14 show the percent site energy and energy use intensity (EUI) savings distributions by end use and climate zones, respectively. Percent savings provide relative impact of the measure at the individual building level while site EUI savings provide absolute (or aggregated) scale of impact. Also, the data points that appear above some of the distributions indicate outliers in the distribution, meaning they fall outside 1.5 times the interquartile range. The value for n indicates the number of ComStock models that were applicable for energy savings for the fuel type category.

Figure 14 of the ComStock thermostat-control-for-load-shedding measure documentation shows the distribution of site energy use intensity savings by ASHRAE climate zone. Fifteen horizontal violin plots with embedded box plots use the same zone rows and counts as Figure 13: 1A (n=49), 2A (n=321), 2B (n=47), 3A (n=373), 3B (n=233), 3C (n=107), 4A (n=899), 4B (n=10), 4C (n=106), 5A (n=756), 5B (n=99), 6A (n=137), 6B (n=5), 7 (n=8), and 7A (n=8). The x-axis reads 'Site EUI Savings by Climate Zone' in kBtu per square foot, spanning -0.8 to +1.8, an absolute intensity rather than a percentage despite the caption's wording. Medians cluster tightly just above zero, roughly 0.1 to 0.3 kBtu per square foot, for nearly every zone, with scattered positive outliers. The small-sample zones 4B and 6B show the widest bodies, reaching past 1.0, and zone 7A collapses to a narrow blob near zero. Compared with Figure 13, the intensity view compresses the differences between zones: the measure produces a relatively larger percent energy impact at the building level, but savings are modest once normalized by floor area.

Both figures show a relatively larger energy impact compared to the EUI savings, indicating that the measure plays a more significant role in energy performance at the building level, but the savings are not significant once adjusted for building floor area. Highlights of savings presented in the figures include:

- 2%-10% savings on electric heating systems,
- Around 5% savings on fans, associated with reduced heating and/or cooling operations during the peak window,
- Around 3% savings on electric cooling systems.

The measure is effective for various weather zones, indicating that the demand flexibility opportunities are not weather-exclusive.
