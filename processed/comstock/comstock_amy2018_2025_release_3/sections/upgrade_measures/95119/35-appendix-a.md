![Figure A-1. Appendix stacked bar comparison of annual site energy consumption for applicable buildings only, across the ComStock baseline and the two HP-RTU measure scenarios](95119_images/image_000014_f528627979d566f4ffba0f24d9bb681f0fb1540a559691b302cd3e5109bafa23.png)

Figure A-1 is the applicable-buildings-only counterpart to Figure 7: a stacked bar chart of annual site energy consumption in trillion British thermal units (TBtu), segmented by end use and fuel with the same 18-entry legend, for three scenarios with totals printed above each bar. Baseline 1890 TBtu, HPRTU_Std_Perf 1541 TBtu, HPRTU_Lab_Data 1542 TBtu -- a reduction of about 18%, much larger than the 7.2% full-stock figure, because buildings that cannot receive the measure are excluded from the denominator. As in Figure 7 the two measure scenarios are essentially identical at 1541 versus 1542 TBtu. Inside the bars, Fans Electricity falls from 257.9 to about 187 TBtu, Cooling Electricity falls from 243.2 to 217.3 TBtu, and a Heating Electricity segment of roughly 178-179 TBtu appears in the measure scenarios. Interior Equipment Electricity holds at 279.3 TBtu across all three bars. Totals are read with high confidence; individual segment values are provisional.

The plot only includes buildings applicable to the HP-RTU measure scenarios.

![Figure A-2. Appendix violin and box plot distribution of site energy use intensity savings in kBtu per square foot by end use and fuel type for the HP-RTU lab data measure scenario](95119_images/image_000015_05bdb189d991bc273a14655c22bba733acc68a9be265b522db881cf9f1e95e5c.png)

Figure A-2 repeats Figure 9 using absolute intensity rather than percentage, which removes the small-denominator distortion that makes a few percent-savings outliers look extreme. It is a horizontal violin plot with embedded box plots annotated "Upgrade 5.0: HPRTU_Lab_Data (unweighted)", with the x-axis "Site EUI Savings by End Use (kBtu/ft2)" running from about -80 to +120 and a reference line at 0. Rows top to bottom, each labeled "Intensity", with model counts, grouped by fuel: Other Fuel -- Water Systems (n=119), Heating (n=3287); Natural Gas -- Water Systems (n=1541), Heating (n=16594); Electricity -- Water Systems (n=1690), Refrigeration (n=6028), Interior Equipment (n=27), Heating (n=33932), Heat Recovery (n=826), Fans (n=34342), Cooling (n=34323). Nearly every distribution is tight against 0 on this absolute scale. The two clear positive rows are Other Fuel Heating and Natural Gas Heating, with boxes spanning roughly 0 to 25 kBtu/ft2 and long right tails past 100. Consistent with the body text, no end use shows a notable EUI increase. Note the Electricity Heating count here (n=33932) is far larger than in the percent-savings version (n=14092), because an intensity difference is defined even for models with no baseline electric heating.

The data points that appear above some of the distributions indicate outliers in the distribution, meaning they fall outside 1.5 times the interquartile range. The value for n indicates the number of ComStock models that were applicable for energy savings for the fuel type category. Each data point represents the pairwise comparison between a ComStock baseline model and the corresponding model after the measure scenario is applied.
