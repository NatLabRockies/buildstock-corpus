## 5.5  Other Findings

The HP-RTU Standard Performance and Lab Data measure scenarios show similar average total COPs by state, with only a few states showing any difference at this aggregation level (Figure 12). This again highlights the consistency between the two data sources used to model similar class HP-RTUs. The COPs show lower average values in colder climates where more supplemental heating is required given the performance and sizing scheme used in this work. Electric supplemental heat has a COP of 1, so a higher prevalence of supplemental heat would bring the total average COPs closer to 1 in this calculation.

Figure 12 is a pair of state-level choropleth maps of the contiguous United States, titled "HPRTU_Std_Perf" (left) and "HPRTU_Lab_Data" (right), showing the annual average total heating coefficient of performance (COP) for the median building in each state. Each state carries a printed numeric value, and a diverging blue-to-orange color scale runs from higher COP (blue) to lower COP (orange or red). Values span roughly 1.7 to 2.8. The geographic pattern is consistent across both panels: the northern tier -- the northern Rockies, northern plains, upper Midwest, and interior Northeast -- is shaded orange with the lowest COPs, while the South, Southwest, and West Coast are blue with the highest. That is the expected consequence of capacity and efficiency derating in cold weather, mapped in Figures 2 and 3. The two panels are very similar to each other, with the lab-data panel showing slightly different values in some northern states, consistent with the roughly 1% stock-level difference reported elsewhere in the document. Individual per-state values are small and dense; the range and the geographic trend are read with confidence but specific state assignments are provisional.

COPs presented include impacts of supplement heat, crankcase heat, and reverse-cycle defrost, but not supply fan.
