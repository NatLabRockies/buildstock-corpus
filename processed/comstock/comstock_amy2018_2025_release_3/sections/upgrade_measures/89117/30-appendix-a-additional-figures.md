Figure A-1. Grouped vertical bar chart, y-axis "Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)" (the source uses lowercase "tbtu") from 0 to about 350, x-axis "Census Division Name", legend "Upgrade Name" with dark blue "Baseline" and light blue "Advanced RTU Controls". Nine divisions in geographic order: New England, Middle Atlantic, East North Central, West North Central, South Atlantic, East South Central, West South Central, Mountain, Pacific. East North Central is by far the largest gas consumer at roughly 360 TBtu, followed by Pacific near 230 and Middle Atlantic near 180; East South Central and New England are the smallest at roughly 60-67. The measure bar is at or slightly below the baseline bar in every division - the reduction is small on this scale, a few percent at most - and it is visually indistinguishable from the baseline in West South Central, Mountain and Pacific. The bars carry no data labels and the pixel-read heights do not reconcile with Figure 8's printed totals to better than about 3%, so only ordering and approximate magnitudes are given here. The stock-level gas heating saving is 18.1 TBtu (2.1%).

Figure A-2. Grouped vertical bar chart, y-axis "Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)" from 0 to about 220, x-axis "Comstock Building Type", legend "Upgrade Name" with dark blue "Baseline" and light blue "Advanced RTU Controls". Fourteen building types: FullServiceRestaurant, QuickServiceRestaurant, RetailStripmall, RetailStandalone, SmallOffice, MediumOffice, LargeOffice, PrimarySchool, SecondarySchool, Outpatient, Hospital, SmallHotel, LargeHotel, Warehouse. RetailStripmall is the largest gas consumer at roughly 215 TBtu, then SecondarySchool near 177, FullServiceRestaurant near 170 and PrimarySchool near 126; SmallHotel is the smallest at under 10. The measure reduces gas most in FullServiceRestaurant, RetailStripmall, SecondarySchool and PrimarySchool. Notably, four types show a small gas INCREASE - RetailStandalone, SmallOffice, MediumOffice and Warehouse - which is the fan heat penalty section 5.5 discusses, observed in about 11.5% of applicable buildings. LargeOffice, Outpatient, Hospital, SmallHotel and LargeHotel are essentially unchanged. The bars carry no data labels, so only ordering and approximate magnitudes are given.

Figure A-3. Grouped vertical bar chart, y-axis "Electricity Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)" from 0 to about 450, x-axis "Comstock Building Type", legend "Upgrade Name" with dark blue "Baseline" and light blue "Advanced RTU Controls". The same fourteen building types in the same order as Figure A-2. RetailStripmall (roughly 450 TBtu baseline) and Warehouse (roughly 440) are the largest electricity consumers, followed by LargeOffice near 315, RetailStandalone near 290 and SecondarySchool and PrimarySchool near 240. Unlike the gas chart, the measure reduces electricity in nearly every building type, and the reductions are visible at this scale: the largest absolute drops are in RetailStripmall, Warehouse and RetailStandalone, and the largest proportional drops are in FullServiceRestaurant, QuickServiceRestaurant, RetailStripmall and SmallOffice, all around 10%. LargeHotel, Hospital, Outpatient, SmallHotel and LargeOffice are essentially unchanged - LargeOffice and LargeHotel being predominantly served by system types the measure does not apply to. The bars carry no data labels, so only ordering and approximate magnitudes are given.

Figure A-4. Grouped vertical bar chart, y-axis "Electricity Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)" from 0 to about 640, x-axis "Census Division Name", legend "Upgrade Name" with dark blue "Baseline" and light blue "Advanced RTU Controls". The same nine census divisions in the same order as Figure A-1. South Atlantic is the largest electricity consumer at roughly 640 TBtu baseline, then Pacific near 525, East North Central near 450, West South Central near 400 and Middle Atlantic near 335; New England, West North Central, East South Central and Mountain all sit near 195-210. Electricity falls in every division. The Pacific division shows by far the largest proportional reduction, on the order of 14%, against roughly 3% to 7% elsewhere - consistent with Figure 14, where the California-only climate zone 3C and the hot-dry zone 3B carry the highest median savings, and with the DEER-based modelling methods used in California. The bars carry no data labels, so only ordering and approximate magnitudes are given.
