Appendix grouped vertical bar chart. The y-axis is "Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (TBtu)" from 0 to 250; the x-axis is "Census Division Name" with nine divisions; the legend "Upgrade Name" distinguishes a dark blue Baseline bar from a light blue HP-RTU bar in each group. Bars are not labelled, so the following are pixel reads against the tick scale and are approximate to within a couple of TBtu. New England 54 to 46; Middle Atlantic 166 to 147; East North Central 245 to 175; West North Central 90 to 67; South Atlantic 169 to 154; East South Central 46 to 39; West South Central 100 to 90; Mountain 70 to 61; Pacific 175 to 150. East North Central is both the largest gas consumer and the largest absolute saver, dropping about 70 TBtu; the Pacific and Middle Atlantic divisions each drop roughly 20-25 TBtu. Summed across divisions the chart gives about 1,116 TBtu of baseline gas falling to about 929 TBtu, a reduction of roughly 187 TBtu, which corroborates the 190 TBtu of stock gas heating savings reported in section 5.2. Figure A-2 shows the same totals by building type.

Appendix grouped vertical bar chart. The y-axis is "Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (TBtu)" from 0 to 300; the x-axis is "Comstock Building Type" with fourteen types; the legend "Upgrade Name" distinguishes a dark blue Baseline bar from a light blue HP-RTU bar. Bars are unlabelled, so the following are approximate pixel reads. FullServiceRestaurant 289 to 257; QuickServiceRestaurant 76 to 70; RetailStripmall 240 to 199; RetailStandalone 42 to 10; SmallOffice 27 to 8; MediumOffice 31 to 27; LargeOffice 40 to 38; PrimarySchool 132 to 113; SecondarySchool 53 to 46; Outpatient 18 to 12; Hospital 59 to 59; SmallHotel 9 to 8; LargeHotel 53 to 55; Warehouse 38 to 18. Restaurants and stripmall retail dominate the baseline gas total, but restaurants save proportionally little because much of their gas is kitchen equipment the measure does not touch, whereas RetailStandalone, SmallOffice and Warehouse lose most of their gas use. Hospital, SmallHotel and LargeHotel are essentially unchanged, consistent with their HVAC systems not being applicable RTUs. The chart totals about 1,107 TBtu falling to about 921 TBtu, a reduction near 186 TBtu, which agrees with Figure A-1 and with section 5.2's 190 TBtu.

Appendix grouped vertical bar chart. The y-axis is "Electricity Total Energy Consumption (TBtu)" from 0 to 600; the x-axis is "Census Division Name" with nine divisions; the legend "Upgrade Name" distinguishes a dark blue Baseline bar from a light blue HP-RTU bar. Bars are unlabelled, so the following are approximate pixel reads. New England 127 to 118; Middle Atlantic 380 to 359; East North Central 401 to 393; West North Central 186 to 182; South Atlantic 655 to 600; East South Central 161 to 144; West South Central 427 to 385; Mountain 194 to 178; Pacific 469 to 401. The South Atlantic division is the largest electricity consumer, and the Pacific and South Atlantic divisions show the largest absolute savings, roughly 68 and 55 TBtu. East North Central and West North Central show very small electricity savings, consistent with the body text: those cold divisions have the most gas-to-electric heating conversion, whose added electric heating offsets the cooling and fan savings. The chart totals about 3,000 TBtu falling to about 2,759 TBtu, a reduction near 241 TBtu, matching Figure A-3's independent total.
