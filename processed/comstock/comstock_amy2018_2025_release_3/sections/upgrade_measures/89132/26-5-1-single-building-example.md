## 5.1  Single Building Example

This section will demonstrate the functionality of this measure on a single building model. The building is a 37,500-square-foot small hotel in Toole County, Montana (climate zone 6B). This building starts with a PTAC system with electric coils. After retrofitting this system with a packaged GHP system, the resulting borefield contained 56 boreholes, each with a depth of 125 meters. The default borehole spacing is 5 meters and the borefield shape is set to 'near-square', therefore this is a 7-by-8 borefield with an area of approximately 1050 square meters. The existing packaged single-zone system had an average direct-expansion cooling COP of 3.45, and the new water-to-air GHPs have an average cooling COP of 6.2. The existing gas coils had an efficiency of 1.0, and the new water-to-air GHPs have an average heating COP of 3.8. The vertical ground heat exchanger object was sized using the GHEDesigner tool [16].

The energy consumption of the building broken out by end use is plotted in Figure 4 before and after the GHP measure was applied. This building experienced total site energy savings of 6%, which includes a 52% decrease in cooling and a 55% increase in electric heating. Pumps also had an increase in consumption and fans had a decrease in consumption because of the heat pump installation. When the GHP is installed, an additional pump is added to the loop, so we expect an increase in pump energy. Fan energy saw a decrease because the new GHP system allows for more outdoor air compared with the PTACs in the baseline, which can reduce the demands on cooling and fans during advantageous times of year.

Two stacked column charts comparing annual end-use energy consumption in kilowatt-hours for one example building (a rural Montana model) before and after the Console GHP upgrade. The left chart, Total Energy Consumption by End Use (kWh), includes all loads; its y-axis runs to 1,200,000 kWh and both columns top out just under 1,000,000 kWh, with the Console GHP column slightly shorter than Baseline. The right chart, HVAC Loads Only, uses a y-axis to 250,000 kWh; the Baseline column reaches roughly 200,000 kWh and the Console GHP column roughly 150,000 kWh, a visibly larger proportional reduction than in the all-loads view. The shared legend covers Electricity Interior Equipment, Natural Gas Interior Equipment, Natural Gas Water Systems, Electricity Interior Lighting, Electricity Exterior Lighting, Electricity Cooling, Electricity Fans, Electricity Heating, and Electricity Pumps. The accompanying text reports 6% total site energy savings for this building, a 52% decrease in cooling and a 55% increase in electric heating, with pump energy up (an added loop pump) and fan energy down. Peak load fell 8.9%, emissions 6.3%, and the annual electricity bill 9.0% ($5,433).

The peak load of the building was reduced by 8.9% as result of the GHP installation. The estimated total emissions from the building decreased by 6.3% as a result of nearly full electrification of the upgrade. While the heating was already electric in the baseline, the energy savings because of the heat pump reduced the total emissions of the building. Typical utility bills for the building are calculated via ComStock for a variety of rate structures available in the utility region of this building. No electricity rate structures were in the utility rate database for this rural Montana building, so the average electricity price of $0.09/kWh for the state was used for the utility bill calculation. After the Console GHP upgrade was applied to this building, the annual electricity bill is estimated to decrease by 9.0% ($5,433 USD).
