![Figure B-1. Grid of 17 histograms showing the distribution of soil thermal conductivity in W/m-K among ComStock model samples, one panel per IECC climate zone from 1A through 8AK](89132_images/image_000016_3b34cb0c2f6132057a2d2ec52dd3fca9e479de6de051a46044a6cfb6e52735e9.png)

Grid of 17 small histograms, one per IECC 2012 climate zone, showing how soil thermal conductivity is distributed across the ComStock sample within each zone. Panels are titled Climate Zone: 1A, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, 3C, 4A, 4B, 4C, 5A, 5B, 6A, 6B, 7A, 7AK, 7B, and 8AK. Each panel shares an x-axis of Thermal Conductivity (W/m-k) from 0 to 4 and has its own y-axis of Samples, so bar heights are not comparable between panels. Sample counts differ by orders of magnitude: the populous zones 2A, 3A, 5A, and 5B hold thousands of models (5A peaks above 6,000 and 2A above 5,000), while 3C, 4C, 7A, 7AK, 7B, and 8AK hold only tens. Most zones are strongly unimodal with a sharp mode just below 2.0 W/m-K and mass concentrated between about 1.5 and 2.5 W/m-K. Zone 7B is the clear exception, distinctly bimodal with a large group near 0.5 to 1.0 W/m-K and a second group near 2.0 to 2.5 W/m-K. Zone 2B and 8AK also show a broader left shoulder. Soil conductivity is a GHEDesigner input that drives required borefield length, so these distributions govern how much ground heat exchanger each model receives.
