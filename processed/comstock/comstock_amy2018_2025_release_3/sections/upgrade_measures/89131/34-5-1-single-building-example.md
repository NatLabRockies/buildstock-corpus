## 5.1  Single Building Example

This section will demonstrate the functionality of this measure on a single building model. The building is a 17,500-square-foot office building in Kansas City, Kansas (climate zone 4A). This building starts with a PSZ RTU with gas coils. After retrofitting this system with a Packaged GHP, the resulting borefield contained 56 boreholes, each with a depth of 427 feet (130 meters). The ground heat exchanger was sized using the GHEDesigner workflow described earlier. The existing PSZ system had an average direct-expansion cooling COP of 4.3, and the new water-toair GHPs have an average cooling COP of 6.7. The existing gas coils had an efficiency of 0.8, while the new water-to-air GHPs have an average heating COP of 3.84.

The energy consumption of the building broken out by end use is plotted in Figure 5 before and after the GHP measure was applied. This building experienced total site energy savings of 22%, which includes a 94% decrease in natural gas and a 25% decrease in electricity. Because the water heating load is natural gas, the building was not completely electrified as a result of the upgrade. However, the natural gas heating load was eliminated and replaced with a smaller electric heat pump load, which is due to the heating efficiency improvements of switching to the GHPs. The cooling load also decreased due to the higher COP of the heat pump, but fan and pump energy increased by a nontrivial amount. These increases are due to the additional equipment added to the loop, as well as reduced supply temperatures resulting in higher flow rates and thus increasing fan and pumping requirements.

Two stacked column charts comparing annual end-use energy consumption in kilowatt-hours for the single example building, a 17,500-square-foot office in Kansas City, Kansas (climate zone 4A), before and after the Packaged GHP upgrade. The left chart, Total Energy Consumption by End Use (kWh), covers all loads; its y-axis runs to 600,000 kWh, the Baseline column reaches roughly 510,000 kWh and the Packaged GHP column roughly 430,000 kWh. The right chart, HVAC Loads Only, uses a y-axis to 350,000 kWh; Baseline reaches roughly 330,000 kWh and Packaged GHP roughly 220,000 kWh, a much larger proportional reduction than the all-loads view. The body text reports 22% total site energy savings for this building, a 94% decrease in natural gas and a 25% decrease in electricity; natural gas heating is eliminated and replaced by a smaller electric heat pump load, cooling falls (COP 4.3 to 6.7), and fan and pump energy rise. Gas water heating remains, so the building is not fully electrified. Its borefield holds 56 boreholes at 427 feet each.

The peak load of the building was reduced by 14.7% as result of the GHP installation. The estimated total emissions from the building decreased by 4.5% because of nearly complete electrification of the building. In addition, typical utility bills for the building were calculated via ComStock for a variety of electricity rate structures available in the utility region of this building. After the Packaged GHP upgrade was applied to this building, the median annual electricity bill increased by 24.5% ($7,697 USD) while the average increased by 19.8% ($6,254 USD). The natural gas bill, however, experienced a 95.0% reduction ($8,051 USD). As a result, the total estimated annual energy bill across all fuels for this building is expected to decrease slightly, despite the increased electric load, as can be seen in Figure 6. However, this could vary based on the actual electricity rate structure used by the building.
