## 5.6  Climate Zone Impacts

Figure 12 shows the percentage of total site energy savings by climate zone. There is a clear trend of increased savings as we move from the hottest climate zone (1A - Hot Humid) to the coldest climate zone (8 - Subarctic). In warmest climate zones (1A through 3A), the Packaged GHP upgrade results in 10% savings or less on average, whereas in colder climate zones (5 through 7) we notice average energy savings up to 15%-40%.

The lower quartile (25%) of buildings in climate zones 1A-3A show an increase in site energy with the upgrade applied. Further investigation of these buildings revealed that large increases in fan and pump energy were the driving end uses behind the increased site energy consumption. In hot climates, cooling tends to dominate the building load, meaning heat is rejected to the ground for much of the year and ground temperatures increase over time. This imbalanced load can result in increased pumping energy to maintain temperatures within a desired range on the main heat pump condenser loop. Compressor heat can also exacerbate imbalanced loads. In climates with a more balanced heating and cooling load, the ground temperatures remain more stable through the year, and over the course of many years of operation. As such, in climate zones 6 and 7, very few buildings show an increase in site energy.

Despite cold climates experiencing more significant air temperature swings throughout the year, the ground temperature stays relatively stable, resulting in consistent and efficient performance of geothermal heat pumps year-round. Because cooling loads are present during most parts of the year in commercial buildings, cold climates tend to exhibit more balanced load profiles. Geothermal heat pumps can be more advantageous in climates with more balanced heating and cooling loads because of the long-term ground temperature implications on the borefield size. For this reason, the warm climates might require more costly installation and realize lower savings compared to a building of similar size in a cold climate.

Distribution plot of percent site energy savings by climate zone for the Packaged GHP upgrade, one horizontal violin with an inner box plot per zone, with a vertical reference line at zero savings. The x-axis, Percent Site Energy Savings by Climate Zone (%), runs from about -60 to 80. Zone rows top to bottom with model counts: 1A (n=4382), 2A (n=31154), 2B (n=4600), 3A (n=36655), 3B (n=21039), 3C (n=6233), 4A (n=54936), 4B (n=1822), 4C (n=8311), 5A (n=59314), 5B (n=11638), 6A (n=13591), 6B (n=1781), 7 (n=346), 7A (n=1343), 7B (n=165). The medians march steadily rightward from the hottest to the coldest zones. Zones 1A through 3A average 10% savings or less and their lower quartiles sit left of zero, meaning a quarter of those buildings use more site energy after the upgrade, driven by increased fan and pump energy where cooling-dominated loads push heat into the ground year after year and raise ground temperatures. Zones 5 through 7 average roughly 15% to 40% savings and very few models in zones 6 and 7 show any increase, because commercial buildings in cold climates carry year-round cooling loads and so present a more balanced, thermally stable borefield load.
