## 5.2  Stock Energy Impacts

Figure 10 and Figure 11 show the comparison of annual site energy consumption between the baseline and the upgrade scenario for the entire building stock and buildings that are only applicable to the upgrade, respectively. The economizer measure with fault (described in section 3.3) reflected in the stock models demonstrates 0.3% total site energy savings (14 trillion British thermal units [TBtu]) for the U.S. commercial building stock modeled in ComStock. The savings are primarily attributed to:

- 2.0% stock cooling electricity savings (13.6 TBtu)
- 1.5% stock district cooling savings (1.4 TBtu)
- 0.9% stock pump electricity savings (0.4 TBtu)
- -0.1% stock fan electricity savings ( -0.7 TBtu)
- -0.1% stock heating gas savings (0.7 TBtu).

Figure 10 (Section 5.2, Stock Energy Impacts) compares annual site energy consumption for the ENTIRE ComStock building stock between the baseline and the economizer upgrade. Two stacked bars are plotted on an "Annual Energy Consumption (TBtu)" axis from 0 to 4500, with printed totals of 4338 for Baseline and 4324 for Economizer - a reduction of 14 TBtu, or 0.3%, matching the body text. Each bar is segmented by end use crossed with fuel, with eighteen legend entries running from Interior Equipment at the bottom through Fans, Cooling, Interior Lighting, Heating, Water Systems and Exterior Lighting to Pumps, Refrigeration, Heat Recovery and Heat Rejection at the top. The two bars are nearly identical in appearance because the savings are small relative to the stock. The changes, all printed on the segments, are concentrated in three end uses: cooling electricity falls from 667.3 to 653.7 TBtu, fan electricity rises from 519.1 to 519.8, and natural gas for heating rises from 854.9 to 855.6. Interior equipment (739.7 electricity, 212.1 gas) and interior lighting (451.9) are unchanged. For the full per-segment breakdown, including the district and other-fuel segments, refer to the savings bullets in Section 5.2.

This figure (Section 5.2) is the companion to Figure 10, restricted to only the buildings that are APPLICABLE to the economizer upgrade rather than the entire stock. It is printed as "Figure 11" in the body but has no List of Figures entry, and it is the extra figure that pushes every later printed caption one above its List of Figures number. Two stacked bars are plotted on an "Annual Energy Consumption (TBtu)" axis from 0 to about 2700, with printed totals of 2639 for Baseline and 2624 for Economizer. The same eighteen end-use and fuel segments appear as in Figure 10, and the two bars again look nearly identical. The changes, all printed on the segments, are the same absolute quantities as in the stock-wide figure but a larger share of a smaller base: cooling electricity falls from 430.0 to 416.4 TBtu, a 3.2% reduction against 2.0% stock-wide; fan electricity rises from 347.4 to 348.2; and natural gas for heating rises from 483.9 to 484.6. Interior equipment (454.6 electricity, 134.3 gas) and interior lighting (281.9) are unchanged. The applicable subset accounts for roughly 61% of stock site energy and about 64% of stock cooling electricity.

Electricity savings for cooling comes from leveraging colder outdoor air when spaces need cooling, which is highlighted in detail in Figure 9. Economizer requirements have been in the energy code in many climates for a long time, thus the opportunity and magnitude of the savings are limited by buildings that are not applicable to this upgrade because they already have economizers. More detailed findings are described in the following sections.
