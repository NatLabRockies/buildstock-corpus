## 5.3.3 Stock Utility Bill Impacts

Figure 16 shows the utility bill cost savings comparison between the upgrade packages with different objectives. Again, the demand flexibility strategy with an individual building peak objective shows higher bill cost savings potential than controls with a grid peak objective, although it also reveals a higher potential of negative savings risk. Further breaking down the mean bill into different categories of utility rate structures in Figure 17 shows that the larger savings come from both peak reduction for demand charges and energy reduction for energy charges.

Figure 16. Comparison of annual electricity utility bill cost savings between the ComStock baseline and demand flexibility upgrade packages with different objectives, for applicable buildings

Figure 16 is a horizontal box plot on an x axis of 'Electricity Utility Bill Cost Savings [%]' with ticks from -3 to 10, comparing the two objectives directly. Six rows are grouped into two blocks of three: the individual building peak reduction objective (teal) and the grid peak reduction objective (orange), each with 'With Max Bill', 'With Mean Bill' and 'With Min Bill'. No values are printed; boxes were measured against the tick labels (zero at 162.5 px, 21.11 px per percent). Individual peak objective, giving Q1, median, Q3 and whisker range: Max Bill 2.82/3.86/5.19, -0.78 to 8.79; Mean Bill 2.96/4.00/5.28, -0.59 to 8.88; Min Bill 2.91/3.96/5.33, -0.78 to 9.02. Grid peak objective: Max Bill 0.92/1.82/2.72, -1.92 to 5.57; Mean Bill 0.97/1.82/2.72, -1.87 to 5.52; Min Bill 0.88/1.87/2.82, -2.11 to 5.80. The individual objective saves about 2.1 percentage points more at the median under every rate scenario, and its lower whisker stops nearer zero, so on this figure it also carries the smaller downside; the surrounding text describes the opposite ordering.

![Grouped bar chart of electricity utility bill cost split into energy charge, flat demand charge, time-of-use demand charge and total electricity, for the baseline and both objectives](92504_images/image_000018_af32b7899921778a11a8da36209978ea46e837e01c34d5dcdd3076a96f56a7d6.png)

Figure 17 is a grouped bar chart with the y axis labeled 'Mean electricity utility bill cost [billion $]'. Four groups of three bars are plotted; within each group the bars are baseline, individual peak reduction objective and grid peak reduction objective. No values are printed, so bar tops were measured against the y-axis tick labels (zero at 291.9 px, 1.194 px per billion dollars). Energy charge 164.8, 159.8, 159.0; demand charge under a flat rate 93.7, 89.5, 90.3; demand charge under a time-of-use rate 82.0, 78.6, 78.6; total electricity 207.5, 199.2, 200.8. Total electricity bill savings are therefore about 8.3 billion dollars for the individual objective and 6.7 billion for the grid objective. The clearest difference between the two objectives is in the flat demand charge, where the individual objective is about 0.8 billion dollars lower; the energy-charge gap between the two is at the one-pixel resolution limit and is not asserted. The four groups are not additive, and the magnitudes are roughly double the stock electricity bill shown in Figure 5, so the aggregation behind this axis label is unclear.
