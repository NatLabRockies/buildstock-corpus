## 5.1.3 Stock Utility Bill Impacts

The annual utility bill impacts are presented in this section, representing additional co-benefits when targeting individual building peak demand reductions. Because we apply many electricity utility rate structures that are available for a building located in a certain geographical location, our data includes many annual utility bills per building model. Figure 5 shows the comparison between two scenarios (i.e., baseline and package) and includes three different electricity utility bill statistics (i.e., maximum, mean, and minimum) at the stock level. Overall, it shows around 2% of savings among the different rates, attributed to reduced costs of electricity consumption and/or demand charges. The comparison in Figure 6 highlights the three statistics across all electric utility bill costs for applicable buildings and breaks down the scenarios of both electric heating+cooling and cooling only, which yields increased bill savings by around 4%. In addition, the capability to flex the load is higher with both electric heating and cooling, and thus the energy bill cost savings are slightly higher as compared to electric cooling only.

Figure 7 is a violin plot titled 'Upgrade 52.0: Package_6 (unweighted)' with the x axis labeled 'Percent Utility Bill Savings by Fuel (%)' running from -140 to 100. Five rows are plotted top to bottom with their sample counts printed at the right: Propane Bill State Average (n = 491), Fuel Oil Bill State Average (n = 1206), Natural Gas Bill State Average (n = 32139), Electricity Bill with Mean Rate (n = 64778) and Bill Mean (n = 64778). Zero savings sits at 315 px and the scale is 1.16 px per percent. The two electricity-driven rows are tightly positive: the electricity bill row's interquartile box spans only about 3.45% to 4.31% and the overall bill mean row spans 2.59% to 4.31%, with whiskers running from about -128% to +83%. Natural gas is centered near zero but its whiskers reach the full -99% to +99% span of the drawn distribution, propane runs -8.6% to +50% and fuel oil -7.8% to +16.4%. The interquartile boxes for propane, fuel oil and natural gas are narrower than the plotted line width and cannot be resolved. The pattern is that the package delivers a consistent few percent off electricity bills while fossil fuel bills are essentially unchanged at the median but extremely dispersed.

The data points that appear above some of the distributions indicate outliers in the distribution, meaning they fall outside 1.5 times the interquartile range. The value for n indicates the number of ComStock models that were applicable for energy savings for the fuel type category.
