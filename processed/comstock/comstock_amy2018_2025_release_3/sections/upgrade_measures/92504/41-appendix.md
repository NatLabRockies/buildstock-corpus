Figure A-1 is a grouped bar chart with the y axis labeled 'Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)' and one pair of bars per census division: baseline in dark blue and Package 6 in light blue. No values are printed; bar tops were measured against the y-axis tick labels (zero at 164.8 px, 0.406 px per tbtu, so one pixel is about 2.5 tbtu). Baseline consumption by division: New England about 59, Middle Atlantic about 241, East North Central about 384, West North Central about 155, South Atlantic about 162, East South Central about 64, West South Central about 123, Mountain about 98 and Pacific about 212 tbtu, summing to roughly 1500 tbtu. East North Central is the largest natural gas consumer by a wide margin, followed by Middle Atlantic and Pacific. Every Package 6 bar is within one pixel of its baseline partner, so the package's 4.9 tbtu national natural gas heating penalty from Table 6 is not resolvable at this plot scale in any single division; the correct reading of this figure is that natural gas consumption is essentially unchanged everywhere.

The only building types whose consumption falls are Small Office, Medium Office, Large Office, Primary School, Secondary School and Warehouse, exactly the six applicable types listed in Section 3.1, which is a useful internal consistency check on the applicability logic.

Figure A-4 is a grouped bar chart with the y axis labeled 'Electricity Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)' and one pair of bars per census division: baseline in dark blue and Package 6 in light blue. No values are printed; bar tops were measured against the y-axis tick labels (zero at 163.5 px, 0.226 px per unit, so one pixel is about 4.4 tbtu). Baseline then Package 6: New England 95 and 95; Middle Atlantic 339 and 335; East North Central 379 and 374; West North Central 219 and 215; South Atlantic 689 and 676; East South Central 202 and 197; West South Central 467 and 459; Mountain 215 and 215; Pacific 476 and 467. The baseline sums to roughly 3080 tbtu. South Atlantic is both the largest electricity consumer, at more than a fifth of the national total, and the largest absolute saver at about 13 tbtu. Every measured reduction is an exact multiple of one pixel, and the measured national total of about 53 tbtu is slightly below the 56.1 tbtu reported in Table 6 because of that quantization.
