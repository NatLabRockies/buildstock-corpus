## 5.5  Site Energy Savings Distributions

Figure 9 and Figure 10 show the percent site energy and end-use intensity (EUI) savings distributions by end use and climate zones, respectively. Percent savings provide relative impact of the measure at the individual building level while site EUI savings provide absolute (or aggregated) scale of impact. Also, the data points that appear above some of the distributions indicate outliers in the distribution, meaning they fall outside 1.5 times the interquartile range. The value for n indicates the number of ComStock models that were applicable for energy savings for the fuel type category.

Nearly every distribution is a narrow spike centered on zero in both panels, consistent with a load-shifting measure that leaves annual energy essentially unchanged. Natural Gas Heating is the conspicuous exception, with a long left tail reaching roughly -40% in the percent panel and the widest EUI spread, reflecting a small number of models where the altered cooling setpoint schedule incidentally changed heating operation. Electricity Heating and Electricity Cooling show modest two-sided spread; scattered points beyond the whiskers are outliers past 1.5 times the interquartile range.

Two stacked violin plot panels giving the distribution of savings across individual applicable ComStock models grouped by ASHRAE climate zone. The top panel's x-axis is Percent Site Energy Savings by Climate Zone (%), spanning -6 to 8; the bottom panel's is Site EUI Savings by Climate Zone (kBtu/ft2), spanning -1 to 1.6. Both panels share the same zone rows with model counts: 1A (n=49), 2A (n=318), 2B (n=47), 3A (n=372), 3B (n=233), 3C (n=106), 4A (n=893), 4B (n=10), 4C (n=106), 5A (n=750), 5B (n=99), 6A (n=136), 6B (n=5), 7 (n=8), 7A (n=8). Every zone is centered essentially on zero with a slight negative bias, meaning a marginal increase in annual site energy, and the bulk of each violin lies within about a percent of zero. Spread is widest in the zones with the largest samples, notably 3B, 4A and 6A, whose whiskers extend to roughly -4% to -6%; the sparsely sampled zones 4B, 6B, 7 and 7A are correspondingly narrow and should not be over-interpreted. There is no systematic climate gradient in the result, which is consistent with the measure changing load timing rather than annual consumption. Points beyond the whiskers are outliers past 1.5 times the interquartile range.

Similar to the stock-level energy savings findings in Section 5.3, most of the savings are neutral or slightly negative for both energy and EUI. Highlights of savings presented in the figures include:

- Both figures show relatively larger energy impact compared to the EUI savings (Figure 3), indicating that the measure plays a more significant role in energy performance at the building level, but the savings are not significant once adjusted for building floor area.

- Hotter climates (smaller climate zone number) yield increased energy use (more negative savings), indicating higher potential of generating new peak load in pre-cooling period with higher outdoor temperatures.
