Grouped vertical bar chart from the appendix quality-assurance set, plotting Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (TBtu) against Census Division Name, with a dark blue Baseline bar and a light blue upgrade bar in each pair. Divisions run left to right: New England, Middle Atlantic, East North Central, West North Central, South Atlantic, East South Central, West South Central, Mountain, Pacific. East North Central is the largest gas consumer at roughly 335 TBtu, followed by Middle Atlantic near 215 TBtu and Pacific near 145 TBtu; South Atlantic and West North Central sit near 135 and 115 TBtu, while New England, East South Central, West South Central and Mountain all fall between roughly 50 and 90 TBtu. In every division the baseline and upgrade bars are the same height to visual resolution, confirming that the measure has no effect on natural gas consumption. That is expected, since the load shifting strategy adjusts only cooling setpoints in large offices with electric HVAC. The figure serves as a quality check that the measure did not perturb non-target fuels or non-applicable regions.

Grouped vertical bar chart plotting Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (TBtu) against ComStock Building Type, with a dark blue Baseline bar and a light blue upgrade bar in each pair. Building types run left to right: FullServiceRestaurant, QuickServiceRestaurant, RetailStripmall, RetailStandalone, SmallOffice, MediumOffice, LargeOffice, PrimarySchool, SecondarySchool, Outpatient, Hospital, SmallHotel, LargeHotel, Warehouse. RetailStripmall is the largest gas consumer at roughly 190 TBtu, with SecondarySchool near 165 TBtu, FullServiceRestaurant near 155 TBtu, Warehouse near 145 TBtu and PrimarySchool near 120 TBtu; SmallHotel is the smallest at roughly 10 TBtu and Outpatient near 30 TBtu. LargeOffice, the only applicable building type, sits near 45 TBtu and is unchanged between baseline and upgrade, as is every other type. The complete absence of change is the intended reading: the measure adjusts cooling setpoints only, so it cannot affect gas consumption even in the buildings it is applied to, and it must leave all non-applicable building types untouched. This is a quality-assurance check rather than a results figure.

RetailStripmall is the largest electricity consumer at roughly 395 TBtu, followed by Warehouse near 360 TBtu, LargeOffice near 290 TBtu and RetailStandalone near 280 TBtu; SecondarySchool and PrimarySchool sit near 230 TBtu, SmallOffice and MediumOffice and LargeHotel near 190 to 200 TBtu, FullServiceRestaurant near 175 TBtu, Hospital near 165 TBtu, Outpatient near 125 TBtu, QuickServiceRestaurant near 65 TBtu and SmallHotel near 35 TBtu. Even for LargeOffice, the one applicable type, the baseline and upgrade bars are indistinguishable at this scale, which is the expected signature of a measure that relocates electricity use within the day rather than reducing it. Annual totals are the wrong resolution to see the effect.

Grouped vertical bar chart plotting Electricity Total Energy Consumption (TBtu) against Census Division Name, with a dark blue Baseline bar and a light blue upgrade bar in each pair. Divisions run left to right: New England, Middle Atlantic, East North Central, West North Central, South Atlantic, East South Central, West South Central, Mountain, Pacific. South Atlantic dominates at roughly 650 TBtu, followed by West South Central near 420 TBtu, Pacific and East North Central near 395 to 400 TBtu, and Middle Atlantic near 355 TBtu; West North Central and Mountain sit near 200 TBtu, East South Central near 180 TBtu and New England near 120 TBtu, the smallest. The baseline and upgrade bars coincide in every division. Compared with the natural gas companion figure this is the more informative check, since electricity is the fuel the measure actually touches: it confirms that annual electricity totals are preserved region by region, so the measure is relocating consumption within the day rather than adding or removing it. Regional magnitudes here are driven by floor area and cooling climate, not by the upgrade.
