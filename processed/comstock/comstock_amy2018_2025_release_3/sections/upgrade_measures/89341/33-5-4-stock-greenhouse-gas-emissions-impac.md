## 5.4  Stock Greenhouse Gas Emissions Impact

Figure 8 shows the annual stock-level impact of the measure on greenhouse gas emissions and presents approximately 0% emission reductions for all the three grid electricity scenarios. This is as expected due to the small energy savings.

![Figure 8. Grouped stacked column chart of annual greenhouse gas emissions in MMT CO2e by fuel, baseline versus load shift, under three grid scenarios (eGRID 2021, LRMER High RE Cost 15, LRMER Low RE Cost 15), all showing 0% change](89341_images/image_000010_fcf6c5d6e7a419129749c7756eb66b840f322c532de70daa480938d9a3f131d2.png)

Greenhouse gas emissions comparison in million metric tons of CO2 equivalent, arranged as three panels, one per electricity grid scenario, each holding a Baseline and a Load shift stacked column. Fuels are color-coded: Electricity (blue), Natural Gas (yellow), Fuel Oil (green), Propane (orange). Totals are printed above the bars with percent change on the upgrade column. eGRID 2021: 402 versus 402 (0%), electricity 314 in both, natural gas 81 in both. LRMER High RE Cost 15: 325 versus 325 (0%), electricity 237, natural gas 81. LRMER Low RE Cost 15: 240 versus 240 (0%), electricity 152, natural gas 81. Every labeled value is identical between baseline and upgrade in every scenario, giving approximately 0% stock-level emissions reduction. This is the expected result: the measure shifts when electricity is consumed rather than reducing how much, and the annual emissions accounting used here does not capture the intra-day timing benefit. Absolute totals fall from eGRID through LRMER High RE Cost to LRMER Low RE Cost as the assumed grid becomes cleaner, while non-electric fuels stay fixed across panels.
