![Grouped bar chart of annual site natural gas consumption in TBtu for the nine U.S. census divisions, baseline against measure](89120_images/image_000020_d0b24dcec8aeb63c3b59af21d53ca33f1dccff0dc1a9af8f2c29c2c72e730af2.png)

Figure A-1. Grouped vertical bar chart with x 'Census Division Name' and y 'Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)', ticked 0 to 350. The legend 'Upgrade Name' pairs a dark blue Baseline bar with a light blue Unoccupied OA Control bar in each of nine groups. The bars carry no printed values, so these readings are measured off the axis and are good to about 2.5 TBtu. Baseline then measure: New England 68 with no measurable change; Middle Atlantic 184 to 176; East North Central 364 to 349; West North Central 120 to 115; South Atlantic 127 to 122; East South Central 63 to 60; West South Central 85 with no measurable change; Mountain 70 to 68; Pacific 228 to 196. East North Central is the largest gas consumer and Pacific shows by far the largest proportional drop, about 14%, consistent with Figure 10's much higher savings in climate zones 3B and 3C. The bars sum to about 1,308 TBtu baseline and 1,239 TBtu with the measure, a 69 TBtu saving - noticeably more than the 50.5 TBtu of section 5.2. Note the stray word 'Figure' baked into the bitmap's top-left corner.

Figure A-2. Grouped vertical bar chart with x 'Census Division Name' and y 'Electricity Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)', ticked 0 to 600. The legend 'Upgrade Name' pairs a dark blue Baseline bar with a light blue Unoccupied OA Control bar in each of nine groups. No values are printed, so these readings are measured off the axis and are good to about 4 TBtu. Baseline then measure: New England 202 to 198; Middle Atlantic 333 to 324; East North Central 450 to 441; West North Central 215 to 207; South Atlantic 637 to 620; East South Central 220 to 211; West South Central 393 to 385; Mountain 198 to 193; Pacific 528 to 467. South Atlantic is the largest electricity consumer; Pacific again shows the largest proportional reduction, about 12%. The bars sum to about 3,176 TBtu baseline and 3,046 TBtu with the measure. That 130 TBtu saving is well above the 89.3 TBtu of electricity savings Figure 4's own labels imply, and the baseline total is about 10% higher than the same quantity disaggregated by building type in Figure A-4.

Figure A-3. Grouped vertical bar chart with x 'Comstock Building Type' and y 'Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (tbtu)', ticked 0 to 200. The legend 'Upgrade Name' pairs a dark blue Baseline bar with a light blue Unoccupied OA Control bar in each of 14 groups. No values are printed, so these readings are measured off the axis and are good to about 1.5 TBtu. Baseline then measure: FullServiceRestaurant 170 to 161; QuickServiceRestaurant 54 with no measurable change; RetailStripmall 215 to 201; RetailStandalone 83 to 73; SmallOffice 57 to 52; MediumOffice 36 to 29; LargeOffice 57 to 45; PrimarySchool 126 unchanged; SecondarySchool 176 to 173; Outpatient 27 to 26; Hospital 88 unchanged; SmallHotel 8 unchanged; LargeHotel 99 unchanged; Warehouse 95 to 83. PrimarySchool, Hospital, SmallHotel and LargeHotel come out exactly unchanged, consistent with the stated exclusions, but SecondarySchool and Outpatient do move. The bars sum to about 1,290 TBtu baseline and 1,218 with the measure, a 72 TBtu saving that agrees with Figure A-1 and exceeds section 5.2's 50.5 TBtu.
