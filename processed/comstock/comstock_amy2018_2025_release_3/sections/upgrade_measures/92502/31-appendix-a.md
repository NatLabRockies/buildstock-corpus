![Figure A-1. Grouped bar chart of annual site natural gas consumption in TBtu by U.S. census division, ComStock baseline versus the light_shed scenario](92502_images/image_000014_3cbe01015fd5dcad47b806a805a9eeca9d96f4368e61914d0c45e2fd758e9841.png)

Grouped vertical bar chart of Natural Gas Total Energy Consumption (TBtu) with one pair of bars per census division, dark blue for Baseline and light blue for light_shed. The nine divisions along the x-axis are New England, Middle Atlantic, East North Central, West North Central, South Atlantic, East South Central, West South Central, Mountain, and Pacific; the y-axis runs to 400 TBtu. East North Central is the largest gas consumer at roughly 385 TBtu, followed by Middle Atlantic near 238, Pacific near 215, South Atlantic near 165, West North Central near 160, West South Central near 125, Mountain near 100, and East South Central and New England lowest at roughly 62 to 65. Within every division the two bars are nearly indistinguishable, with the light_shed bar equal to or very slightly taller than the baseline. That small increase is the expected direction: dimming lighting removes internal heat gain and raises gas heating demand, worth -6.0 TBtu nationally per Table 6. The geographic ranking is driven by heating climate and gas infrastructure prevalence, not by the measure. Bars are unlabeled, so magnitudes are axis estimates.

Grouped vertical bar chart of Electricity Total Energy Consumption (TBtu) with one pair of bars per census division, dark blue for Baseline and light blue for light_shed, y-axis to 700 TBtu. The nine divisions along the x-axis are New England, Middle Atlantic, East North Central, West North Central, South Atlantic, East South Central, West South Central, Mountain, and Pacific. South Atlantic dominates at roughly 690 TBtu, well ahead of Pacific and West South Central near 470 to 478, East North Central near 375, Middle Atlantic near 335, Mountain and West North Central near 222 to 225, East South Central near 200, and New England lowest near 105. In every division the light_shed bar is slightly shorter than the baseline, a consistent small electricity reduction from the lighting shed, in contrast to the slight increases seen for natural gas in Figure A-1. Comparing this figure with A-1 shows the geographic pattern of the two fuels is nearly inverted: the gas-heavy northern and eastern divisions are not the electricity-heavy ones. Bars are unlabeled, so magnitudes are axis estimates.
