## 5.3  Stock Energy Impacts

The annual energy impacts in stock level are presented in this section, but these are not necessarily the target objective of demand flexibility, and the daily dispatch strategy proposed in this measure may have distinctive impacts compared to other general demand flexibility/response programs. The lighting control for the load shedding measure with peak load reduction objective demonstrates 0.52% total site energy savings (22.5 trillion British thermal units [TBtu]) for the U.S. commercial building stock modeled in ComStock, and 1.10% savings for the applicable buildings. The savings contributions by end use and fuel type are summarized in Table 6 and are illustrated in Figure 8.

Paired stacked column charts of annual site energy consumption in TBtu with per-segment values printed inside the bands and column totals above. The left panel covers the whole stock, Baseline 4838 TBtu versus light_shed 4817 TBtu; the right panel covers applicable buildings only, 2345 versus 2324. Both show the same 21 TBtu reduction, so the entire stock-level saving comes from applicable buildings. The shared legend spans 17 fuel-and-end-use categories from Heat Rejection and Heat Recovery Electricity through Pumps, Refrigeration, Exterior Lighting, the Water Systems categories, the Heating categories, Interior Lighting, Cooling, Fans, and Interior Equipment. The dominant change is Interior Lighting Electricity, falling 417.0 to 392.4 in the whole stock and 210.9 to 186.2 in applicable buildings, a 24.6 to 24.7 TBtu saving that matches Table 6. In applicable buildings Cooling Electricity falls 355.8 to 352.8 (3.0 TBtu) and Fans Electricity falls 269.3 to 268.5 (0.8 TBtu). Interior Equipment Electricity (724.7 stock, 373.2 applicable) and Water Systems Natural Gas (193.9 stock, 119.4 applicable) are unchanged. Table 6 gives the offsetting penalties: natural gas heating -6.0 TBtu and electric heating -1.2 TBtu, from lost lighting heat gain.

Energy consumption is categorized both by fuel type and end use.

The measure focuses on load shed for electric lighting systems in offices, schools and warehouses, so the energy savings are less prominent at the stock level, which includes various non-applicable building types and fuel types. The site energy increase for natural gas and electric heating are mainly attributed to the increased heating load caused by lowered heat gain from lighting equipment, which is expected whenever internal loads are reduced. On the other hand, the cooling load is reduced for the same reason and results in cooling electricity savings. The objective of the measure is to provide demand flexibility by shaving load peaks on a daily basis instead of improving energy efficiency , so the energy savings are treated as side benefits from the measure. However, the developed load shed measure is not exclusive to other energy efficiency measures; it could be integrated with them to provide demand flexibility while saving energy.
