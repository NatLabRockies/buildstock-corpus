## 5.1.1  Daily Peak Load Reduction

Figure 5 shows the load profiles and (representative) lighting schedules of the test model from simulations corresponding to baseline (orange dash line) and default upgrade (blue solid line) scenarios for comparison. Comparison between the baseline profile and the load shed window (appearing as valleys) illustrates the timing of the peak window for each day. The variation of peak window timings - morning, noon, and afternoon peaks all present in this representative week - indicates that the measure is capable of tracking the change of daily peak time and adjusting the lighting schedule accordingly, resulting in reduced peak load every day.

Two stacked time-series panels for the test small office model over five summer weekdays, showing building electric load in kW on top and the interior lighting dimming schedule below, baseline versus lighting load shed](92502_images/image_000006_ae3dfdb0cdf950c4bd85681e2aaf49aff3709df51903a5dde1d904bf0263add6.png)

Two vertically stacked time-series panels for the single-building test, a small office model, plotted over five consecutive summer weekdays 7/16 through 7/20, comparing the baseline (dashed orange) with the default lighting load shed upgrade (solid blue). The upper panel is Building load (kW) on a y-axis from 10 to 35. Each weekday shows a load hump; the baseline peaks range from roughly 32 kW on 7/16 down to about 21 kW on the later days, and on every day the blue upgrade trace is visibly clipped below the orange baseline at the peak, forming a notch. The lower panel is Lighting schedule (dimming level) on a y-axis from 40% to 100%. The baseline schedule sits near 80% during occupied hours, while the upgrade trace drops to roughly 55% to 60% in a four-hour block each weekday and returns to the baseline value afterward. The shed windows fall at different times of day across the week, with morning, noon and afternoon peaks all represented, demonstrating that the dispatch schedule tracks each day's predicted peak rather than using a fixed clock window. The default settings are a 4-hour window centered on the daily peak with a 30% absolute lighting reduction.
