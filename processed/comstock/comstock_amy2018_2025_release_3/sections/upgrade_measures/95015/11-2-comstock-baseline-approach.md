## 2  ComStock Baseline Approach

Of the buildings represented in ComStock™, approximately 26% are served by boiler heating systems; however, these systems represent more than 56% of the natural gas heating energy in the baseline stock. This measure will be applicable to the heating, ventilating, and airconditioning (HVAC) system types shown in Table 1., along with the respective percentages of the ComStock floor area and natural gas heating energy that they represent.

The current version of boilers in ComStock are gas- or fuel oil-fired, noncondensing boilers, with efficiencies generally around 80% [5]. Their efficiencies were determined using the U.S. Department of Energy's reference building templates and capacities. The values are summarized in Table 2 [5]. Three different cubic performance curves based on the part load ratio (PLR) are used to adjust the boiler efficiency, as shown in Table 3, based on size and vintage. Figure 2 shows the variations of boiler efficiency multipliers with PLR based on the three performance curves, a graphical representation of the curves' output [6]. All ComStock boilers have a heating set point of 180°F and can modulate flow based on heating load.

![Figure 2. Scatter plot of boiler efficiency multiplier as a function of boiler part load ratio for the three performance curves used in ComStock](95015_images/image_000003_18effbc7ce6772b778c0216213ef43a162be9d5ebd183127eb0f292a837e6b86.png)

Figure 2 is a scatter plot with "Boiler Part Load Ratio" (PLR) on the x-axis from 0 to 1.4 and "Boiler Efficiency Multiplier" on the y-axis from 0 to 1.4. It plots the output of the three cubic performance curves listed in Table 3. One series is flat at a multiplier of exactly 1.0 across the whole PLR range -- this is the boiler constant efficiency curve, which applies no part-load correction. The other two series are nearly coincident and rise monotonically: they start at roughly 0.75 and 0.78 at PLR 0, cross a multiplier of about 1.0 near PLR 0.4-0.5, sit near 1.0 through the middle of the range, and continue up to roughly 1.16-1.30 at PLR 1.2-1.3. These two are the "Boiler with minimum turndown" and "Boiler with no minimum turndown" curves; their coefficients differ only slightly (intercepts 0.7791 and 0.7463 per Table 3) so they overlap for most of the range and cannot be reliably told apart by eye in this plot. The practical reading is that boilers assigned a turndown curve lose roughly 20%-25% of their rated efficiency at very low load, whereas boilers on the constant curve are modeled at rated efficiency regardless of load.
