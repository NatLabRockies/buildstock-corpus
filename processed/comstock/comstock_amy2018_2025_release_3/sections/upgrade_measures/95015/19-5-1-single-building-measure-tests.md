## 5.1  Single-Building Measure Tests

This section demonstrates the electric boiler measure application on a 300,000-ft 2 large office test model in Boise, Idaho. The building has a 'VAV chiller with gas boiler reheat' HVAC system. The measure replaced the model's central 80% efficient natural gas boiler with a 100% efficient electric resistance boiler. In addition to the efficiency improvement and heating fuel switch, the boiler performance curve was updated to the curve in Figure 3. A summary of the energy use intensity, annual site energy, and heating site energy impacts are shown in Table 7.

Table 7. Energy Impacts of Electric Boiler Measure Scenario for Single-Building Example

| Baseline Scenario Electric Boiler   |                  | Measure Scenario              | Percent Savings                             |
|-------------------------------------|------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 58.8                                | 52.1             | 11.4%                         | Energy use intensity (kBtu per square foot) |
| 17,636                              | 15,635           | 11.3%                         | Annual site energy consumption (MBtu)       |
| Natural gas- 8,391 MBtu (2.5 GWh)   | Electric-1.9 GWh | 24.0% overall heating savings | Heating site energy consumption/fuel        |

This 24% savings in heating site energy is consistent with what we would expect for a 20% improvement in boiler efficiency, combined with an updated boiler performance curve. Note that these are site energy savings, which might not translate to source energy savings or bill savings due to fuel switching of the heating load. Although the annual site energy for this building was reduced by 11%, the switch to electric heating is likely to result in utility bill increases due to the price differences between natural gas and electricity. The building's exact natural gas and electricity pricing structures would be required to calculate the change in annual utility bills.

Figure 5 shows the breakdown of energy consumption by month and end use before and after the electric boiler measure scenario was applied. In the figure, the hashed bars represent natural gas end uses, and solid fill bars represent electricity. As shown, the natural gas heating load in the baseline is replaced with electric heating in the electric boiler measure scenario.

Figure 5 is a pair of stacked bar charts for one example building, titled "Monthly Energy Consumption by End Use - Baseline Building" (top) and "Monthly Energy Consumption by End Use - Electric Boiler Measure Scenario" (bottom). Both plot Energy Consumption in kWh on the y-axis against the twelve months January through December, with a shared legend covering Water Systems, Heat Rejection, Pumps, Fans, Interior Equipment, Exterior Lighting, Interior Lighting, Cooling, and Heating. Hatched bars denote natural gas end uses and solid fill denotes electricity. In the baseline chart the y-axis reaches about 700,000 kWh and the winter months are dominated by a very large hatched Heating segment -- roughly 450,000 kWh in January and December -- that disappears entirely in summer. In the measure chart the y-axis tops out lower, around 600,000 kWh, and the same winter mass reappears as a solid red electricity Heating segment of roughly 360,000 kWh in January. All other end uses are visually unchanged between the two panels. The figure is a clean illustration of pure fuel switching: the gas heating load is replaced by electric heating, and because the electric boiler runs at essentially 100% efficiency versus about 80% for the gas boiler, the winter peaks are somewhat shorter in the measure scenario.

Hashed colored bars indicate natural gas energy, and solid colored bars indicate electricity.
