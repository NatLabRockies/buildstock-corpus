## 5.6  Peak Impacts

Figure 10 shows the impact of the LED Lighting + HP-RTU Standard Performance + ASHP Boiler package on seasonal peak hours compared to both the baseline and Package 2. Compared to the baseline, winter season peaks are shifted earlier in the day by about 1 hour, and shoulder season peaks are shifted earlier by less than 30 minutes. With the fuel switching of heating loads, morning electric heating now has more influence on peak load in these seasons, shifting the peak timing earlier in buildings with non-trivial heating loads. Compared to Package 2, which has a higher performance HP-RTU, the winter and shoulder peaks are slightly earlier in the day with Package 3, but the difference is minimal. The summer peak timing remained relatively unchanged between the baseline and the two upgrade packages. This is because the cooling loads that dominate summer peaks changed in magnitude with the swap out for HP-RTUs and LED Lighting, but this is not expected to shift peak timing.

Figure 11 shows the impact of the LED Lighting + HP-RTU Standard Performance + ASHP Boiler package on seasonal peak intensity compared to both the baseline and Package 2. As seen in the figure, summer peaks reduced across all climate zones compared to the baseline due to the cooling and lighting savings from this upgrade package. In most climate zones, the summer peak intensity reduction for Package 2 was 2% -3% larger than for Package 3.

Winter peaks in heating-dominated climates increased due to fuel switching of the heating load. In cooling-dominated and moderate climates, the peak intensity was reduced or had minimal change. This is because heating is not as big a driver of the winter peak in these climates, so the HVAC efficiency and lighting improvements offset the added heating load. Across all climate zones, the winter peak intensity for Package 3 was up to 7% higher than Package 2 as a result of the difference in HP-RTU performance.

In shoulder seasons, the peak intensity was reduced across all climates, meaning the effects of the cooling and lighting improvements outweighed the added electric heating load. The shoulder peak intensity for Package 3 was 2% -6% higher across all climate zones compared to Package 2.
