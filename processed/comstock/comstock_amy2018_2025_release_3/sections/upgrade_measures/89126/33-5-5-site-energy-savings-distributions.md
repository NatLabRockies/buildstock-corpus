## 5.5 Site Energy Savings Distributions

This section discusses site energy consumption for quality assurance/quality control purposes. Note that site energy savings can be useful for these purposes, but other factors should be considered when drawing conclusions, as they do not necessarily translate proportionally to source energy savings, greenhouse gas emissions avoided, or energy costs.

Figure 9 shows the percentage savings distributions of the baseline ComStock models versus the LED Lighting + HP-RTU Standard Performance + ASHP Boiler package by end use and fuel type for applicable models. In other words, each data point in the distribution represents the percentage energy savings between a baseline ComStock model and the corresponding upgrade model with measures applied.

The end uses with the highest percentage savings observed are natural gas heating, other fuel heating, and interior lighting. Some buildings show 100% gas or other fuel heating savings, indicating that the HP-RTU and ASHP Boiler measures fuel switched the entire heating end use in those buildings. Buildings with negative electric heating savings were either: a) only applicable to the LED Lighting measure, or b) started with a small amount of electric heating (such as an office with baseboards in the entryway space only). Therefore, when the rest of the building's natural gas heating system was switched to electric heating, it resulted in a very large increase in electric heating. The relatively lower internal gains given off by the LED lights compared to lower-efficiency lighting options being replaced result in a higher heating load in winter. However, the efficiency improvements of the heat pump compared with the existing heating system often outweighs this heating penalty from the LED lights. The percentage savings (positive or negative) can be deceivingly high in buildings with low heating demands, as even small absolute changes represent a large percentage of the load. Buildings that began with 100% gas heating are not reflected in the 'Electricity Heating' row in Figure 9 because they technically have an infinite increase in electric heating, which cannot be shown in this plot.

Interior lighting savings were in the range of 30%-60% for most buildings that were applicable to the LED lighting measure, depending on the starting lighting technology in the building. Cooling and fan savings in most buildings is in the range of 0%-15%, which is a result of switching to a heat pump with higher-efficiency DX cooling than the existing cooling system. In addition, the switch to LED lighting results in lower internal gains and thus lower cooling loads in summer. District heating saw increases in energy, which again is a result of only the LED Lighting measure being applied, causing higher heating demands in winter. Pump energy saw increases in most buildings primarily due to the ASHP Boiler measure, which adds an additional circulation pump to the heat pump loop. However, pump energy represents a relatively small portion of the total energy, so the stock-level impacts are minimal.

Minimal differences are observed for water systems, refrigeration, and interior equipment, which see small changes in the baseline due only to minor changes in ambient air temperature that affect the operation of these systems.
