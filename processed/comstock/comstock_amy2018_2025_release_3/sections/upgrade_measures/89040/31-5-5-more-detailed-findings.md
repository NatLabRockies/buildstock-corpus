## 5.5  More Detailed Findings

Detailed findings in the previous VRF DOAS analysis mostly show the same trends and provide similar insights compared to this 25% upsizing allowance analysis. As discussed previously, the major difference of the 25% upsizing allowance versus the regular sizing comes from (1) the amount of backup heating triggered by the VRF system (and the corresponding heat pump heating) and (2) rated and operating COPs affected by the upsizing.

Figure 13: box plots of annual average VRF COP_comp&fan,operating by ASHRAE climate zone, two columns for cooling and heating on x-axes of 2 to 10, each zone comparing VRF with regular sizing against VRF with 25pct upsizing allowance. Cooling medians sit near 5 and heating near 3 to 4, and upsizing shifts both distributions only slightly. Section 5.5 defines the metric.

Figure 13 shows annual average operating COP comparisons between two sizing scenarios. Because the upsizing is geared toward the colder region, the upsizing in those regions reduces the rated COP (i.e., bigger units tend to have lower-rated COP), resulting in decreased operating COPs. Also, installing larger units (to handle heating demand) also means lower part load ratios when handling cooling demand during cooling season and this will result in more cycling losses at mild conditions.

The impact of upsizing is less in warmer regions.
