## 5.2  Stock Energy Impacts

The measure scenario package demonstrates 6.4% total site energy savings (306 trillion British thermal units [TBtu]) for the U.S. commercial building stock modeled in ComStock (Figure 3). Note that results can vary based on building type, climate, and several other factors. The ComStock public dataset enables users to view results to the specific segment they are interested in, which is recommended over extrapolating the national average. Note that roof insulation only impacts zone loads for the top floor of buildings with roofs. The relative impact of added roof insulation will decrease for buildings with more floors, where the roof area is a smaller portion of the overall envelope. Alternatively, single story buildings generally stand to gain the highest relative impact with roof insulation. The ComStock model includes variation in number of floors, and therefore the ratio of roof area to total envelope area.

Results for the standard performance heat pump RTU scenario are shown in isolation as well for comparison (HP RTU Std Perf).

The gas heating savings are due to replacing RTUs that previously used gas heating with electric HP-RTUs. These occur irrespective of the performance of the new system because the gas heating is entirely removed and replaced with heating of a different fuel source. For similar reasons, the additional roof insulation has little to no impact on the gas heating. The only exception to this is buildings wherein some gas systems are not applicable to the measures (e.g., gas unit heaters); the added roof insulation can reduce the heating load and therefore gas usage in these systems.

Electric heating shows increased use for both measure scenarios compared to the baseline, as expected, because we are replacing gas RTUs with those that use electric heating. However, some buildings with electric resistance RTUs generally show electric heating savings because the COP of a heat pump can be 2 to 4 times higher than that of electric resistance. The measure package that includes added roof insulation and the heat pump retrofit will include some decrease in heating load to help offset some of this increase. The added roof insulation reduces electric heating usage by 3% compared to the heat pump scenario alone when looking at applicable buildings (Figure 3b).

Cooling and fan end uses also show reduction in electricity usage. This is simply due to replacing older, less-efficient RTUs with newer RTUs that include more efficient staged cooling and fans. Many existing RTUs in the building stock have both single-stage cooling and fans, making them less efficient. Staged cooling and fans will have increased benefit over single stage systems in buildings that commonly operate under part-load conditions (mild to moderate load relative to the design load). Note that the decrease in fan energy also reduces the cooling load on the system, as there is less waste fan heat in the airstream that needs to be cooled.

Other end uses show minimal change, as expected.
