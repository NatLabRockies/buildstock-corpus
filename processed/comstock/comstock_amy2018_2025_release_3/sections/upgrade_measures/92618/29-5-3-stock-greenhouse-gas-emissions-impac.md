## 5.3  Stock Greenhouse Gas Emissions Impact

The measure scenario package shows between 4% and 7% GHG emissions avoided for the U.S. commercial building stock compared to the ComStock baseline depending on the electricity grid scenario (Figure 3). The HP-RTU scenario with roof insulation (Std Perf w Roof) shows slightly higher emissions avoided compared to the HP-RTU scenario alone (HP RTU Std Perf).

Figure 5. Three-panel stacked bar chart of Annual GHG Emissions (MMT CO2e), y-axis 0 to about 450, under three grid-emissions scenarios (eGRID 2021, LRMER High RE Cost 15, LRMER Low RE Cost 15). Each panel has three bars (Baseline, HP RTU Std Perf, Std Perf w Roof) stacked by fuel (Electricity, Natural Gas, Fuel Oil, Propane). eGRID 2021: Baseline 436 (Electricity 330, Natural Gas 98), HP RTU Std Perf 419 (-4%), Std Perf w Roof 417 (-4%). LRMER High RE Cost 15: Baseline 338, HP RTU Std Perf 321 (-5%), Std Perf w Roof 319 (-6%). LRMER Low RE Cost 15: Baseline 241, HP RTU Std Perf 225 (-7%), Std Perf w Roof 224 (-7%). Emissions reductions of roughly 4-7% grow under cleaner grid assumptions, with the roof variant marginally better.

Three electricity grid scenarios are presented: Cambium Long-Run Marginal Emissions Rate (LRMER) High Renewable Energy Cost 15-Year, Cambium LRMER Low Renewable Energy Cost 15-Year, and eGrid. MMT stands for million metric tons. Results for the Standard Performance Heat Pump RTU scenario (HP RTU Std Perf) are shown in isolation as well for comparison.

The emissions avoided are attributed to decreases in both electricity and natural gas across all grid scenarios. The natural gas emissions avoided are simply due to replacing gas RTUs with electric heat pumps. The electricity emissions avoided are more complicated. Electric heating usage increases because we added electric heat with heat pumps. However, electric heating decreases in buildings with electric resistance RTUs in the baseline model. The added roof insulation also decreases the heating and cooling loads. Cooling and fans also show reduced electricity usage because the newer HP-RTUs are replacing older, less-efficient RTUs in the stock. Taken together, these factors result in a small increase in electricity emissions at the national scale. However, these results may vary considerably based on climate, local electricity grid, and many other factors.
