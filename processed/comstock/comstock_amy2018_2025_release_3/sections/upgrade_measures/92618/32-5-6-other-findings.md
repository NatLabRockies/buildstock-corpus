## 5.6  Other Findings

Figure 8 compares average utility bill savings by state for the HP-RTU scenario alone and the HP-RTU scenario with added roof insulation. Positive values indicate utility bill savings. Most states on average show bill savings for both scenarios, with a few exceptions in colder climates. The addition of roof insulation generally increases bill savings versus the HP-RTU scenario alone. On average, the colder climates show lower bill savings, with a few states showing average bill increases. This is in part due to heat pump performance in cold climates but can also be impacted by regional utility bill differences. The latter has not been investigated as part of this study. Also note that these averages can be helpful to understand general trends, but as discussed in Figure 4, there is considerable variation in utility bill results, even within a climate zone. It is advised to leverage the ComStock public dataset to draw more specific conclusions and trends, many of which have not been investigated in this study.

Right map: Std Perf w Roof (adds roof insulation). Most states show positive mean bill savings of roughly 1-4% (deeper blue in parts of the West and Southeast), while a handful of states show small negative savings (bill increases, e.g. labels near -0.7% and -0.6% in the north-central states). The roof-insulation package (right) shows modestly larger savings than the HP-RTU-only scenario (left) in most states. (Many small per-state labels -- flag exact state values for QA.)

The plot uses the mean applicable electric rate structure for each model, and only shows buildings that are applicable to the measure.
