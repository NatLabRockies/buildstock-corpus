## 3.2.5.3  Location Type

Cambium's Generation and Emission Assessment (GEA) Regions are geographic representations of grid zones defined by NREL. They are created by aggregating balancing areas (the smallest geographic unit in which Cambium data are reported) that share similar regional grid characteristics, such as generation mix and transmission interconnections. There are 20 GEA regions defined in the Cambium 2022 dataset based on the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's (EPA) eGRID regions covering the contiguous United States. The measure extracts the 20 hourly (i.e., 8,760 values) load profiles and assigns the profile corresponding to the GEA region property of the building model.

In summary, models within the same GEA region share a Cambium hourly net load data with Mid-Case Scenario projected through 2035, to represent the (averaged) aggregation of demand for the whole grid in the region. The load data are then used to infer the daily dispatch windows based on the timings of daily grid peaks, corresponding to the daily critical times of the grid in the region. Note that Cambium data are available only for the contiguous United States, so for models/samples outside of the available regions (e.g., buildings in Alaska and Hawaii), the corresponding demand flexibility measure will be determined to be not applicable.
