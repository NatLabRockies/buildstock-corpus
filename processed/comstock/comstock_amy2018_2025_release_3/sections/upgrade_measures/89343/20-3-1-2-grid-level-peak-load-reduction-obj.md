## 3.1.2  Grid-Level Peak Load Reduction Objective Function

Grid-level load is one of the major targets of demand management of utilities, as it helps balance electricity supply and demand throughout the grid to prevent or mitigate grid stress, enhance grid resilience and reliability, and improve efficiency. Designing building controls with a focus on grid-level load management can enhance alignment with actual grid stress that is caused by high regional grid peak, leading to more efficient energy distribution and more practical demand flexibility to the whole grid, compared to methods based on individual building load, or indirect data reflecting grid stress such as TOU schedules, in which the flexibility will be limited within a fixed schedule control/policy scheme.

For ComStock workflow and data specifically, grid peak may not necessarily coincide with the commercial stock peak, or the peak of individual buildings, so it is expected that the performance analysis results based on available commercial stock data would be biased. However, it is still beneficial to the grid to act on the grid peaks, and the benefits will be estimated by designated metrics.

The measure utilizes the Cambium load data [28] extracted for buildings in a region (e.g., grid region) that share similar grid characteristics such as sources of power plants, available utility services (e.g., tariffs), and electricity transmission boundaries [29]. The load data serve as representatives of the grid-level load profiles that reflect the demand/load of the grid a building belongs to and impacts. Based on this input, the measure will find the grid peak for a given region each day, and apply demand flexibility control to shed or shift building loads in the corresponding periods, thereby helping reduce the peak demand of the grid.
