## 5.2  Stock Energy Impacts

The heat/energy recovery measure demonstrates 7% total site energy savings (304 trillion British thermal units [TBtu]) for the U.S. commercial building stock modeled in ComStock (Figure 7). The savings are primarily attributed to:

- 23% stock heating savings (268 TBtu)
- 10% stock cooling savings (82 TBtu)
- -8% stock combined fan and heat recovery savings ( -46 TBtu)
- o Note that the heat recovery end use includes the additional fan energy associated with the static pressure increase of the new energy recovery systems, plus the fan energy and motor power associated with the energy recovery systems and enthalpy wheels in the existing building stock.

Stock-level stacked column chart of annual site energy consumption in TBtu comparing the ComStock baseline against the heat/energy recovery measure scenario, with column totals printed above the bars: Baseline 4638 TBtu and Energy Recovery 4334 TBtu, a 304 TBtu reduction, reported as 7% total site energy savings for the U.S. commercial building stock. The legend lists 19 fuel-and-end-use categories, from Heat Rejection and Heat Recovery Electricity through Pumps, Refrigeration, Exterior Lighting, the four Water Systems categories, the four Heating categories, Interior Lighting, Cooling (District Cooling and Electricity), Fans, and Interior Equipment (Natural Gas and Electricity). The text decomposes the total into 23% stock heating savings (268 TBtu), 10% stock cooling savings (82 TBtu), and a penalty of -8% (-46 TBtu) for the combined fans and heat recovery end uses. Heating savings appear for both natural gas and electricity, with natural gas much larger because the stock heats mostly with gas. The Fans band is essentially unchanged, labeled 588.3 falling to 587.1, under 1% savings; a new Heat Recovery Electricity band appears only in the upgrade column, holding the added enthalpy wheel and static pressure fan power. Interior Lighting Electricity (451.0) and Interior Equipment Electricity (836.5) are identical in both columns.

The heating and cooling savings are from the energy recovery system preconditioning the outdoor ventilation air. This reduces or removes the load on the HVAC heating and cooling coils, saving energy. The heating end use shows savings for both gas and electricity, although there are considerably more natural gas heating savings, as the building stock uses more natural gas for heating.

The fan end use shows negligible savings (&lt;1%), likely from a combination of reduced cycling times for systems and reduced fan flow fraction in VAV systems. However, the increase in the energy recovery end use reflects added fan power due to the energy recovery system. So, this measure demonstrates a net addition of fan energy compared to the existing baseline building stock.
