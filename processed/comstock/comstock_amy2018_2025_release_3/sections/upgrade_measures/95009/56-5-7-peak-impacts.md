## 5.7  Peak Impacts

Figure 10 shows the impact of the Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope + LED Lighting package on seasonal peak hours, compared to the Baseline and the other Comprehensive GHP packages. Compared to the baseline, summer and shoulder peak times have minimal change, with the median moving earlier in the day by a fraction of an hour. Compared to the Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope package, there is effectively no change in summer and shoulder peak times.

The median winter peak time moved earlier in the day by less than an hour compared to the baseline. Compared to the Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope package, the winter peak hour moved very slightly earlier in the day. However, it is fair to say that the addition of the lighting measure does not have any substantial impact on the daily timing of seasonal peaks.

Figure 10. Maximum daily peak timing by season between the baseline, Comprehensive GHP scenario, Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope scenario, and Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope + LED Lighting scenario

Figure 10 is a set of three stacked panels labeled "summer", "winter", and "shoulder". Each panel shows a box plot of the maximum daily peak timing, in hour of day, on an x-axis running 0 to 24, for four rows under the heading "Upgrade Name": Baseline, Comprehensive GHP, Comprehensive GHP + Envelope, and Comprehensive GHP + Envelope + Lighting. In summer, all four distributions are similar with interquartile ranges roughly spanning hours 11 to 15 and whiskers from about hour 5 to hour 21; the upgrade medians shift earlier than the baseline by only a fraction of an hour. In winter, the baseline box sits around hours 9 to 12 with whiskers reaching from about hour 2 to hour 22, and the three upgrade scenarios shift slightly earlier and tighten. In shoulder season, the four rows are again nearly identical, with boxes around hours 10 to 14. The takeaway is that these packages barely move when the peak occurs; the effect is on peak magnitude rather than peak timing, and there is effectively no difference in summer or shoulder peak timing between the GHP-only package and the packages that add envelope and lighting measures.

Figure 11 shows the impact of the Comprehensive GHP packages on seasonal peak intensity for the median building. In all climate zones and seasons, the peak intensity is reduced further and further as more efficiency measures are added. The Comprehensive GHP + High Efficiency Envelope + LED Lighting package demonstrates the lowest peak intensity of the four scenarios shown for all climate zones and seasons. This indicates that the envelope and lighting measures are lowering the HVAC loads during critical hours of the day throughout the year.

![Figure 11. Data-bar matrix of median summer, winter, and shoulder electricity peak intensity in watts per square foot by ASHRAE climate zone for the baseline and three Comprehensive GHP scenarios](95009_images/image_000012_7cf01912ec88309fc2c1a82efe72e50559517b02ae2ad64537bbf9b0e79c83e1.png)

Figure 11 is a matrix of small horizontal data bars with printed values. Rows are grouped by climate zone (1 through 8), and within each zone there are four rows under "Upgrade Name": Baseline, Comprehensive GHP, Comprehensive GHP + Envelope, and Comprehensive GHP + Envelope + Lighting. Three columns give "Median Summer Electricity Peak Intensity (W/sqft)", "Median Winter Electricity Peak Intensity (W/sqft)", and "Median Shoulder Electricity Peak Intensity (W/sqft)". Within every zone and every season the value decreases monotonically down the four rows, so each added measure lowers median peak intensity and the full package is lowest in all 24 zone-season cells. Baseline summer peak intensity is highest in zone 2 at about 4.1 W/sqft and ranges down to about 2.6 W/sqft in zone 8; under the full package summer values fall to roughly 1.5-2.3 W/sqft. Winter and shoulder baselines run roughly 1.9-3.4 W/sqft and drop to roughly 1.3-2.2 W/sqft under the full package. The reduction indicates the envelope and lighting measures are cutting HVAC load during the critical hours of the day year-round, not just shifting energy. Individual cell values are dense and should be treated as provisional; the monotone ordering and the approximate ranges are read with higher confidence.
