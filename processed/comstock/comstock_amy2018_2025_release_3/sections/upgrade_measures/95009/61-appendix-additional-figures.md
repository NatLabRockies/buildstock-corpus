![Figure A-1. Map of the United States shaded by ASHRAE climate zone, with moisture regime bands for marine, dry, and moist](95009_images/image_000015_2f4648e2f2118e9e25105b19c82bb153adcf4859a2b2f8898891c2a08eed7295.png)

Figure A-1 is a county-level choropleth map of the United States colored by ASHRAE climate zone, used as the reference for every by-climate-zone chart in this document. Vertical dividers across the top split the country into three moisture regimes labeled "Marine (C)" on the West Coast, "Dry (B)" through the interior west, and "Moist (A)" over the eastern two-thirds. Numbered zones run from 1 (hottest, red, along the southern tip of Florida) through 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 (coldest contiguous, purple, across the northern plains and upper Midwest), with zone numbers labeled directly on the shaded regions. A white line across the Southeast marks the "Warm-Humid" boundary, with warm-humid counties below it. Two inset text boxes carry the non-contiguous rules: all of Alaska is in Zone 7 except the boroughs of Bethel, Northwest Arctic, Dellingham, Southeast Fairbanks, Fairbanks N. Star, Wade Hampton, Nome, Yukon-Koyukuk, and North Slope, which are Zone 8; and Zone 1 includes Hawaii, Guam, Puerto Rico, and the Virgin Islands.
